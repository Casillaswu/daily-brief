01 · Headline Regulation ○Neutral N. America · Prediction Markets

Prediction-market legal war: this week it flips to the federal CFTC suing New York, and the incumbent AGA siding with the states

Courthouse News · Bettors Insider · SBC Americas · 07/22

First, the background. Prediction markets (PMs) are platforms that let people bet on whether an event will happen, including who wins a football match; the main names are Kalshi (holds a US federal CFTC license) and Polymarket. For the past two months it was states going after these platforms one by one, saying they are just gambling and belong under state gambling law. This week it went the other way.

First the federal regulator sued a state. The CFTC (Commodity Futures Trading Commission) took New York's gaming regulator to court, saying event contracts are federally regulated products and states have no right to block them under gambling law — the same play as its earlier suit against Wisconsin. Then the incumbent industry openly picked a side. The AGA (American Gaming Association, representing Las Vegas casinos and the main licensed operators) moved to join the CFTC-vs-Wisconsin case on the states' side; more awkwardly, someone found AGA-written lobbying text appearing word-for-word in Maryland's filing to the CFTC — putting "the licensed industry is quietly aligning the states' script" out in the open. The same week, the AGA and the Indian Gaming Association told Congress that prediction markets, by bypassing state licensing, have already cost states about $1.2 billion (roughly NT$38bn) in gambling tax.


Bottom line: short-term mildly +EV for operators that hold state licenses and pay tax by the book, but how it ends depends on the federal side — hence Neutral. The point is who's leading the fight now: last week it was states suing platforms, this week it's the federal CFTC suing a state, with the incumbent AGA stepping in openly too. The licensed industry clearly treats prediction markets as its top rival now — paying lawyers, using its trade body, lobbying Congress — and "$1.2bn in lost tax" is the best card it can hand lawmakers. Action: for markets sensitive to offshore platforms like the Philippines and Vietnam, remember the pattern — once local licensed operators find a new platform siphoning tax, they push the government to strike on tax grounds, faster and harder than the consumer-protection angle. If you have a traffic or tech partnership with a prediction-market platform, write it into the contract now: when a state bans it, who eats the loss.

Evolution (the live-casino tech leader) served formal notice of termination on 7/21, cancelling its roughly $85m (about NT$2.7bn) acquisition of Galaxy Gaming. Last week it was "reconsidering"; this week it happened.


Bottom line: -EV for mid-sized suppliers that rely on being acquired to raise cash, +EV for large groups with plenty of cash that can wait. State-approval delay has escalated from "slowing deals down" to "killing deals." SIGNAL 03 · LatAm · Brazil

Brazil's SPA opens a fresh consultation: redefining how operators obtain commercial authorization, closing 9/9

Brazil's SPA (the Finance Ministry's betting-regulation secretariat) published a public-consultation notice in the federal gazette on 7/22 to draft a new ordinance on how fixed-odds betting operators get "commercial authorization." The comment window runs 7/27 to 9/9 (45 days), following two advertising ordinances on 7/10. Market context: as of 6/30, 87 operators held active federal licenses with another 14 pending — brands include Betano, bet365 and Superbet.


Bottom line: +EV for large brands that have already spent on compliance and hold licenses, -EV for smaller brands hoping to slip into Brazil cheaply. Brazil is moving from the "2025 opening bonanza" into a phase of "detail rules filled in one by one, the bar raised step by step." The key here isn't ads or tax — it's that the rules for getting authorization themselves are being rewritten, i.e. the definition of the entry ticket is changing. Action: for anyone doing Brazil, watch the draft's new authorization requirements closely over these 45 days before the 9/9 close — they directly decide whether you can still operate next year on your current structure. SIGNAL 04 · Asia · Macau

Macau's post-World-Cup read splits: Citi sees daily GGR up 9% and recovery, CLSA cuts July to -12%

In the first week after the World Cup ended (7/19), two brokers gave opposite reads. Citi said Macau's daily GGR (gross gaming revenue, what players lose) in the past week ran about 9% higher week-on-week and kept its July forecast at -5% y-o-y; CLSA cut July to -12% and trimmed 2026 full-year growth to just 2%. Another figure: Macau's first-half gaming tax already reached US$6.34bn (about NT$200bn), only 55% of the full-year target, meaning the second half must collect more to catch up.


Bottom line: Neutral for Macau casinos, but the split between analysts is itself a signal. With the World Cup no longer diverting players' gambling budgets, whether GGR actually rebounds is a coin-flip between Citi and CLSA. Action: for those serving Macau's high-end players, don't rush to pick a direction before the official numbers land in early August — anchor decisions on July's official GGR, the hard data. First-half tax at only 55% of target matters most: if the second half falls short, fiscal pressure will translate into tighter operating demands on casinos. Read original →

SIGNAL 05 · Asia · Philippines

PAGCOR B2B accreditation is 5 days out: from 8/1, unaccredited systems, platforms and games are forcibly decommissioned

PAGCOR (the Philippine gaming regulator, which both licenses private operators and runs its own Casino Filipino venues) set its B2B supplier accreditation deadline at 7/31 — 5 days away. Suppliers that haven't met the four requirements (a non-refundable application fee, documents including a due-diligence report, on-site inspection plus live system testing, and a cash deposit per entity) have their systems, platforms and games forcibly decommissioned from 8/1; the transition window for those who filed before 5/31 also ends this week.


Bottom line: +EV for accredited licensees with a local anchor, -EV for offshore suppliers with no local anchor still watching from the sidelines. The judgment was fully laid out in W29; this week only marks the time dimension — from "two weeks to prepare" to "8/1 actually enforces it." Once 8/1 hits, players see "some games on this platform are gone," and won't parse whose fault it is. Action: over these 5 days, do a final check of every content supplier's 7/31 accreditation status, list the games that could be pulled after 8/1, and have a fallback content list ready now. Ohio's credit-card deposit ban advances, nearing a late-summer vote

SBC Americas · 07/07

Ohio's gaming regulator is advancing a rule to bar players from funding sportsbook accounts with credit cards; the comment window closed 7/17, with an administrative rules review next and a vote possible by late summer. The World Cup ends; the question for licensees is unchanged: how to make betting as frictionless as a prediction market

📍 Editorial synthesis from Signal 01, §2, §8 — no single source link

The World Cup's end (7/19) leaves two things at once: the wider legal channels are laid, the bigger the volume; and the more frictionless the bet, the bigger the volume (prediction markets need no ID verification, no deposit cap, and hit 85% of sports-betting handle in June). Bottom line: +EV for brands with wide channels and the capital to self-build a frictionless product, -EV for those keeping customers on compliance narrative alone.
