01 · LEAD Market −Negative Asia · Philippines

Philippine Q2 e-gaming revenue falls 37.2% year on year; PAGCOR H1 profit down 85%, and the plan to split it from its own casinos goes to the Palace for review

Inside Asian Gaming · Asia Gaming Brief · 08/10

The Philippines published second-quarter figures on 10 August. Industry-wide gross gaming revenue (GGR, total wagered less what players win back) came to PHP88.1bn (about US$1.45bn), down 20.3% year on year. Licensed land-based casinos held up, accounting for 51.5% of the total; the collapse is in electronic gaming — E-Games and E-Bingo together at PHP39.9bn (about US$675m), down 37.2%. The regulator's own books look worse: PAGCOR (the Philippine gaming regulator) reported H1 revenue of PHP43.3bn (about US$705m), down 26.6%, with profit down 85%.

That 37.2% did not come from nowhere. In August 2025 the central bank, BSP, ordered the two dominant e-wallets, GCash and Maya, to cut their links to online gambling platforms. Player numbers on licensed platforms then fell by about 70%, while unlicensed platforms saw a roughly 40% rise. Q2 2026 is the first quarter that can be compared cleanly against a full pre-delinking period, so this year-on-year figure is the real invoice for that piece of payments surgery. The same week brought a second development: the plan to decouple PAGCOR from its own casinos was submitted to the Palace in August by the state enterprise governance commission, GCG. Once separated, PAGCOR becomes a pure regulator and its Casino Filipino venues go up for sale, valued at an estimated PHP30bn to PHP50bn (about US$530m to US$890m), targeted for completion in late 2026 or early 2027.


Bottom line: the contraction in Philippine online is policy-driven, not cyclical, so it will not recover on its own unless the payment rails reopen — and there is no sign of that. −EV for brands and suppliers that depend on Philippine online for the bulk of revenue; +EV for integrated resorts (IRs) and their newly launched online platforms, because the players who left the licensed channel have partly gone to unlicensed sites and are partly being picked up by the IRs' new online products. In practice: Philippine volume forecasts have to be rebuilt on a post-delinking baseline; projecting H2 2026 off H1 2025 numbers will overstate everything. Two things to watch this quarter — when the Palace signs off on the decoupling plan, and who bids for Casino Filipino, because whoever buys those land-based assets will almost certainly apply for an online licence alongside them.

SIGNAL 02 · LATAM · BRAZIL

Brazil's Finance Ministry orders Pixbet to stop taking bets and refund open stakes, with a BRL 200,000 daily fine for non-compliance

Brazil's Federal Police launched "Operation Arena" on 13 August, working with prosecutors, the Finance Ministry and the Federal Revenue Service to investigate currency evasion and money laundering routed through sports betting. The Finance Ministry issued its order the same day: licensed operator Pixbet (whose domains include pix.bet.br, ganhei.bet.br and betdasorte.bet.br) must immediately stop accepting new bets, cancel all open bets and return the money to players, with a BRL 200,000 daily fine (about US$37,000) if it fails to comply. The stated grounds from the SPA (Brazil's Secretariat of Prizes and Betting) were not the laundering case itself but two administrative failures: the company had not filed mandatory documents, and lacked mechanisms to monitor compulsive players.


Bottom line: this is the harshest action taken against a licensed operator since Brazil opened its market, and it differs in kind from previous enforcement — Anatel (the national telecoms agency) has been blocking unlicensed sites, whereas this hits a company on the licence register. The legal basis is what matters: the SPA used missing filings and absent player-protection mechanisms, not a laundering conviction. In other words, a documentation gap alone is enough to void a licence the same day. −EV for licensees with loose compliance, +EV for those with tight compliance. In practice: Brazilian partners need re-checking on two points — whether SPA filings are up to date, and whether the platform has a verifiable compulsive-play monitoring function. Both should be written into contracts as the counterparty's warranties, because once a partner is suspended, open bets must be refunded in full and that quarter's marketing spend goes to zero. SIGNAL 03 · NORTH AMERICA · REGULATION

The CFTC invokes Section 8a(9) emergency powers telling Kalshi to disregard state court orders; a day later a Washington court rules its event contracts are gambling

CFTC (Commodity Futures Trading Commission) Chairman Mike Selig signed Release 9281-26 on 11 August, invoking Section 8a(9) of the Commodity Exchange Act to direct Kalshi (a CFTC-registered prediction market) to keep operating nationwide and not to suspend voluntarily in response to New York Attorney General Letitia James's US$36bn action. That emergency authority has been used only six times before, most recently in the 1980 Hunt brothers silver case; the CFTC had already used the same move against Michigan a month earlier. Then on 12 August a Washington state court ruled outright that Kalshi's event contracts are gambling, ordering it to stop offering sports, elections, politics, entertainment, culture, tech and "mention" contracts, with initial geofencing due by 19 August and a GeoComply multi-source system by 2 September.


Bottom line: a federal agency telling its own registrant to disregard a state court order has no precedent in US regulatory practice, and no court has yet ruled on whether the CFTC can do it. The agency's reasoning is transparent: rather than wait for the preemption question to be settled, keep Kalshi trading every day, because the longer it trades the stronger the fait accompli argument becomes. For operators holding state licences and paying state tax, the Washington ruling is good news in the short term — it is the first state court decision explicitly labelling event contracts as gambling. But the federal track is unresolved, so the overall read is neutral. In practice: this pattern — local licensees pushing government to act on illegal-gambling grounds while the challenger produces a higher-tier licence as a shield — recurs in the Philippines, Vietnam and Brazil, with the higher-tier licence replaced by an offshore jurisdiction's permit. Do not read direction from who won this week; watch whether technical measures such as geofencing actually get installed, because once live they are hard to remove and say more about where jurisdiction really sits than any ruling. SIGNAL 04 · NORTH AMERICA · OPERATORS

Before NFL kickoff: prediction markets forecast to trade US$36.8bn, overtaking sportsbook handle of US$32.3bn, with majors accepting over US$500m in lost earnings

Legal US sportsbooks are projected to take a record US$32.3bn in NFL handle (the total amount players wager) this season. Over the same period prediction markets are forecast to trade US$36.8bn on NFL outcomes, more than double last season and the first time the figure exceeds sportsbook handle. The cost shows up on the income statement: leading US online operators collectively project over US$500m in forgone adjusted operating earnings this year from building and marketing their own prediction market products, with DraftKings and FanDuel each committing US$200m to US$300m or more. Flutter will fold FanDuel Predicts into its single app before the regular season, alongside sportsbook, casino and fantasy sports.


Bottom line: these figures turn last quarter's earnings-call narrative into a measurable scale — prediction markets are not incremental to sports betting, they take the same money from the same people, and this autumn on the largest book of all, the NFL, they will out-trade sportsbooks. −EV for mid-sized sportsbook brands competing on marketing budget: when the leaders will absorb US$500m in lost earnings to hold the entry point, the floor under customer acquisition cost rises for everyone, and brands that cannot match it get squeezed out of inventory this quarter. In practice: when forecasting sportsbook volume, treat prediction markets as a deduction from existing handle, not an addition. Flutter's approach is also worth copying — folding the product into an existing app turns a new launch into one more tab, avoiding the cost of acquiring users all over again, which is currently the cheapest route to market. SIGNAL 05 · EUROPE · UK

The UK scraps the "aim to permit" presumption, letting councils refuse new betting shops outright

The UK government announced on 11 August that it will remove the "aim to permit" presumption in Section 153 of the Gambling Act 2005. That provision required licensing authorities to lean towards approval whenever an application met the statutory licensing objectives; in practice councils could attach conditions but could not refuse simply because residents objected to another betting shop opening nearby. Once removed, councils can refuse outright. The change applies across Great Britain. In England, adult gaming centres (AGCs) — 24-hour venues built around slot machines — will additionally require planning permission. Housing Secretary Angela Rayner said the public was unhappy about such venues opening near schools, with some linked to criminal activity.


Bottom line: this is the biggest change to UK retail gambling rules in a decade, because it moves the decision on whether a shop may open from a statutory presumption back to local politics. −EV for UK betting shops and adult gaming centres: expansion is effectively frozen, though existing sites become more valuable precisely because newcomers cannot get in. Limited direct effect on UK online brands, but squeezing retail is usually the prelude to the next round of pressure online, and the UK remote gaming duty only rose to 40% this April. In practice: the case demonstrates a regulatory route worth noting — leave the gambling act itself alone and remove a single procedural presumption, and expansion stops. It is cheap and politically easy, which makes it readily copied by local government elsewhere, including Philippine local authorities dealing with branch venues and outdoor advertising. Regulation○Neutral📌 Editor's Pick
EDITOR'S PICK

Twice in two months the CFTC has told Kalshi to disregard restraining orders: Section 8a(9) emergency powers are now its routine answer to state courts

📍 Editorial synthesis from Signal 03 and §1 — no single source link

The frequency deserves its own note. Last month the CFTC used Section 8a(9) against Michigan, telling Kalshi to disregard that state judge's restraining order; this month it did the same to New York (see Signal 03). The authority sat unused for more than forty years after the 1980 silver case, and has now been used twice in two months. The timeline explains how it got there: on 4 August Judge Rakoff denied the CFTC's emergency restraining order, on 7 August Judge Marrero refused to pause the New York case, and on 11 August, with judicial routes exhausted, the agency switched to an administrative order.


Bottom line: doing something once is an exception; doing it twice in two months is settled practice — and it costs almost nothing, since signing an administrative order requires no court at all. That suggests the CFTC intends to apply it in every state that follows. The risk is that it has no judicial backing: the moment a federal court rules the agency cannot do this, everything that traded under the order becomes contested. In practice: this is a reminder that regulatory conflict has two modes — litigate and act only after winning, or create facts on the ground and make the other side undo them. When reading any market, look past litigation progress at what administrative tools each side can use without a court.

EDITOR'S PICK

There are now three sovereigns in this fight, each with a federal statute to cite

📍 Editorial synthesis from Signal 03 and §7 — no single source link

The US conflict is no longer federal versus state but three-sided: the CFTC citing the Commodity Exchange Act, individual states citing their own gambling laws and attorney general powers, and tribes citing IGRA. Each has statutory footing and enforcement tools, and none has authority to decide for the other two. That is why this has run for over a year without direction — not because the law is unclear, but because three bodies of law are simultaneously clear and in conflict. Only the Supreme Court can resolve it, and that takes years.


Bottom line: the regulatory status of US prediction markets will not be settled for the foreseeable future, and each side will keep using its own tools to create facts on the ground. For prediction markets that is a long-running cost of uncertainty; for sportsbooks it is long-running competitive pressure, because the opponent will not be killed off by a single ruling. In practice: any plan premised on waiting for the courts to finish does not work, because that day is not coming. Cross-market agreements should be drafted on the assumption that the product may be banned in some jurisdiction at any time, with loss allocation and exit conditions written in — which applies well beyond the US.

Put this week's threads together and sportsbook brands face pressure on two levels this season. The first is money: the leaders will absorb over US$500m in lost earnings to hold the prediction market entry point (see Signal 04), which lifts the floor under customer acquisition cost for everyone. The second is product: prediction markets break the sportsbook's book into event contracts and sell the same thing in a form gambling law does not reach — and on the NFL, the largest book of all, they will out-trade sportsbooks this year.


Bottom line: the two require different answers. On cost there is no clever fix, only spreading acquisition cost across retention and value per player. On product a choice is required — either add event-contract formats, or make licensed, taxed and verifiable sporting integrity an explicit point of difference. The middle path is the dangerous one: spending on a new format without being able to explain how it differs from the competition. In practice: brand messaging is worth rewriting this season, moving from odds and promotions to why betting on a regulated platform is safer — which is precisely where the opposition has taken its hardest blows in recent weeks, being ruled gambling, investigated for insider trading, and having an entire product category pulled.
