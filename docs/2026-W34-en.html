01 · Top Story Regulation −Negative Australia · Ad law

Australia writes the whole marketing rulebook into law: no betting ads around live sport, no athlete or influencer endorsement, no inducements for 14 days after signup

Inside Asian Gaming · SBC News · 08/21

Australia's parliament passed the Interactive Gambling Amendment (Gambling Reform) Act 2026 on 19 August, in force 1 January 2027. On advertising: between 5am and 8:30pm, betting ads are banned from 15 minutes before a live event starts until 5 minutes after it ends; in-venue placement is banned, and player and referee kit cannot carry brand marks; athletes, celebrities and influencers cannot be used to promote. Television is capped at three spots per hour in the same window, radio is blacked out during school drop-off and pick-up, and broadcasting odds promotions is banned. Online placement is allowed only against users who are logged in, over 18 and able to opt out.

On inducements: direct marketing inducements to new customers are banned for 14 days after signup, banned for three months after a person deregisters from the national self-exclusion register BetStop, and banned permanently for customers assessed as high risk. Paying staff commission tied to customer activity is also banned. The package includes a national advertising opt-out register, lets banks block payments to unlicensed operators, and widens the site-blocking powers of the Australian Communications and Media Authority ACMA; gambling companies share the cost of building the register.


Bottom line: what matters here is not how strict the act is but that it pins down every step of marketing execution — airtime, spots per hour, who can front a campaign, when an inducement can be sent, how staff get paid. Rules like these used to sit in voluntary codes or licence conditions, where there was room to argue. In statute there is none. −EV for brands that acquire through live-sport slots and influencers, +EV for brands that already moved budget into owned content and retention CRM. In practice: the text works as a checklist. Run it against current practice in the Philippines and Brazil — Senate Bill 2347 in the Philippine Congress and the state-level ad limits in Brazil cover the same steps. Whatever Australia has already banned belongs on a 12-month list of replacement options.

SIGNAL 02 · Asia · Philippines

Over 60% of Philippine licensees earn less than the PHP 9m MGF

Philippine legal advisory firm Arden Consult said on 20 August that, extrapolated from second-quarter revenue run rates, more than 60% of licensed online operators earn less each month than the PHP 9 million minimum guaranteed fee (MGF) that PAGCOR (the Philippine gaming regulator) now requires — about US$147,000, or NT$4.6 million. Operators pay the higher of the percentage regulatory fee or the MGF, against a minimum monthly gross gaming revenue benchmark of PHP 30 million; from January 2027 the MGF rises to PHP 10.5 million and the benchmark to PHP 35 million. The floor is fixed, so it is due even when revenue falls short. Philippine online gross gaming revenue was about US$1.19 billion in the first half (about NT$36.9 billion), down 31% year on year. PAGCOR froze new licence applications in March 2024, and existing accreditations are being quoted at US$3 million to US$15 million (about NT$93 million to NT$465 million). Arden chief executive Tonet Quiogue notes the accreditation itself is not transferable: any change of shareholding or control needs prior PAGCOR board approval and a full probity review, and dormant-company or nominee structures are treated as an undisclosed transfer of control.


Bottom line: the contraction in the Philippines this year is not demand falling away. The regulator deliberately raised the minimum cost of staying in the market. A small operator that used to survive on PHP 10 million a month now owes PHP 9 million in fixed fees, so the rule itself removes it. −EV for licensees without scale, +EV for mid-size and large operators that can absorb the freed share and cover the MGF. In practice: the list of viable Philippine partners will get materially shorter over the next six months. Screening a partner on licence status alone is not enough — check whether monthly gross gaming revenue actually clears the benchmark, because those that fall short will exit when the floor rises again in January 2027. Quiogue's line is worth keeping: do not build the structure first and declare it later. These deals usually fail on a mismatch between the ownership on file and how the business is actually run, not on price. Read original →

SIGNAL 03 · North America · Prediction markets

The prediction-market fight reaches the regulator's table: CFTC advisory committee's first meeting, CME and Kalshi clash; Korea and Denmark block Polymarket the same week

The reconstituted innovation advisory committee of the CFTC (the US Commodity Futures Trading Commission) met for the first time on 20 August, with the chief executives of Coinbase, Kalshi (the CFTC-licensed US prediction market), Ripple and Polymarket (the offshore prediction market that settles in crypto) at the table alongside senior figures from CME Group (the Chicago Mercantile Exchange) and Nasdaq. The clash came between CME chairman Terry Duffy and Kalshi co-founder Luana Lopes Lara: Duffy said federal regulators were letting manipulable event contracts reach ordinary consumers; after Lara pushed back, Duffy replied that CME's compliance department alone has more people than Kalshi has employees. In June, Kalshi and Polymarket together handled about US$44.8 billion (about NT$1.39 trillion), against average monthly handle of about US$14 billion across all legal US sports betting in 2025. Internationally the sector took two hits in the same week. Korea's Communications Standards Commission voted on 18 August to block Polymarket, on the grounds that a winner-take-all structure encourages speculation. Denmark's gambling authority published a block list of 98 unlicensed sites on 19 August with Polymarket on it, citing those sites' use of Danish, Danish kroner and marketing aimed at local users.


Bottom line: the US line will not resolve soon, but the international line answered the question this week. Outside the US, mature regulators are treating prediction markets as unlicensed online casinos rather than financial derivatives. Korea and Denmark both used existing gambling law and existing blocking procedures, with no new statute needed. That is real bad news for international expansion and good news for operators already holding local licences. Neutral overall, because federal pre-emption inside the US is still unsettled. In practice: two things predict how a market will treat prediction platforms — whether the local regulator already has a site-blocking process, and whether the platform is localising. A platform running local language, local currency and local marketing is almost always handled as an unlicensed operator. The same test applies to how the Philippines and Brazil treat offshore sites. SIGNAL 04 · LatAm · Brazil

Lula calls a snap ministerial meeting on the future of sports betting: "if it were up to me I would shut the bets down"; Minas Gerais bans ads in public spaces the same week

President Lula called ministers to the presidential residence at short notice this week to discuss whether the sports betting industry should continue; the summons went out that morning. Defending a prohibitionist position, he said that if it were up to him he would shut the bets down. That runs along the same line as his instruction in late July for Workers' Party ministers to support PL 2.258/2026, the bill banning online casino, which the government wants through Congress before the October election. In the same week the Minas Gerais state government issued a decree banning betting companies from advertising in state-owned public spaces, covering the Mineirão stadium, the Mineirinho arena, bus terminals, airports and state highways, in formats including big screens, banners, stickers and audio spots. State concession holders must terminate existing contracts with betting companies.


Bottom line: the federal move is political intent and the state move is an administrative order. They point the same way, but only the state one is already in force. Whether the president's words become law depends on the October election and the shape of the next Congress, which is highly uncertain; the state decree carries no such doubt — it is written, so it is executed, and the ad inventory clears that day. −EV for online casino businesses in Brazil, because PL 2.258 is aimed squarely at them; brands running sports betting only are less exposed, since the bill is designed to ban casino and keep sports betting. In practice: Brazil planning should split into two tracks this quarter. Online casino should be prepared on the assumption it could be banned within 12 months, with a conversion or exit plan. Sports betting should be planned as open but with shrinking ad inventory. The Minas decree needed no legislation, only a state order, so other states can copy it easily, and it hits the most expensive inventory: stadiums and transport hubs. Brazilian venue and outdoor contracts should move to short terms with termination clauses from here. Read original →

SIGNAL 05 · Europe · UK

The Premier League kicks off with no gambling brand on a shirt front for the first time in 20 years; financial firms take five clubs

The 2026-27 Premier League season opened on 21 August with no gambling brand on the front of any of the 20 clubs' shirts. The ban was voted through by the clubs themselves in 2023 with a three-year transition, which expires this season. The scope is narrow: only the shirt front is off limits, and sleeve badges, training kit, pitchside boards and other club partnerships are all still allowed. Eight clubs needed a replacement sponsor this season, and Nottingham Forest, Sunderland and Chelsea were still unconfirmed at kick-off. Financial services took the largest share of the replacements, sponsoring 5 of the 20 clubs: Everton swapped Stake for trading platform CMC Markets. Technology moved in too, with Crystal Palace signing application development platform Temporal and Fulham signing data technology firm ClickHouse.


Bottom line: the value here is not the Premier League itself but the sequence it demonstrates — the industry restricts itself first, the regulator follows. The clubs voted the ban in themselves in 2023; three years on, the UK government is tightening retail rules and online tax at the same time, and the two reinforce each other. −EV for brand marketing built on Premier League exposure: the most expensive and most recognisable slot is gone, and what is left — sleeve badges and pitchside boards — costs less per unit and is far weaker on recall. In practice: financial and technology buyers took the vacated shirt fronts, which means clubs now have alternative buyers and will negotiate harder on the remaining inventory. If the same pressure lands in the Philippines or Brazil, the first mover will likely be a league or a club acting on its own rather than a law, so sports sponsorship contracts should keep a clause allowing inventory to be reassigned when the event organiser changes its rules. Regulation○NeutralUS · Ad self-regulation

Eight major US operators write their own ad code: no campus placement, no framing gambling as a way to make money

CDC Gaming · 08/18

The Responsible Online Gaming Association ROGA published marketing and advertising standards on 18 August, adopted by all eight member companies, among them bet365, BetMGM, DraftKings, FanDuel, Hard Rock Digital and Penn Entertainment. The specific terms: no advertising on college campuses or targeting college students, no partnerships with amateur college athletes, and no language describing gambling as a way to invest or an easy way to make money.


Bottom line: this is self-regulation with nothing to enforce it, but it draws a line first and hands future legislators a ready-made text to copy — the same route the Premier League shirt ban took (see Signal 05). +EV for brands already building creative to this standard, −EV for brands converting on "guaranteed returns" and "easy doubles". In practice: "gambling must not be described as a way to invest" belongs directly in the creative review checklist, and it is easy to apply — if copy contains investment, yield, return or doubling the money, replace it. The same rule appears in the Australian, UK and Brazilian frameworks, so it holds as a cross-market minimum.

SBC Americas · Yogonet · 08/19–08/20

Florida attorney general James Uthmeier filed suit on 19 August in the Hillsborough County circuit court against online sweepstakes casino operators, naming Stake and VGW's Chumba Casino, LuckyLand and Global Poker as defendants and alleging breaches of Florida gambling law and the state's deceptive and unfair trade practices act. Bottom line: suing the platforms is routine. Suing the payment providers is not. The Philippines used the same logic in 2025 when it required GCash and Maya to disconnect from online gambling platforms, and licensed platforms lost about 70% of their players. −EV for grey-area sweepstakes casinos, +EV for licensed operators. In practice: when assessing a partner, payment-channel stability deserves the same weight as licence status. Ask whether their main payment provider still serves other clients under regulatory challenge; if it does, that channel can be cut off wholesale by the provider's own risk policy, regardless of the partner's own compliance record.

Regulation○Neutral📌 Editor's Pick

Five markets changed ad rules in one week, using five different instruments

📍 Editorial synthesis from Signal 01, Signal 05 and multiple §1 sources — no single source link

Australia used an act of parliament: fine-grained clauses, explicit penalties, in force January 2027. The Premier League used an internal league vote: no legal force, but every club voted together, so it binds like a rule. The Philippines is going through a congressional bill, still in committee, and local media have already started pulling back. The US went with industry self-regulation: eight members signed, none of them compelled. The Brazilian state of Minas Gerais used a state administrative decree, the fastest route of all — written, then executed.


Bottom line: these five instruments govern the same set of things — celebrity and influencer endorsement, exposure during live broadcast windows, how inducements are delivered, copy that frames gambling as a way to make money. They differ only in how fast they take effect and how hard they bite. Tighter ad rules are not one market's local quirk; each jurisdiction picks whichever route is fastest for it. In practice: rather than tracking legislative progress market by market, build one list of everything these five markets have already banned, assume every market bans it within two years, and order replacement development against that. The cost of working this way is giving up some channels while they still work. The cost of not working this way is the whole acquisition pipeline stopping the week a rule lands. The first can be planned around. The second cannot.

Operators○Neutral📌 Editor's Pick

Capital this quarter is concentrating in interactive; land-based development gives way

📍 Editorial synthesis from multiple §2 sources — no single source link

Bally's Intralot paid £243 million for Evoke's online assets. Bally's is weighing a sale of an US$1.1 billion land-based development and has split the build into three stages. The reason is practical: ad rules pushed acquisition costs up over the past two years, so building a market from zero takes longer to pay back, and buying something that already runs is quicker. +EV for mid-size online operators with a stable user base, which are the targets now; −EV for companies holding a licence and no users. In practice: any operator sitting on measurable user assets — active players, retention curves, cost per acquired customer — should spend this quarter turning those numbers into material it can show, because that is exactly what buyers are looking at.

Regulation−NegativePhilippines · Ad ban

If the Philippine ad ban passes, acquisition is down to owned apps

The Manila Times · Asia Gaming Brief · 08/17–08/19

Senate Bill 2347, the gambling advertising prohibition bill filed by Senate President Francis Escudero, gained visible momentum in Congress and in the media this week. The bill cuts licensed operators' advertising channels down to their own website and their own app: television, radio, print, online platforms and social media are all banned, and celebrity endorsement and influencer marketing are banned with them. Penalties reach operators, advertisers, media companies, platforms and company officers, with a first offence carrying up to PHP 200,000 (about NT$110,000) or up to one year in prison, and a third or later offence PHP 500,000 or three years. The bill allows a one-year transition.


Bottom line: this bill does more damage to acquisition than the minimum guaranteed fee does. The MGF raises the cost of staying in the market; the ad ban removes the means of bringing people in. And because the penalties reach media companies and platforms, local media and agencies will pull back on their own as soon as it passes, so enforcement starts earlier than the text does. −EV for brands whose main revenue comes from the Philippines. In practice: the share of current acquisition that runs through channels this bill would close should be calculated now, because that share is the exposure. What remains is the owned website, the owned app, and formats that are not advertising: player referral, CRM against existing lists, and unbranded content. All three take more than six months to build, so starting now fits the one-year transition.
