01 · LEAD Regulation ＋Positive US · Circuit split

Ninth Circuit rules states may regulate prediction markets as gambling, contradicting the Third Circuit and sending the question to the Supreme Court

CNN · CoinDesk · 08/28

The US Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals ruled 3-0 on 28 August that states have the authority to treat sports event contracts offered by platforms such as Kalshi (a prediction market platform licensed by the CFTC, the US Commodity Futures Trading Commission) as gambling. The case came out of Nevada, where the state required Kalshi to obtain a state licence first; all three judges on the panel were appointed during the Trump administration. On the same day a separate panel refused to let Kalshi resume trading sports and election contracts in Nevada while the litigation continues.

The ruling contradicts this year's Third Circuit decision, in which a panel held 2-1 that federal commodities law pre-empts state gambling law in New Jersey's enforcement case. With two circuits reaching opposite conclusions on the same question, only the Supreme Court can settle it. The CFTC has invoked emergency powers three times this year — after 46 years of disuse — to instruct prediction market platforms to disregard state regulators, including an August order requiring Kalshi to keep offering sports contracts in New York.


Bottom line: For eighteen months the argument has turned on one question: is an event contract a financial derivative or a bet? The Ninth Circuit's answer is that if the product looks like a sports bet, state gambling law reaches it, and a federal licence cannot be used to block state enforcement. The opposite conclusion in the Third Circuit makes Supreme Court review close to unavoidable, probably in the first half of 2027. Until then, prediction market platforms must operate under two conflicting lines of precedent, and legal and compliance costs will rise noticeably. +EV for licensed sportsbooks with state licences, whose competitors now carry more uncertainty; −EV for prediction market platforms. Execution: the reasoning matters more than the outcome — the court looked at what the product actually is, not how it is registered. The Philippines and Brazil have applied the same logic to offshore platforms. Any product entering a new market on an "we are an exchange, not a casino" positioning is more likely to be reclassified over the next two years, so evaluate partnerships on the worst-case assumption.

SIGNAL 02 · ASIA · PHILIPPINES

PAGCOR budget hearing: full-year income seen down 18%, with activity falling around 40% after e-wallet delinking

PAGCOR (the Philippine gaming regulator) Chairman Tengco told the House Committee on Appropriations on 24 August that total income for 2026 is expected to fall about 18% year on year to PHP86.9 billion (about US$1.41 billion, NT$47.8 billion). The main cause is the central bank order in 2025 requiring licensed online platforms to be delinked from e-wallets; activity dropped roughly 40% after it took effect. Pressure on lower-income consumers from the Middle East conflict has also hit the core user base of online platforms.He offered two signs of recovery: online activity rose about 10% in July and held at that level in August, and visitation and spend at integrated resorts picked up slightly from late July into early August. In the first half PAGCOR net income fell 85.3% year on year to PHP1.58 billion and total revenue fell 26.6% to PHP43.3 billion.


Bottom line: These numbers locate the problem precisely: demand did not disappear, the payment path from wallet to platform was cut, players now have extra steps to fund an account, and conversion fell accordingly. The July and August recovery shows demand is intact; how fast it returns depends on how smooth compliant deposit methods become. −EV for operators and suppliers whose revenue is concentrated in the Philippines; +EV for technology vendors that can deliver compliant deposit integration. Execution: for the remaining four months of the year the Philippine priority is the deposit flow itself, not acquisition creative. Pull the drop-off rate for every step from registration to first deposit — most of the recoverable loss sits there, not in advertising. Read original →

SIGNAL 03 · LATAM · BRAZIL

Brazilian right-wing bill would terminate every federal licence within 180 days, block sites and cut payment flows

Federal Deputy Caroline de Toni of the Liberal Party has filed Bill No. 5,153/2026, which would end legal online betting in Brazil: every federal licence issued by the SPA (the betting regulator under the Ministry of Finance) would be terminated within 180 days, betting sites would be blocked and financial flows to the sector suspended. The main target is fixed-odds sports betting. The Liberal Party, led by former president Bolsonaro's son Flávio Bolsonaro, is the largest opposition party with 91 of the 151 opposition seats.That puts pressure on the sector from both sides. The Lula government backs Bill No. 2,258/2026, which would ban online casino; the Liberal Party bill targets sports betting. Vice-presidential hopeful Alfredo Gaspar says the party has not decided between an outright ban and tighter regulation. De Toni's justification cites state fiscal bulletin estimates that BRL65.5 billion (about NT$380 billion) flows annually from Brazilian households to the betting sector, roughly 0.68% of household gross disposable income. Brazil elects a president, governors and Congress on 4 October.


Bottom line: As of this week, online betting has no clear defender left in the Brazilian Congress. The working assumption had been that casino would be banned and sports betting left alone; this bill puts sports betting in range too. Its odds of passing are lower than the casino ban, but it removes the assumption that a change of governing party would resolve the issue. Bills filed before an election rarely complete their passage, so the real risk sits with the Congress seated after October. −EV for online casino in Brazil; for sports betting brands the risk moves from negligible to something that has to be priced. Execution: review Brazilian contracts this quarter, focusing on termination and settlement terms if a licence lapses. The bill specifies 180 days — that is not enough time to renegotiate, only enough to execute what is already written. Anything absent now cannot be added later. Read original →

SIGNAL 04 · EUROPE · UK

Entain set to drop out of the FTSE 100, shares 42% below their 12-month high, confirmed 2 September

Entain, parent of Ladbrokes and Coral, is widely expected to be relegated from the FTSE 100 to the FTSE 250 in this quarter's index review. Shares traded at 517p on the morning of 27 August, down 2.3% on the day, valuing the company at about £3.3 billion (about NT$132 billion) — roughly 42% below the 52-week high of 916p and down about 30% year to date. FTSE Russell's indicative list already flags the removal; the decision is confirmed after the close on 2 September.The main driver is the UK government's November announcement raising remote gaming duty from 21% to 40% and online sports betting duty from 15% to 25%. Chief Executive Stella David has said the remote gaming duty change alone will cost the company £250 million (about NT$10 billion) across 2026 and 2027.


Bottom line: One tax change pushed a decades-old betting group out of a major index in under ten months. This is the first clear measurement of how fast tax policy transmits into valuation: announced in November, below the index threshold by the following September. Once out of the FTSE 100, index-tracking funds are forced sellers, which keeps pressure on the share price and raises the cost of capital. −EV for established UK betting groups; +EV for operators outside the UK tax base. Execution: before entering any market, model a doubling of the tax rate as a base scenario rather than a tail risk — the UK moved from 21% to 40%. The question to ask is whether current acquisition costs still pay back at the doubled rate. If not, the cost per acquired customer is already too high today. Read original →

Regulation○NeutralEstonia · Tax reversal

Estonian PM orders an early review of the online gambling tax cut: the promised new operators never arrived

SBC News · 08/28

Estonian Prime Minister Kristen Michal asked parliament on 28 August to bring forward the review of the phased cut in online gambling tax from 6% to 4%, citing the fiscal shortfall and difficulty finalising the 2027 budget. The Finance Ministry confirmed in June that no new online casino had entered the market since the policy passed, with two licence applications still under consideration. The ministry had warned the cut would reduce gambling tax receipts by about €6m in 2026, €8m in 2027, €10m in 2028 and €13m in 2029 if the expected influx failed to materialise. A drafting error earlier in the year briefly exempted some remote gambling from tax, corrected by parliament in February.


Bottom line: This is a clean counter-example: cutting tax to attract operators only works if the market is already attractive and tax is the last obstacle. Estonia has 1.3 million people; two percentage points does not compensate for market size, so the country got neither new entrants nor the tax it used to collect. −EV for operators already licensed in Estonia, since the cut could be withdrawn at any time. Execution: when assessing small markets, multiply addressable population by per-capita annual gambling spend, then set that against the fixed cost of licensing and compliance. If the arithmetic fails, no tax rate justifies entry — and the same test is the first filter for judging whether any headline tax incentive is durable.

Regulation○Neutral📌 Editor's pick

Three countries spent this week revisiting rules they wrote less than two years ago

📍 Editorial synthesis from Signals 03, 04 and §1 of this issue — no single source link

Estonia is reviewing a tax cut passed last year. The UK raised duties last November and by this September a major operator has fallen below the index threshold. Brazil's licensing regime only started in January 2025, and two parties have now filed bills to end it. In all three cases, less than two years separates the rule from the reconsideration.


Bottom line: Market planning used to assume a stable regulatory environment for three to five years, with investment amortised on that basis. That assumption no longer holds. The drivers differ — a revenue shortfall in Estonia, a tax target in the UK, election politics in Brazil — but the effect on operators is identical: the case made at entry gets rewritten within two years. Execution: use a two-year payback period for new markets, not three to five. Anything that only pays back over a longer horizon — proprietary payment rails, long-term sponsorships, large brand campaigns — should move to short terms or staged commitments with break clauses. Unit costs rise slightly; that is cheaper than having the whole commitment stranded by a rule change.

Gaming Corps put content into several brands within one group. Yggdrasil brought a small studio into its distribution programme. GiG used an existing platform to launch a new market. Each adds a distribution route on top of existing products and platforms; none is a new feature or format.

📍 Editorial synthesis from Signal 01 and §7 of this issue — no single source link
