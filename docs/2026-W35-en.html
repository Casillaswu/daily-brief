01 · LEAD Regulation ＋Positive US · Key defeat

Prediction markets lose the big one: US states can now use gambling law to shut them out

CNN · CoinDesk · 08/28

On 28 August three judges of the US Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals ruled unanimously against Kalshi. Kalshi holds a licence from the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) and sells contracts on questions like "will this team win". It has always argued it answers to federal financial law and that state gambling law cannot reach it. The court disagreed, holding that Nevada may require it to obtain a state licence first. The same day, a separate panel refused to let it resume Nevada trading while the case runs.

The awkward part is that the Third Circuit reached the opposite conclusion earlier this year in the New Jersey case, holding that federal financial law comes first and state law must give way. Two courts of the same rank, two answers to the same question — only the Supreme Court can settle it, probably in the first half of 2027. Meanwhile the CFTC is still backing the platforms: three times this year it has used an emergency power that had sat unused for 46 years, telling platforms to disregard state regulators. The August instance required Kalshi to keep selling sports contracts in New York.


Bottom line: Kalshi's "we are finance, not gambling" argument has been rejected head-on by a higher court for the first time. The court looked at what the product actually is, not which licence it holds. The weight of this is not the loss itself but the reasoning it hands every state to copy: previously a state could send a letter telling a platform to stop and the platform could ignore it; now states can obtain court orders, and ignoring one is contempt. Competitors lose ground over the next six months, +EV for sportsbooks holding state licences, −EV for prediction market platforms. The Philippines and Brazil apply the same test to offshore sites — what is the product actually doing — so any product entering a new market on an "we are an exchange, not a casino" positioning is markedly more likely to be reclassified over the next two years. Price that risk at the worst case when evaluating these partnerships.

SIGNAL 02 · ASIA · PHILIPPINES

What the Philippines lost this year was not players — it was the deposit path

PAGCOR (the Philippine gaming regulator) Chairman Tengco told the House appropriations committee on 24 August that the regulator's total income this year should come in about 18% below last year, at PHP86.9 billion (about US$1.41 billion, NT$47.8 billion). He named the cause plainly: the central bank last year required licensed platforms to be cut off from e-wallets, so players could no longer fund an account in one tap, and overall activity fell about 40% afterwards. Pressure on lower-income consumers from the Middle East situation hit the same group that makes up the core of online platform users.July online activity did recover by roughly 10% and held there in August; footfall and spend at integrated resorts also turned up from late July. In the first half the regulator's own net income fell 85.3% to PHP1.58 billion, and total revenue fell 26.6% to PHP43.3 billion.


Bottom line: A 40% drop is not demand disappearing. It is players having to take several extra steps to deposit, and conversion falling accordingly. The July and August recovery shows the appetite is still there; how much comes back depends on how smooth the compliant deposit route is. −EV for operators and suppliers whose revenue leans on the Philippines, +EV for technology vendors that can make compliant deposits work. For the remaining four months, what needs fixing is the deposit flow, not the ad creative: pull the drop-off rate at every step from registration to first deposit, because that is where the recoverable loss sits, not in media. SIGNAL 03 · LATAM · BRAZIL

Online betting in Brazil no longer has a party in Congress on its side

Liberal Party deputy Caroline de Toni filed Bill No. 5,153/2026: every federal licence issued by the betting regulator under the Ministry of Finance (SPA) would lapse within 180 days, with sites blocked and payment flows cut. The target is fixed-odds sports betting. The Liberal Party is the largest opposition party, holding 91 of the 151 opposition seats, and is led by former president Bolsonaro's son Flávio.That means both sides are now moving. The Lula government is pushing Bill No. 2,258/2026, which would ban online casino; this one goes after sports betting. Vice-presidential hopeful Alfredo Gaspar says the party has not decided between an outright ban and tighter rules. De Toni's justification cites state fiscal bulletins: BRL65.5 billion (about NT$380 billion) flows from Brazilian households into betting each year, roughly 0.68% of household gross disposable income. Brazil elects a president, governors and Congress on 4 October.


Bottom line: The working assumption was "casino banned, sports betting survives", and that the worst case was a change of governing party. That assumption is gone — the largest opposition party has now proposed taking sports betting with it. This particular bill is unlikely to pass, since pre-election bills mostly do not complete their passage; the real risk is how the Congress seated after October ranks its priorities. −EV for online casino business in Brazil; for sports-betting-only brands, the risk moves from "barely worth modelling" to "needs a number". Review Brazilian contracts this quarter, focusing on how termination and settlement work if a licence lapses — the bill says 180 days, and if it ever came to that, there is only time to execute what is written, not to renegotiate. Whatever is not in there now cannot be added later. Read original →

SIGNAL 04 · EUROPE · UK

Ten months after the UK raised duties, Entain is dropping out of the main index

Entain, parent of Ladbrokes and Coral, is all but certain to fall from the FTSE 100 to the FTSE 250 in this quarter's index review. Shares traded at 517p on the morning of 27 August, down 2.3% that day, leaving a market value of about £3.3 billion (about NT$132 billion) — 42% below the 916p high of the past year and down roughly 30% year to date. FTSE Russell's indicative list already marks it for removal, confirmed after the close on 2 September.The starting point was last November, when the UK government raised online casino duty from 21% to 40% and online sports betting duty from 15% to 25%. Chief Executive Stella David has said the casino duty change alone means £250 million (about NT$10 billion) more in tax across 2026 and 2027.


Bottom line: This is the first clear reading of how long a tax rise takes to reach the share price: announced last November, below the index threshold by this September. There is a second wave after the exit, as index-tracking funds are forced sellers, the price falls further and borrowing gets more expensive. A group decades old could not absorb one doubling of the rate — −EV for established UK betting groups, +EV for operators outside the UK tax base. Before entering any market, treat "the rate doubles within a year" as a case you have to model, not a tail risk: the UK went from 21% straight to 40%. The question to ask is whether current acquisition cost still pays back at the doubled rate. If it does not, the cost per acquired customer was already too high. Read original →

SIGNAL 05 · NORTH AMERICA · SPORTS BETTING

US operators will give up US$500 million this year rather than leave prediction markets to someone else

With the NFL season starting, licensed sportsbooks and prediction market platforms have both pushed advertising and acquisition budgets to their highest levels. DraftKings CEO Jason Robins says the company will put another US$200-300 million (about NT$6.2-9.3 billion) into its own prediction business this year. FanDuel will fold FanDuel Predicts into Flutter's single app before the regular season, alongside sports betting, casino and fantasy, and expects about US$50 million (about NT$1.55 billion) in market-making revenue from it this year. The NFL signed new multi-year sports betting deals with DraftKings, FanDuel and Fanatics this week, and said it will not work with any prediction market platform this season.


Bottom line: Operators will accept US$500 million less profit this year to hold the position; the league will only put its name next to licensed sportsbooks. Together those two decisions say prediction markets are treated as something that will stay, but not yet as a partner you can brand alongside. This season produces the first comparable data — how much the same people spend on sports bets versus event contracts. −EV for US licensed sportsbooks, which now carry a loss-making line on the cost sheet; +EV for content and advertising suppliers. This wave of spending will also lift US media prices and pull up rates in Latin America and Asia over the same period: if Q4 sports inventory outside the US is on the plan, lock the rate now rather than negotiating mid-season. Regulation○NeutralEstonia · Tax cut failed

A tax cut does not attract operators: Estonia dropped two points and not one new entrant came

SBC News · 08/28

Estonian Prime Minister Kristen Michal asked parliament on 28 August to bring forward the review of the online gambling tax cut from 6% to 4%, because receipts are short and the 2027 budget will not balance. The Finance Ministry confirmed in June that no new online casino had entered since the policy passed, with two applications still under review. The ministry had warned that if the entrants never arrived, the cut would cost €6m in receipts this year, €8m next year and €13m a year by 2029. A drafting error early in the year briefly left some remote gambling untaxed, corrected by parliament in February.


Bottom line: Whether a market appeals to operators is decided long before the tax rate; the rate is the last gate, not the first. Estonia has 1.3 million people, and two percentage points does not compensate for a market that small — so no new entrants arrived and the tax take fell. −EV for operators already licensed there, since the concession could be pulled at any time. For small markets, run one calculation first: addressable population times per-capita annual gambling spend, set against the fixed cost of licensing and compliance. If it does not clear, no tax rate makes it work — and that is also the first test of whether any headline tax incentive will last.

Regulation○Neutral📌 Editor's pick

Payback periods for new markets now have to be modelled over two years

📍 Editorial synthesis from Signals 03, 04 and §1 of this issue — no single source link

Estonia is reviewing a tax cut passed last year. The UK raised duties last November and by September a company has fallen out of the index. Brazil's licensing regime started in January 2025 and two parties have now filed bills to end it. The drivers are completely different — receipts short, a revenue target, an election — but in each case less than two years separates writing the rule from moving to overturn it.


Bottom line: Market planning used to assume regulation would hold for three to five years, with investment amortised on that basis. It no longer does: the case made at entry gets recalculated within two years, regardless of how mature the market is. So use a two-year payback for new markets, and move anything that only pays back over three to five years — proprietary payment rails, long-term sponsorships, large brand campaigns — onto short terms or staged commitments with break clauses. Unit costs go up a little; that is far cheaper than having the whole commitment stranded by a rule change.

evoke sells Africa, GiG buys back into B2C: one deal, two companies' priorities

SBC News · EGR Intel · iGaming Business · 08/26

GiG Software announced on 26 August that it is buying 80% of 888Africa from a subsidiary of evoke (formerly 888 Holdings, whose brands include William Hill and 888casino) for €16.4 million (about NT$590 million) — €6 million upfront, €10.4 million deferred. The founders keep the remaining 20% and stay on. GiG is raising €8.5 million (about NT$310 million) through a directed share issue and convertible loans to fund it, and guides to combined 2026 revenue of €44-48 million and adjusted profit of €5-7 million, with 888Africa counted only from the fourth quarter. Completion is expected by end-September. evoke is itself being acquired by Bally's Intralot; shareholders approved that £243 million deal in mid-August with over 99% in favour.


Bottom line: One deal, and both sides' priorities are visible. Mozambique passed its first dedicated online gambling framework in August, so taking over now means being in place before the detailed rules arrive. evoke is converting a non-core market into cash before its own takeover completes, keeping the established UK and European brands and letting the emerging-market operation go. Note that GiG is not cash-rich: a €16.4 million deal assembled from an €8.5 million raise plus deferred consideration, with the deferred portion over 60% — a ratio that usually signals the seller has reservations about future performance. +EV for mid-sized African operators, who now have a buyer; −EV for pure technology suppliers, as clients start running consumer businesses themselves. Leaving 20% with the founders and keeping them in place is standard for emerging-market deals, because local relationships and access to government do not transfer — whether the founding team stays predicts the first year after completion better than the financials do. Separately, companies in a takeover process shed non-core markets before completion, so if a partner is mid-acquisition, check whether your market is core to them.

Operator○Neutral📌 Editor's pick

Buying a company now means buying people and regulator access, not product

📍 Editorial synthesis from §2 of this issue — no single source link

GiG paid €16.4 million for 888Africa to get the African operating entity and the founders who stayed. Gokongwei put PHP2.02 billion into a Philippine company building compliance, risk and anti-fraud tools. Both capabilities are available on the open market; the technology is not what the premium was for.


Bottom line: Compliance requirements are increasingly specific to each jurisdiction, and compliance practice does not copy from one market to another — it only comes from people who have actually done it there. So the premium has shifted from product features to "a local team already running" plus "able to hold a conversation with the local regulator". +EV for mid-sized companies with a local operating record, who are now the ones being approached; −EV for teams with product but no landed experience. If you have a local team and a real record of regulatory dealings, this is the quarter to turn it into something presentable — licence maintenance history, inspection outcomes, a timeline of contact with regulators. That is what buyers read now, not the feature list.

Product○Neutral📌 Editor's pick

Supplier growth now comes from adding a distribution route, not building something new

📍 Editorial synthesis from §3 of this issue — no single source link

Gaming Corps put content into several brands within one group. Yggdrasil brought a small studio into its distribution programme. GiG used an existing platform to launch a new market. All three add a route on top of existing products and platforms; none is a new feature or format.


Bottom line: The reason is practical: compliance requirements are specific enough that a new game clears more approvals than it used to, while the marginal cost of adding a distribution route is close to zero. This holds until a clear product gap opens somewhere — +EV for platforms with many routes, −EV for single-product suppliers. In content procurement, the leverage is in the number of routes rather than the content: the same games are available from several sources, and what suppliers actually want is one more route to market, so trading distribution for price works better than trading volume for price.

Market○NeutralPhilippines · Licensing and enforcement

The regulator said it plainly: let the weak leave first, licences and fees stay put

Philstar · 08/27

PAGCOR Chairman Tengco said on 27 August there is no need to lift the freeze on new online gambling licences imposed in March 2024. The licensee count stays at 48 and fees are unchanged — 30% for e-games operators, 25% for integrated resorts. His words were that he wants the industry to consolidate first, let those who cannot cope leave, and then decide about new licences. At the same hearing he disclosed that PAGCOR had fined a licensed company PHP1 million (about NT$550,000) and ordered it to stop a promotion: a giveaway of 100 high-end handsets promoted by influencer Ivana Alawi.


Bottom line: A regulator saying openly that it wants the weaker operators out is an admission that the thresholds were built to remove them. The fine is the more interesting part — for the first time the Philippines penalised not the advertising content or the channel but the shape of the promotion itself: high-value physical prizes plus celebrity promotion to acquire customers, a combination already restricted under Australia's new law and UK rules. +EV for the larger operators among the 48, as share concentrates; −EV for sub-scale licensees, whose exit is a matter of timing. Partner selection in the Philippines happens inside this list of 48 and the list keeps shrinking, so judge on whether a partner survives consolidation rather than on this quarter's numbers. Promotion plans need a pre-check on four points: prize unit value, total prize value, entry conditions and who is promoting it. If any one resembles something already banned in Australia or the UK, assume the Philippines follows. The fine is small; the real cost is writing off finished assets and booked slots when a campaign is stopped.

Market○Neutral📌 Editor's pick

The Philippine problem is no longer the market — the regulator is short of money too

📍 Editorial synthesis from Signal 02 and §5 of this issue — no single source link

Income guidance down 18%, licences still frozen, fees unchanged, the regulator carrying PHP37 billion of its own debt, and enforcement now reaching the shape of promotions. Each has its own explanation; together they produce one result — the cost of staying only goes up, and the number of operators only goes down.


Bottom line: The difficult part of this combination is that the party raising the bar is itself short of cash. A regulator short of money does not ease off; it collects what it is owed more carefully and inspects more often. Licensees already established will pick up the share that concentrates; anyone still deciding whether to enter faces the highest cost and the worst position. Shift the Philippine focus this quarter from winning share to whether partners survive: rank current partners on monthly gross gaming revenue, deposit conversion and compliance record, and line up alternatives for those at the bottom before something goes wrong.

Brazil's federal revenue service reported BRL7.284 billion (about US$1.456 billion, NT$42.2 billion) collected from betting platforms in the first half of 2026, 83% more than the BRL3.8 billion a year earlier. What happens after October depends on how the new Congress trades those two off — banning it means giving up around BRL14 billion a year and dealing with traffic moving offshore. Keep the governor's remark in the external communications library: when explaining sector risk to Brazilian partners, media or payment providers, his own words carry far more weight than industry-commissioned research. Plan Brazil on two tracks: the tax-data track is that the market stays open with advertising and promotions tightening; the political track is online casino banned first and sports betting left. Both share shrinking advertising inventory, so moving budget toward owned channels and retention is work that is not wasted either way.

In Congress, a deputy from the largest opposition party wants every licence gone within 180 days. On the fiscal side, the revenue service reports first-half receipts up 83% and the central bank governor attributes household debt to credit cards. Same week, same government, opposite directions.

Sports betting needs 21, event contracts need 18 — four leagues want the gap closed

CNN Business · 08/28

Twenty-one is the standard floor for legal sports betting across US states, while event contracts sit under financial regulation with a floor of 18.


Bottom line: This is the hardest point for prediction markets to defend. The same match result requires 21 when packaged as a bet and 18 when packaged as a contract, and once that gap carries a dollar figure it is difficult to keep explaining as a difference in regulatory category. The leagues asking for it themselves turns a regulatory dispute into an industry consensus, and this will most likely change — −EV for prediction market platforms. Age verification is also the item regulators fine most readily in any market, because it is the cheapest to test: they try to register with an underage account and find out. Regulation○Neutral📌 Editor's pick

How far prediction markets shrink depends on court orders per state, not the Supreme Court

📍 Editorial synthesis from Signal 01 and §7 of this issue — no single source link

In order this week: an appeals court gave states a legal basis, one state obtained a hard blocking deadline, another sued with a three-way announcement, the leagues asked for the age floor to be raised, and the platform withdrew the products under review itself. The argument is no longer whether an event contract is gambling, but which procedure each state uses and how quickly. More than twenty states are now in litigation, over forty signed a letter opposing the platforms, and enforcement has moved from administrative cease-and-desist orders to court injunctions.


Bottom line: That shift matters for forecasting. The definitional question waits on the Supreme Court, perhaps eighteen months out; the enforcement question does not wait, because states are acting now and copying each other. A platform can ignore a cease-and-desist order; ignoring a court injunction is contempt. So what sets the near-term size of prediction markets is not how the Supreme Court rules but how many states obtain court orders over the next six months — competitors' addressable market shrinks materially in that window. Change the tracking metric from which court ruled which way to how many states obtained orders this quarter and what blocking periods they set: the second converts into market size, the first does not. The same test reads enforcement strength against offshore sites in the Philippines and Brazil — platforms generally withstand administrative orders and rarely last a year once the matter is in court.

Sports○NeutralUS · Reading the numbers

That "US$17 billion in August" figure for prediction markets is not comparable to betting handle

Covers · Next Event Horizon · 08/26

Kalshi and Polymarket together saw more than US$17 billion (about NT$527 billion) in sports trading volume in August, of which about US$416 million was football. Extrapolating from pre-season growth, football alone could pass US$4 billion in September. For scale: a single 2025 pre-season game drew about US$163,000, against about US$5.8 million for a comparable game in 2026.


Bottom line: The scale is real, but read the basis carefully. US$17 billion is trading volume, the value transacted between buyers and sellers; event contracts can be traded repeatedly, so the same money is counted more than once. Betting handle is what players actually stake — a different measure. The comparable figure is what the platform retains as revenue, and that is not public yet. When reading this kind of data, establish first whether the number is volume or handle, then find platform revenue. Comparing volume directly against handle overstates prediction markets, and market judgements built on it come out skewed.

Sports○Neutral📌 Editor's pick

By January there will be an evidence-based answer on whether to build one

📍 Editorial synthesis from Signal 05 and §8 of this issue — no single source link

Assessing what prediction markets do to sports betting has until now relied on inference. This year is different: the NFL regular season runs September to January, licensed sportsbooks and prediction market platforms are spending record marketing budgets on the same people, and operators' third and fourth quarter results will disclose both product lines separately.


Bottom line: Over the next five months these two categories go head to head for the first time in one season in front of one user base. The number that matters is not whose volume is bigger but, inside a single operator, how far revenue per customer and the retention curve differ between sports betting and event contracts. That gap will drive product investment decisions in 2027 and shape how regulators elsewhere classify event contracts. From September, record the disclosed figures for both lines at the major US operators in one table each quarter, with revenue per customer and retention as the key columns. By January there will be four comparable data sets, and the decision about building something similar in another market can rest on evidence rather than guesswork.
