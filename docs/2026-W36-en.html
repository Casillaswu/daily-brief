Five Headline Signals

 
01 · Lead Operators ○Neutral Europe · All-share merger

Lottomatica agrees a €2.8bn all-share merger with Cirsa, forming a group with over €4.4bn in revenue.

SBC News · next.io · Lottomatica filing · 09/02

On 2 September Italy’s Lottomatica and Spain’s Cirsa signed a binding merger agreement. Cirsa will be absorbed into Lottomatica through an EU cross-border statutory merger; the name and the Rome headquarters both stay. The exchange ratio is 0.668 new Lottomatica shares for each Cirsa share, valuing Cirsa at €16.55 per share, a premium of just over 21%, for a total of roughly €2.8bn. Existing Lottomatica holders end up with about 67.5% and Cirsa holders about 32.5%; Blackstone, Cirsa’s majority owner, takes roughly 24% and two of thirteen board seats.

Combined annual revenue exceeds €4.4bn, with pro-forma adjusted EBITDA (earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation) of about €2bn, making it the second-largest listed betting and gaming group behind Flutter (FanDuel’s parent, listed in London). The two sides expect about €115m of annual pre-tax cash synergies by the third full financial year after completion. Cirsa will pay a €262m extraordinary dividend before the merger takes effect, and Lottomatica’s board intends to propose a further €744m capital return afterwards.


Bottom line: Tax rates and advertising rules across Europe have kept moving for two years and mid-sized operators cannot absorb it, so this deal makes "merge before it gets worse" the mainstream answer. The €115m of savings does not come from selling more; it comes from running one platform across two countries and one trading team across both books. Negative EV for independent mid-sized European operators, positive EV for platform and content suppliers: two buyers become one, so individual contracts get bigger while there is one fewer party to negotiate against, and renewal terms will be squeezed. Anyone with European clients should start by listing where the two groups’ current suppliers overlap, because half of that overlap will be cut next year.

SIGNAL 02 · North America · State enforcement

A Michigan court bars Kalshi from sports contracts, with a $500,000 daily fine.

Michigan Attorney General Nessel obtained a preliminary injunction against KalshiEx on 2 September. Kalshi holds a licence from the US Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) and sells contracts on questions such as which team wins. The injunction requires geofencing that blocks everyone inside the state, with a $500,000 fine for each day of breach. Judge Aquilina of the 30th Circuit Court in Ingham County said in court that the operation is "a sports betting operation masquerading as an investment opportunity."This is the second order in the same case: a temporary restraining order was issued in June, and this converts it into an injunction that holds for the length of the litigation. Nessel filed in March, arguing Kalshi has no licence from the Michigan Gaming Control Board and is in breach of the state’s Lawful Sports Betting Act.


Bottom line: Last week the Ninth Circuit handed states a reasoning they could copy, and a week later a state turned it into money. The difference matters: when a state sent a letter, a platform could ignore it; ignoring a court order now costs $500,000 a day. Positive EV for licensed sportsbooks holding state permits, negative EV for prediction market platforms. The Philippines and Brazil apply the same test — what the product actually does — so any partnership pitched on being an exchange rather than a casino should be valued on the worst case. SIGNAL 03 · Asia · Philippines

Melco’s City of Dreams Manila weighs Philippine online entry, the last of four resorts without one.

Brokerage Seaport Research Partners, after meeting Melco Resorts management at its annual summer conference in late August, said City of Dreams Manila is evaluating entry into Philippine online gaming, with no decision yet. Of the four large resorts in Manila’s Entertainment City, Newport World Resorts, Okada Manila and Solaire already run online operations; only this one does not. Melco also said it would still sell the City of Dreams Manila operation at the right valuation.The online numbers are going the other way. PAGCOR (the Philippine Amusement and Gaming Corporation) published second-quarter data on 10 August showing electronic gaming revenue of PHP39.85bn, down 37.2% year on year, while licensed land-based casinos rose 2.9% to PHP45.4bn in the same quarter.


Bottom line: Online down 37% while land-based is up 2.9% points at distribution, not demand: since the central bank required licensed platforms to unlink from e-wallets last year, depositing takes several extra steps. The groups evaluating entry now are the ones that already have floor traffic and payment relationships, betting that the deposit problem gets fixed while the licence freeze keeps newcomers out. Negative EV for pure online operators with no land-based assets: once all four resorts are live online, they can move members from the floor, and bargaining power over acquisition cost and payment rails shifts to them. SIGNAL 04 · LatAm · Brazil

Lula says two thirds of the betting decision is made, with no operator at the 1 September hearing.

On 1 September Lula (Brazil’s current president) met religious bodies, business groups and artists at the presidential palace and said that if it were up to him he would end the betting business outright, that the government is close to a more drastic measure, and that "two thirds" of the decision is already taken, with the rest depending on hearing society. No betting company was invited. Groups attending told the press beforehand they would ask for advertising restrictions, operating limits on platforms, and public risk awareness campaigns. Lula said he would meet his own team on betting regulation later the same week.This is a separate track from congress: there the largest opposition party has filed a bill to void federal licences, while the executive can change advertising and operating rules without waiting for congress. Brazil elects its president, state governors and congress on the same day, 4 October.


Bottom line: A hearing held for the opposing side with no operators present means the conclusion is written and what remains is procedure. An executive decree on advertising does not go through congress and can land within weeks, far faster than a bill. Negative EV for Brazilian advertising and club sponsorship budgets: exposure is hit before licences are. Anyone holding Brazilian contracts should pull the advertising and sponsorship agreements this month and check for a regulatory-change termination clause and how prepaid amounts are refunded, because what is not written in now cannot be added later. On the creative side, prepare a version with brand exposure removed and keep it ready. Read original →

SIGNAL 05 · North America · Product

Fanatics merges sportsbook, casino and prediction markets across three state footprints.

On 2 September Fanatics launched Fanatics Sports & Casino, folding three separate products into one app with a single account, a shared FanCash balance and one Fanatics ONE loyalty programme. Existing users migrate by updating, keeping balances and loyalty status.The point is that the three footprints differ: the sportsbook covers 23 states plus Washington DC, the casino only four states, and prediction markets 24 states and four US territories. Merged into one app, users in California, Texas, Florida, New York and Pennsylvania — none of which license online sportsbooks — open it and still find something to play.


Bottom line: Prediction markets in the US have moved from a legal question to a shelf-space question. Using the prediction market footprint to reach states with no sportsbook licence puts them all behind one account, so acquisition can run nationally instead of being cut state by state. Positive EV for licensed operators holding several licence types, negative EV for single-category prediction market platforms. The Philippines and Brazil will meet this structure within two years: whether one account can share a balance and a KYC (know your customer) record across licence categories is a question to put to platform suppliers now, because waiting for the regulator to raise it leaves no time to rebuild. Hungary reviews abolishing regulator SZTFH over an untendered deal.

SBC News · Magyar Közlöny · 08/31

A resolution published in Hungary’s official gazette on 31 August instructs the government to examine how SZTFH — which supervises gambling, tobacco and mining — could be abolished and its powers transferred to bodies under direct government control, with the justice minister to report back by 30 September. The trigger was SZTFH granting CAI Hungary Kft, a company linked to businessman Garancsi, a 35-year concession for the Sopron casino running to 2061, without a public tender. The prime minister said the body "served private interests."


Bottom line: Half of a licence’s value comes from the stability of whoever issued it. Compliance can be perfect and still leave room for existing licence terms to be reinterpreted once the issuing body is dismantled and its powers move into a ministry. Negative EV for operators already licensed in Hungary. When assessing smaller EU markets, alongside the rules themselves, check how long the regulator has existed and whether it has a political history — a factor rarely priced in before, and one that has now come up twice this year.

Regulation○Neutral📌 Editor’s Pick

Hungary swaps regulator, Britain stops a new licence, Mexico taxes 50%.

📍 Editorial synthesis from §1 and §6 of this issue

Three markets each produced a different failure this week: Hungary wants to dismantle its licensing body, Britain stopped a brand five months after it went live, and Mexico has raised tax to 50% while its governing statute still dates from 1947. There is no shared cause, but the result is the same — holding a licence is not the same as holding certainty.


Bottom line: Market assessment used to run on tax rates and licence fees, both calculable. There is now a third item that is not: how long the rules stay unchanged. Britain went from 21% to 40% in a year, and Hungary’s licensing body could be gone within a month. Set payback inside two years in the investment model, and keep any assumption longer than that out of the base case.

trigger-2026-09-05.md (second pass) · focusgn · 09/04

The Secretariat of Prizes and Betting fined Brilliant Gaming R$929,700 under article 41(III) of Law 14.790/2023. With nobody found at the registered address, notice was served by publication; if unpaid within ten days the debt goes to the federal attorney’s office for the national debtors register. This is neither Pixbet nor part of the batch of 14 suspensions the SPA issued on 18 August.


Judgment at the time: This runs on the same mechanism as the CONAR conclusion in the 08-28 correction line — responsibility pushed toward the operator and travelling down the contract — except that CONAR is self-regulatory with no power to fine, while Procon and the SPA can impose administrative penalties. Put together, Brazilian operators have a clear motive before the end of September to attach compliance clauses and creative record-keeping obligations to sponsorship contracts. Writing exit clauses early and keeping compliance records of creative remains the asymmetric right move (positive EV: low cost, and being wrong only means the clause goes unused).

One week on: No follow-up on payment or referral a week on. The "first SPA fines targeted identity verification" card in §6 covers the technical execution side; this case adds the other pattern, where the company itself cannot be found and notice goes by publication. Whether a Brazilian counterparty can actually be reached at its registered address is worth adding as a due diligence item.

Markets−NegativeBrazil · Revocations and fines

Brazil’s SPA has revoked or suspended 26 operators over verification.

Bright Side of News · SPA/MF · early September

By mid-year Brazil’s Secretariat of Prizes and Betting had revoked or suspended authorisation for 26 operators, while more than 180 brands held valid authorisations. The first wave of fines under Ordinance 722 hit two situations: "ghost" accounts, where a player registers with a valid CPF (tax number) but never completes a biometric liveness check; and self-exclusion leaks, where the national register did not take effect fast enough and players could still bet seconds after being added. Penalties run to BRL 2bn, plus revocation, a ten-year bar on acquiring new holdings and disqualification from directorships.


Bottom line: Both fined items are technical execution — whether liveness checking is wired in, and how often the exclusion register syncs. Contracts usually put those on the platform supplier’s side, but the fine goes to the licence holder. Negative EV for licensed operators, positive EV for technology suppliers doing KYC and self-exclusion sync. When contracting with a Brazilian platform, write the exclusion register sync interval as a number of seconds rather than "real time", because "real time" means nothing in a hearing.

Markets○NeutralBrazil · Blocking widens

Anatel has blocked 39,000 sites, now covering prediction markets.

Convergência Digital · Agência Gov · early September

Brazil’s national telecommunications agency (Anatel), working with the finance ministry’s campaign against illegal markets, has blocked more than 39,000 illegal betting addresses, 15,000 of them in the first half of the year. That blocking logic now covers prediction market platforms under the same standard. At a congressional hearing Anatel said enforcement faces technical obstacles — VPN use hides the final destination — and asked for its statutory powers to be widened.


Bottom line: Brazil files prediction markets in the same category as betting, which is a different approach from the American jurisdiction fight: the US argues about which level of government decides, Brazil blocks on what the product is. Negative EV for platforms hoping to enter Brazil on a prediction market positioning. It also means "we are an exchange, not a casino" has no room for discussion in LatAm. If a partner brings that positioning to a Brazilian conversation, assess it as unlicensed.

Policy○NeutralTrigger 4 · BR funding dispute

The Senate cut the 30% betting levy for public security.

trigger-2026-09-05.md (first pass) · iGaming Brazil · 09/04

The Senate constitution and justice committee approved Rogério Carvalho’s new report on 2 September, deleting article 139 of PEC 18/2025, which would have routed 30% of fixed-odds betting revenue to the FNSP and FUNPEN. On 4 September police organisations publicly opposed the decision.


Judgment at the time: Triggers 1 and 4 read together are more interesting than either alone — the justice ministry has just built the process that puts illegal betting money into the FNSP, and in the same week the Senate cut the clause that would have put legal betting money into it. One week on: No movement a week on; the judgment stands. Signal 04 in this issue happened slightly later the same week — the executive raising its own stakes while congress cuts the funding clause are two sides of the same board and should be read together.

Markets○NeutralMexico · Old law, new tax

Mexico’s online market nears $970m under a 1947 law taxed at 50%.

next.io · iGaming Business · 09/02

Mexico’s online gambling market is estimated at about US$970m this year, with a forecast compound annual growth rate of 8.6% to 2031, and World Cup betting volumes more than four times those of Qatar 2022. The governing statute is still the 1947 Federal Law on Games and Raffles, with regulations from 2004 amended once in 2023. The IEPS excise on iGaming has gone from 30% to 50%, and since January both domestic and foreign platforms must register with the federal taxpayer registry, withhold and file monthly. The government said in late 2024 it would draft a new law; the process entered working groups in early 2025 and has not moved since.


Bottom line: Tax first, law later is the hardest combination to work with: cost is certain to rise while the rules stay undefined, so compliance cannot be built into the product. Positive EV for operators playing short, negative EV for anyone building for the long term. Mexico now suits being treated as a market you can walk away from — media spend and creative can go in, but do not tie development work or long contracts to it, because a new law resets everything.

Markets−Negative📌 Editor’s Pick

Brazil is tightening on four fronts at once, none synchronised.

📍 Editorial synthesis from Signal 04 and §1, §6 of this issue

The president says two thirds of the decision is made and wants advertising limited; the secretariat is drafting licensing detail while issuing fines and revocations; the telecoms agency is widening blocking and asking for more powers; and on 4 September the consumer protection system set up a federal working group and opened checks on 36 firms at state level (see Triggers 5 to 7 in this issue). Each has its own timetable and there is no single effective date. With the 4 October election on top, nobody can say in what order the rules land before year-end.


Bottom line: Rules arriving on four tracks is difficult less because they are stricter than because they cannot be scheduled — any one of them can land without notice. The workable approach in Brazil is to split operations into parts that can be stopped separately: advertising, sponsorship, payments and content each able to halt on their own rather than bundled into one launch plan. The concrete task this month is to pull the termination clause from every Brazilian contract into one table and mark which need 30 days’ notice and which are immediate.

Regulation○NeutralUS · Federal sues states

The CFTC has sued nine states, arguing federal law overrides state law.

DLA Piper · Steptoe · CFTC releases · early September

The Commodity Futures Trading Commission sued nine states through the year: Arizona, Connecticut and Illinois on 2 April, Wisconsin in late April, then New York, New Mexico, Minnesota and Rhode Island, and Kentucky on 23 June. The argument is the same each time, that state enforcement against prediction market platforms is preempted by the Commodity Exchange Act. The CFTC separately reaffirmed exclusive jurisdiction in a filing to the Massachusetts Supreme Judicial Court, and on 10 June proposed amendments to Rule 40.11.


Bottom line: A federal regulator suing state governments is itself the sign that there is no settled answer. Nine suits on top of more than twenty states already in litigation ends at the Supreme Court. Negative EV for prediction market platforms: whoever wins, the number of open states changes every quarter until judgment, so the product cannot be planned nationally. What this means in Asia and LatAm is that the reasoning will be quoted but the conclusion will not — the Philippines and Brazil have no federal-state split, so how the product is characterised is the final answer.

Regulation○NeutralUS · Two opposite answers

Minnesota’s July ruling went the opposite way to Michigan’s.

Court records · Bloomberg Law · 07/27, 09/02

On 27 July a federal judge found Minnesota’s felony ban on prediction markets likely preempted by the Commodity Exchange Act, granting Kalshi, Polymarket and the CFTC a preliminary injunction. On 2 September a Michigan state court went entirely the other way, granting the injunction to the state and attaching a $500,000 daily penalty (see Signal 02). The difference is that one went through a federal court and the other through a state court.


Bottom line: The same product drew opposite rulings in two states six weeks apart. Which court level a case is filed in decides the outcome more than the facts do, which is why state attorneys general now prefer state courts. Negative EV for prediction market platforms: litigation cost rises linearly with the number of states rather than one win covering the country. The number worth tracking is not the reasoning but how many states move each month from warning letter to court order, because that is the speed at which the footprint shrinks.

Regulation○Neutral📌 Editor’s Pick

The prediction market fight now runs on which government decides.

📍 Editorial synthesis from Signal 02, Signal 05 and §7 of this issue

Three things this week read together: a federal regulator suing state governments, a state court attaching a daily fine, and Fanatics folding prediction markets into its main app as one more shelf position. Nobody is arguing about characterisation any more — a judge said in open court that it is sports betting dressed up as investing. The fight is over who has the power to act.


Bottom line: Agreement on what it is and disagreement on who governs it means the product keeps selling while the map is redrawn every quarter. For anyone outside the US there is one transferable point: courts look at what the product actually does, not what it is registered as. Any plan resting on a change of legal framing to enter a market should be assessed on the assumption that the wrapper comes off — a test that applies equally in the Philippines and Brazil, where there is not even a litigation stage.

Sports○Neutral📌 Editor’s Pick

Sponsorship has moved from shirt fronts to shorts and regional leagues.

📍 Editorial synthesis from §5, §6 and §8 of this issue

Britain removed shirt fronts, Brazil’s president is preparing advertising limits, and a Philippine legislator has filed a ban on online gambling ads. The methods differ but the thing being compressed is the same: where a brand can be seen. The operator response is also consistent — keep the budget, change the placement.


Bottom line: When exposure channels compress, money moves from buying placements to owned channels and content. For creative teams that means two things: prepare every campaign in a branded and an unbranded version, and shift content’s job from attracting new customers to holding existing ones, with the measure changing from reach to return visits. In these three markets in the second half, the teams with both versions ready will lose two or three fewer weeks of airtime than their competitors.
