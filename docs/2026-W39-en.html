01 · LEAD Regulation −Negative Brazil · Decree in force

Lula's 25 September decree bans sports betting and online casino in Brazil, with every site offline by 6 October.

planalto.gov.br full text · Reuters (via MarketScreener) 09/25 · Bilyonaryo 09/26 · Agência Brasil · iGaming Brazil · Metrópoles · 09/25–09/27

On 25 September Lula (Brazil's sitting president) signed Provisional Measure No. 1,394 (a medida provisória takes effect on signature and lapses unless Congress votes on it within 120 days), published the same day in an extra edition of the official gazette. Article 1 bans the operation, offer, intermediation and advertising of fixed-odds betting, online and physical, including offshore operators serving Brazilians; the same article expressly covers real sports events and virtual online games. The line circulating for two weeks, that only online casino would be banned and sports betting kept, was overturned by the text. Licences issued by the states and the Federal District also lapse (Art. 2).

The timeline, counted from the text: deposits stopped on 25 September and advertising and sponsorship were banned immediately (Art. 16); by about 5 October all physical and digital ads and sponsor branding must come down and sites and apps go offline (Art. 7); around 25 October the licences are extinguished, and the R$30 million (about NT$170 million) that each of the 85 licensees paid is not refunded, with no state compensation (Art. 4). Platforms face fines of 10% of group revenue in Brazil or up to R$50 million per violation (Art. 22), and banks and Pix must stop processing betting transactions under central bank rules still to come (Arts. 14–15). The Finance Ministry estimates about R$1.7 billion still sits in player accounts. Trade body ANJL and representatives of about 180 operators plan a joint Supreme Court suit by 29 September. Reuters (25 September) cites a Finance Ministry estimate that Brazilian households spend about R$60 billion (about NT$340 billion) a year on online betting while the industry pays about R$10 billion in tax, the contrast behind the government's public-health case for a full ban. Philippine business outlet Bilyonaryo, republishing the story on 26 September, added that the Philippines, with fast-growing online gambling and tightening rules, could draw attention to the precedent.


Bottom line: The text is heavier than the market expected on two points: sports betting is in scope, and licence fees are not refunded. The earlier call to leave sportsbook creative untouched is void from 25 September. −EV for every Brazilian betting brand and partner: stop all ads, KOL placements and sponsorship exposure now, and do not bet on Art. 17's carve-out for pre-existing content. For the Philippine business the effect is indirect: DigiPlus's Brazilian licence lapses around 25 October, so budget meant for Brazil may flow back to the Philippines (see §5 Trigger 2); but Philippine business media have already linked Brazil's ban to the Philippines (Bilyonaryo, 26 September), handing Philippine ban advocates a large-market example, so comments to BSP before 2 October should use Brazil's 428 new illegal sites in three days to show what a full ban costs (see §5 Trigger 1 and §6). Three jobs for this week: review every Brazilian contract's regulatory-change and force majeure clauses, because the state pays nothing and losses can only be recovered through contracts; inventory all Brazilian placements and branding before 5 October; and track the Supreme Court case number and any injunction, which most lawyers think is unlikely before the election.

SIGNAL 02 · US · 1–2 AT THE CIRCUITS

The Sixth Circuit let Ohio and Tennessee apply gambling law to Kalshi, which is now 1–2 at the circuit courts.

On 26 September a three-judge Sixth Circuit panel ruled unanimously that Kalshi (a prediction market regulated by the US Commodity Futures Trading Commission, CFTC) had not shown its sports event contracts are swaps under the Commodity Exchange Act, and that even if they were, federal law would not displace state gambling law. The ruling upheld Ohio's denial of an injunction and threw out the injunction a Tennessee court had granted Kalshi. The opinion cites Kalshi's own earlier admission that its sports contracts have no inherent economic significance. Kalshi is now 1–2 at the circuit level: a win in the Third Circuit (New Jersey), losses in the Ninth (Nevada) and Sixth, with Maryland pending in the Fourth. New Jersey's cert petition drew a supporting brief from gaming-state legislators this week; Kalshi's response is due 9 November.


Bottom line: The more the circuits disagree, the likelier the Supreme Court takes the case, with a final answer in the first half of 2027 at the earliest. Until then Ohio and Tennessee can enforce against Kalshi, and state licences keep their value. +EV for state-licensed sportsbooks, −EV for prediction markets relying on federal status to operate across states. For anyone writing US creative, claims that prediction markets are legal nationwide can no longer appear in public copy. Read original →

SIGNAL 04 · MACAU · PREMIUM PULLBACK

UBS puts Macau's early-September daily GGR at MOP610 million, 14% below August, as a trust tax hits premium play.

UBS counted average daily GGR of MOP610 million (about NT$2.4 billion) for 1–20 September, flat year on year and about 14% below August, short of the MOP650–670 million consensus; the seasonal drop is steeper than the 5% average of 2015–2019. Mass fell 13% to 16% a day and VIP volume 15% to 18%. UBS ties the premium weakness to China's 20% tax on appreciation of offshore trust assets, with back taxes due by 22 October. Citi's survey showed observed premium-mass wagers down 45% year on year and cut its September forecast by 10% to MOP18 billion. On the other side, GGRAsia found 21 of 36 resort hotels sold out for at least five nights of October Golden Week.


Bottom line: It is premium players who pulled back, for tax rather than economic reasons, so Golden Week crowds will not fill that gap, and the September figure due 1 October will probably miss the optimistic forecasts. −EV for Macau's premium business. For the Philippines, money from the same pool of Asian high-end players is tightening too, so do not overestimate a VIP recovery at Solaire and other resorts. In regional budgets, plan Q4 premium acquisition on the conservative case. Read original →

SIGNAL 05 · EU · ADS AND AFFILIATES

Bulgaria's ruling party drafted a near-total gambling ad ban that ends all affiliate licences on 1 January 2027.

Prime Minister Roumen Radev's party published a Gambling Act rewrite for consultation until 23 October. It bans gambling ads on TV, radio, print, outdoor, online, social, apps and streaming, leaving operators to communicate only on their own sites and in their venues; casino signage is capped at 20% of the façade and 50 square metres, at least 10% of it an addiction warning. Club and league sponsorship survives, but digital broadcast overlays are banned. All affiliate licences end on 1 January 2027, though Bulgaria only opened this licence category in August 2026. A follow-up published on 25 September adds rules aimed at payment providers.


Bottom line: This is among the strictest ad drafts in the EU, and it even revokes affiliate licences issued four months ago, so the open-then-close cycle is getting shorter. A UK House of Lords committee urged a full ad ban in September and Brazil's Art. 16 banned ads and sponsorship outright: all three moved first on ads and affiliates. −EV for brands relying on affiliate and KOL traffic, +EV for owned traffic and membership. Work out what share of revenue comes through affiliates and KOLs; the higher it is, the more budget should move to owned channels. Read original →

Europe tightened enforcement this week, and Brazil banned outright.

📍 Editorial synthesis from §1, Signal 01 and Signal 05 — no single source link

The UK suspended small operators, France fined individual managers for the first time, Germany asked for criminal prosecution of black-market operators, Bulgaria's draft revokes affiliate licences, and Brazil shut its entire market by decree. Revenue concentrated in one market now drew a deeper discount.

📍 Editorial synthesis from §2 and Signal 01 — no single source link

Product○Neutral🆕 Malta · AI charter

Malta's MGA issued an AI charter that warns against AI washing.

iGaming Expert · 09/21

The Malta Gaming Authority (MGA), working with the Malta Digital Innovation Authority, published its first AI Gaming Charter. It is a voluntary, principles-based framework with no legal obligations, but it will inform future policy under GDPR, the EU Data Act and the EU AI Act. It warns B2C and B2B licensees against "AI washing" (overstating what their AI can do), calls for diverse and representative training data and bias testing, and asks for channels where players can request human review and redress; back-office automation is treated as low impact.


Bottom line: The charter points the same way as Massachusetts' review of AI-driven sportsbook promotions this week (see §7): AI in the back office is fine, but AI personalising offers to players gets scrutinised. −EV for operators using AI for personalised promotions and CRM. For creative, any copy saying "AI picks" or "AI recommended" needs a documented explanation of how the feature actually works.

Regulation○NeutralTrigger 1 · Payments draft

BSP payment draft comments close 2 October amid de-risking fears.

trigger-2026-09-21.md · Inside Asian Gaming · 09/20

Samuel Tarng, COO of Philippine gaming law adviser Arden Consult, said the central bank (BSP) draft, an amendment to its Manual of Regulations for Payment Systems (MORPS), requires visibility of merchants, beneficiaries, ultimate owners and settlement accounts, and lets casinos and betting operators connect only as direct merchants, not through aggregators. The direction is right, but careless implementation could lead banks and payment firms to reject the whole industry and push money into channels no one can see. BSP has already closed more than 8,000 disguised merchant accounts collecting for online casinos.


Judgment at the time: The draft has not moved; the industry argument has. The 2 October comment deadline is the only lever, and Arden's case that de-risking feeds the black market is exactly the framing licensed operators need. The +EV move is to use it as material and submit comments through an industry association or payment partner before 2 October: the cost is minimal, and if the payment layer is cut wholesale, deposits suffer, not advertising.

One week on: No change in a week: BSP's page still shows a 2 October deadline and an empty publication field, so the 15-calendar-day clock after publication has not started. The judgment stands, with five days left. This week Brazil's Arts. 14–15 banned banks and Pix from processing betting outright, and 428 illegal sites appeared in three days after the ban (see §6), a ready counterexample for the submission.
