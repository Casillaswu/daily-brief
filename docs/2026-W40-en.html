01 · LEAD Regulation −Negative Brazil · Ban enforced

Brazil blocks bet payments, sites go dark on 6 October, and the AGU asks the Supreme Court to strike down the betting laws.

BNLData · Poder360 · iGaming Brazil · Focus Gaming News · 09/28–10/03

Provisional Measure No. 1,394 (the total ban signed on 25 September) moved into enforcement this week. Brazil's central bank (BCB) signed Resolutions 595, 596 and 597 on 30 September and published them in the official gazette on 2 October. They bar TED transfers, boleto payment slips and Pix payments to betting platforms, with a single exception for refunds to existing customers, putting Articles 14 and 15 of the measure into effect. Ordinance 3,005 from SPA, the finance ministry's betting secretariat, makes operators take player balances as of 23:59:59 on 5 October, report them by CPF (individual tax number) by 7 October, and settle all tax, payment and reporting obligations by 15 December. Banks refund players from 9 to 14 October, and anything they cannot return goes to an account at state bank Caixa. At a 2 October briefing the finance and justice ministries said player balances had fallen from R$2.1 billion to R$1.453 billion (about NT$8.2 billion) across 28.65 million CPFs; about R$600 million a day flowed into platforms before the ban and the figure is now close to zero; more than 10,000 sites have been sent to telecoms regulator Anatel for blocking; and the Federal Police will set up a unit dedicated to illegal betting.

On the same day the AGU (the office that represents the federal government in court) changed its position in ADI 7749, a constitutional challenge brought by the prosecutor-general. It now asks the Supreme Court to declare the core of Law 13,756 of 2018 and Law 14,790 of 2023 unconstitutional. Those two laws are the legal basis of regulated betting in Brazil. The AGU cites a 140% rise in gambling-disorder cases in the public health system (SUS) from 2018 to 2025. On the other side, Legitbet, the monitoring platform of operator association ANJL, counted 6,401 new illegal betting sites from 22 to 28 September, an average of 914 a day against 54 a day in early September. Congressional amendments have reached 40 and the deadline has moved to 13 October; today, 4 October, is the first round of the general election.


Bottom line: The biggest new variable is the AGU filing. If the Supreme Court accepts it, regulated betting loses its legal basis even if Congress lets the measure lapse, so the path of waiting for Congress to rescue the market gets narrower. Payments were cut before the sites went dark, so the 5 October withdrawal deadline and the 6 October shutdown will not move. −EV for every Brazilian betting brand and partner: amendments and the Supreme Court suspension requests are the only variables left, neither has produced a ruling, and neither can be an operating assumption. For the Philippines, Brazil shutting Pix, TED and boleto at once, followed by 6,401 new illegal sites in a week, is a full case study of what an overly strict version of the BSP payments draft would do, and it can be cited before the BSP finalises its rule (see §5). Two things to finish this week: confirm every Brazilian placement and sponsor mark is down by 5 October, and chase any revenue share or prepayment owed by Brazilian partners before the 15 December settlement deadline.

SIGNAL 02 · BRAZIL BAN · EARNINGS HIT

Flutter sees a US$70 million revenue hit from Brazil, Allwyn drops its 37% margin guide and Better Collective suspends guidance.

Flutter said on 28 September it had closed its Betfair and Betnacional businesses in Brazil. If they stay shut to year-end, 2026 revenue falls by about US$70 million (about NT$2.2 billion) and adjusted EBITDA (an operating profit measure) by about US$20 million, and it is reviewing a write-down of the US$539 million of goodwill on the Brazilian business. Its shares hit an intraday low of US$74.39 on 29 September, the lowest since March 2020, and DraftKings fell to its lowest since April 2023 the same day. Entain kept its full-year EBITDA guidance of £910–960 million but expects the low end, and cut online NGR (net gaming revenue) growth to 4–6%; Brazil was about 5% of online NGR. Allwyn, which owns 36.75% of Betano's parent, said its 37% margin guidance no longer applies if the ban lasts the year, and Betano is weighing a lawsuit to protect its five-year licence. Affiliate Better Collective suspended its 2027–28 guidance and its buyback; Brazil had been expected to bring in about 12% of its 2026 revenue. On the B2B side, Kambi called Brazil a low single-digit share of revenue, and Pareto expects a low-to-mid single-digit short-term revenue hit at Evolution.


Bottom line: For the big groups Brazil is a loss they can absorb: Flutter's US$70 million is under 0.5% of roughly US$17.9 billion in annual revenue. The hardest hit are affiliates and media companies whose revenue is concentrated in Brazil. −EV for affiliates and KOL agencies heavily exposed to Brazil, limited impact on diversified groups. Better Collective pulling its guidance first means the affiliate layer will cut partnerships and budgets next; anyone with a revenue-share deal with such partners should check settlement cycles and minimum-guarantee clauses now. SIGNAL 03 · US · CFTC RULES

The CFTC sent the White House two swap rules, one effective on publication, that may change how prediction markets are regulated.

On 1 October the US Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) sent two rules to the White House Office of Management and Budget (OMB) for review. One is an interim final rule that excludes casino-style gambling products from the definition of a swap; it takes effect the day it is published in the Federal Register, with no comment period. The other is a proposed rule that brings event contracts into the swap definition. Neither text is public yet. The same week, prosecutors in the Southern District of New York argued in a Polymarket insider-trading case that binary event contracts are swaps, with a hearing on 7 October; Czechia listed Kalshi as unlicensed gambling, with blocking from 15 October (see §1); and Kalshi is reportedly raising about US$1 billion (about NT$32 billion) at a valuation near US$40 billion, according to Reuters, up from US$22 billion in May.


Bottom line: Kalshi has one win and two losses in the circuit courts, and the CFTC is now trying to fix that through rulemaking. If the new rules clearly place sports event contracts under federal jurisdiction, room for state enforcement shrinks; carving out casino-style products draws a line between prediction markets and gambling first. Lawyer Daniel Wallach expects a lawsuit under the Administrative Procedure Act as soon as the rules are published. Short-term −EV for US state-licensed sportsbooks (federal rules may override state law), +EV for prediction markets. Change no US partnership until the text is out, and watch for the end of OMB review. Read original →

SIGNAL 04 · MACAU · SEPTEMBER GGR

Macau's September GGR fell 1.2% to MOP18.06 billion, the year's weakest month, and J.P. Morgan cut its full-year forecast.

Macau's gaming regulator DICJ reported September gross gaming revenue of MOP18.06 billion (about NT$71 billion) on 1 October, down 1.2% year on year and about 17.5% below August, the fourth straight monthly decline. Third-quarter GGR was MOP60.21 billion, 1.3% below the second quarter, and January–September reached MOP187.12 billion, up 3.2%. September averaged MOP602 million a day, the lowest since September 2024. J.P. Morgan cut its 2026 forecast to MOP249 billion (1% above 2025, about 85% of pre-pandemic) and expects October and the fourth quarter each to fall 6%; Seaport expects another 5% drop in the fourth quarter. Both cite China's pursuit of tax on offshore trusts. Golden Week day one (1 October) brought 169,600 visitors, up 22.3%, with mainland visitors about 86%.


Bottom line: Rising visitor numbers alongside falling revenue mean what dropped is premium players' wagering, not footfall. That gap is unlikely to close before the 22 October offshore-trust back-tax deadline. −EV for Macau premium and VIP business, smaller impact on operators focused on mass and non-gaming revenue. For the Philippines, the same pool of Asian high-end money is tightening, so Manila casinos should not count on a VIP recovery in the fourth quarter (see §2 on Okada). Read original →

Regulation−NegativeUK · Machine duty

UK may double every machine duty band, standard rate to 40%.

SBC News · 09/28

The Betting and Gaming Council (BGC) launched its "Back Our Betting Shops" campaign on 28 September. The government is reportedly considering doubling all three bands of machine games duty (MGD, the tax on gaming machines in betting shops and casinos) from 5%, 20% and 25% to 10%, 40% and 50%. Bottom line: This week's news is that all three bands could rise, not just the standard rate. Retail margins would be squeezed past breaking point, closures would run into 2027, and legacy UK groups' marketing budgets would shrink with them. −EV for UK betting shops, limited impact on online-only operators. Regulation−NegativeUK · Machine design

New UK B machines must show time and net result from mid-2027.

SBC News · TechRound · 09/30

The UK Gambling Commission (UKGC) published its machine design consultation response on 30 September (1,065 replies). Machines already on the floor are exempt. New B1 to B4 machines must display play time and net win or loss in real time; turbo spin, quick spin and slam stop are banned; celebratory effects are allowed only when a win exceeds the stake; and play must pause for at least 10 seconds after 20 minutes and 20 seconds after 40 minutes. The main rules take effect on 30 June 2027. The same day, phase two of the online deposit-limit rules took effect: operators must offer a gross deposit limit, displayed no less prominently than other financial limits.


Bottom line: Showing net results, forcing pauses and banning celebrations of small wins treat the product interface as a safer-gambling tool, and online and land-based rules tend to borrow from each other. −EV for UK machine makers (redesign costs), and an early warning for online brands. Drop "loss dressed as a win" celebration animations from creative and app screenshots for European markets now.

trigger-2026-09-29.md · BNLData · Poder360 · 09/28

On 28 September the AGU sued 17 operators in federal court in Pernambuco, including Betano, Bet365, Superbet, Sportingbet, Betnacional, Pixbet, Betfair and KTO, together about 80% of Brazil's fixed-odds market.
