01 · LEAD Regulation −Negative Brazil · Courts and data

Brazil's licensed sites went dark Oct 6, Stake's permit was suspended in a day, and the top court may wait for the runoff.

GGRAsia · SBC News · iGaming Brazil · Gaming Intelligence · 10/06–10/10

The Oct 6 shutdown deadline under Provisional Measure 1,394 arrived on schedule: every licensed platform closed, and banks refund players Oct 9–14. What is new this week is on the court and data fronts. On Oct 8, the 9th Civil Court of the federal court in São Paulo gave Stake a temporary permit to operate until Oct 25, on conditions that it add no customers, launch no new sign-up bonuses or marketing creatives, and not obstruct withdrawals and refunds; it was the first case-level relief any operator won after the ban. On the night of Oct 9 the president of the Third Regional Federal Court (TRF3) suspended that permit, putting the measure back in force for Stake; the Attorney General's Office (AGU) had filed a request (per CNN Brasil and Exame; the ruling itself was not read). At the Supreme Court, a UOL journalist relayed that Justice Fux prefers to hear the case only after the Oct 25 presidential runoff; this is hearsay, with no official ruling or schedule. Congressional amendments stand at 48 and close Oct 13, and the joint committee still has no chair or rapporteur (relator).

On data, research firm Blask counted through Oct 1 that traffic to licensed sites ran 73.5% below normal, searches for licensed brands fell more than 80%, and illegal-site traffic stayed within 11% of its normal level. Blask used India's August 2025 ban as the reference (three months on, 62.9% of users were still gambling, mostly offshore) and projects that about 21.6M of Brazil's 25.2M licensed players will keep gambling; but Blask itself says it has only one week of data, and among 5,970 monitored users who had only used licensed platforms, 6.7% visited illegal sites in week one against 5.1% in a normal week, so no large outflow is visible yet. On jobs and politics, a partial survey by the industry association ANJL shows more than 3,500 dismissals since Sept 25 (23% of the workforce) and another 4,167 on collective leave, with a possible 5,600 within 90 days; Finance Minister Durigan called the ban a pre-election "plebiscite," said the government will not compensate operators who paid R$30M (about NT$170M) per licence, and said about 70,000 illegal sites have been blocked (a different basis from the federal task force's 13,241 URLs in 12 days as of Oct 6; do not mix them).


Bottom line: The route to reopening through the courts is more restricted than expected: Stake's individual relief lasted only one day, and the Supreme Court now reportedly waits until after the runoff, so operators hoping to return through the courts cannot do so before Oct 25 at the earliest. The ban is also more political: the finance minister calls it a plebiscite, and operators and clubs now emphasise job losses and lost sponsorship. Blask's data says illegal sites did not immediately catch licensed players, but it covers one week, and India's precedent is that most were still gambling after three months. −EV for licensed Brazilian operators and their partners, short-term +EV for offshore sites, though site blocking is accelerating. Do not treat Stake's Oct 25 date as an operating assumption; for prepayments and sponsorship contracts with Brazilian partners, chase refunds or negotiate exits against the Dec 15 settlement deadline. For the Philippines, once Brazilian payment flows break and licensed traffic falls 70% in a week, PAGCOR and the central bank (BSP) can cite it when they discuss payment rules.

SIGNAL 02 · US · Supreme Court

The NFL, the AGA and 145 tribes ask the Supreme Court to hear the Kalshi case; an Illinois judge ruled for Kalshi the same week.

On Oct 8 the NFL filed an amicus brief at the Supreme Court backing New Jersey, arguing sports event contracts are not "swaps" under Dodd-Frank and belong under state gambling law. It gives three reasons: the 2010 federal law still banned sports betting nationwide when Dodd-Frank passed; the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) had only 543 staff as of September 2025; and prediction markets will not accept the NFL's prohibited-bettor lists. The American Gaming Association (AGA), 145 tribal governments and organizations, and the 39 states plus DC that filed earlier are on the same side. The background is that the Third Circuit ruled for Kalshi in April while the Sixth and Ninth Circuits ruled for the states; the Supreme Court has not agreed to hear the case, and Kalshi's response is due Nov 9. In the same period, Illinois federal district judge Martha Pacold on about Oct 2 partly granted a preliminary injunction in Kalshi, Coinbase and the CFTC's suit against Illinois regulators, finding Kalshi's core sports contracts likely to be swaps and several state laws likely to conflict with federal law, the opposite of the Sixth Circuit's Sept 25 ruling for Ohio and Tennessee; the Ohio Casino Control Commission on Oct 2 sent cease-and-desist letters to nine firms including Underdog, Coinbase, Polymarket and Webull, giving them 14 days to confirm compliance.


Bottom line: Trial courts and circuit courts have given opposite answers to the same question, so the Supreme Court will have to settle it. The two swap-contract rules the CFTC sent to the White House for review on Oct 1 would become another point of dispute if published. Short-term −EV for pure prediction-market platforms (state enforcement and litigation costs rise), +EV for state-licensed sportsbooks (they keep in-state exclusivity). The NFL and its partners also disagree: its own sportsbook partners FanDuel, DraftKings and Fanatics all run prediction markets, so the league and its partners are already on opposite sides. For US-facing partnerships and ad creatives, do not place "sports predictions" or "event contracts" next to state-licensed brands until the Supreme Court decides whether to take the case. SIGNAL 03 · Macau · Crowds and GGR

Macau Golden Week drew 1.14M visitors, but Seaport still sees October GGR down 5% and the full-year MOP236B forecast stays.

The seven-day Golden Week brought 1.14M visitor arrivals, with a record 210,000 in a single day and 750,000 in the first four; hotel occupancy was 91.3% and room rates eased. Seaport expects October GGR down 5% year on year to about MOP22.9B (about NT$90B), flat in the fourth quarter and up 2.3% for the year, because last October's high win rate lifts the comparison base and China is taxing offshore trusts. Chief Executive Sam Hou Fai said on Oct 10 that the government is not considering lowering its MOP236B 2026 forecast, though he admitted GGR began falling in the second quarter and there is "no definitive conclusion" on why, with October the key number. January–September GGR totals MOP187.1B, so an October–December average of MOP16.3B a month would meet the target, below September's MOP18.06B; last June the government cut its 2025 forecast from MOP240B to MOP228B, and the actual full-year result was MOP247.4B.


Bottom line: Record arrivals with falling GGR expectations suggest the gap is in per-head betting by high-end and overnight guests, not in footfall. The government target needs only MOP16.3B a month, so a downgrade is not the immediate concern; the real variable is how VIP money reacts after the Oct 22 offshore-trust tax deadline. +EV for Macau operators built on mass and non-gaming revenue, −EV for VIP-reliant ones. For the Philippines, Asian high-end spending is shrinking rather than disappearing, so Manila's land-based VIP business should not be expected to recover in the fourth quarter (Cambodia's NagaCorp saw direct premium VIP GGR fall 40.1% in nine months; see §5). SIGNAL 04 · Philippines · DigiPlus

Maybank cut DigiPlus's 2026 core profit 43% to PHP7.8B; spend per user is only 64% of what it assumed.

Malaysia's Maybank cut its forecasts for DigiPlus (parent of ArenaPlus and BingoPlus) in early October: 2026 core profit down 43% to about PHP7.8B (about NT$4.1B), 2027 down 48% to about PHP8.6B, and 2026–2028 revenue forecasts down 18% to 32%, with 2026 revenue at about PHP65.4B (about NT$34.7B). The reasons are first-half revenue of PHP32.9B, down about 31%, and EBITDA of PHP5.5B, down about 40%; with low growth and high inflation, monthly spend per active user is about PHP805 against Maybank's earlier PHP1,250 assumption, while active users total about 5.75M, above the forecast 4.75M. Maybank says recovery depends on spend per user, retention, returns on land-based casino investment and overseas business; Brazil and South Africa are not yet in the forecast, and DigiPlus says the Brazil ban has no material effect on it.


Bottom line: User numbers did not shrink, but spend per user fell. For marketing, DigiPlus spending will shift from acquisition to increasing spend and retention among existing users, consistent with ArenaPlus's daily rebate and daily reload copy this week (see Signal 05). −EV for partners who rely on DigiPlus for traffic (budget tilts to retention and in-house creators), +EV for rival brands going after existing players (rebate spending from the other side will turn more aggressive). SIGNAL 05 · Philippines · Creators and content

Bloomberry launched OnlyFun.PH and ArenaPlus is hiring content producers, and Philippine operators are adding creator and content teams.

Bloomberry (Solaire's parent) announced OnlyFun.PH on Oct 8, which it calls the Philippines' first regulated live-streaming interactive entertainment platform; it belongs to the FUNaloMAX online business, is PAGCOR-licensed, includes KYC checks and player controls, combines online games with live hosts, and will add hosts and creators over time. The Meta Ad Library shows ArenaPlus's official account opened at least 130 new ads from Oct 5 to 10: on Oct 5 "chance to win PHP1M" was upgraded to "chance to win PHP10M" (about NT$5.3M); on Oct 6 about 14 ads for "Daily Rebate up to 2,888, Daily Reload up to 3,235, cashback" went up; from Oct 10 a "New User 7-Day Rewards" line was added; and four job ads seek a "Content Producer" and a "Content Operations Specialist" who know sports, know internet culture and write in a human voice. Bottom line: All three are bringing content and creators into their own teams and platforms rather than only buying placements. ArenaPlus already recruited sports KOLs and now hires a content team, while OnlyFun.PH launches a streamer platform. −EV for independent KOLs and agencies (operators building in-house content capacity will squeeze outsourced budgets), +EV for creative teams that can deliver complete content with compliant assets. Fixed peso amounts are not percentages, and whether they cross PAGCOR's 1.5% rebate line depends on the landing page; brands making similar assets should avoid wording like "daily rebate" and "100% back" for now. Regulation○NeutralDenmark · Ad draft

Denmark's ad bill, read Oct 22, bans gambling ads 10 minutes around live matches.

SBC News · Focus Gaming News · 10/08

The draft gambling act from Tax and Growth Minister Jakob Engel-Schmidt has its first reading on Oct 22, with a target start of Jan 1, 2027. Ads may not present gambling as entertainment, imply it can change a life, or use people under 25; outdoor ads are banned on public transport and must sit at least 200 metres from schools and universities; no marketing at all is allowed in the 10 minutes before and after live sports; sports club and venue sponsorship stays. Influencers and affiliate marketers fall under the same ad rules. Fines for online operators are set as a share of GGR, and a first offence at a physical venue costs DKK60,000 (about NT$290,000). The regulator Spillemyndigheden also gains power to block unlicensed intermediary sites, and the government is weighing a phased rollout.


Bottom line: The bill would retain sponsorship while strictly limiting advertising and promoters. It targets ad reach rather than all brand exposure. The 10-minute window hurts most the brands that acquire users through in-play slots. −EV for brands that depend on match-time buying and influencer traffic, smaller impact on brands with club sponsorships. For European assets, drop "entertainment" and "life-changing" language and young faces ahead of time.

Regulation−NegativeGreece · Influencers

Greece files legal complaints against 18 influencers, 3.6M followers combined.

Focus Gaming News · 10/08

Greek Finance Minister Kyriakos Pierrakakis warned social media influencers on Oct 8 that promoting illegal gambling will be enforced against: "fame is not immunity, influence carries responsibility." The gaming commission EEEP has filed legal complaints against 18 well-known online figures, including a rapper and a model, with 3.6M followers combined. Under Law 5313/2026, in force since June, influencers face fines of €5,000 to €50,000, and third parties such as internet providers and advertisers face up to €2M per violation (about NT$74M). The government estimates the 2025 black market at about €1.8B in wagers and about 900,000 players, and has blocked 15,000 sites.


Bottom line: Enforcement now extends from platforms to promoters themselves, and the fines fall on individuals. The same logic will show up in Philippine KOL deals: if a brand lacks a PAGCOR licence or its assets are unapproved, the influencer also faces promotional risk. −EV for influencers endorsing unlicensed or grey brands, +EV for creators who accept only licensed brands. Check a brand's licence before taking work, and write asset review and takedown terms into the contract.

Lottomatica to absorb CIRSA at 0.668 shares each, forming a €4.5B-revenue group.

SBC News · 10/09

The boards of Italy's listed gaming group Lottomatica and Spain's casino group CIRSA approved a common merger plan: CIRSA is absorbed into Lottomatica without liquidation, and each CIRSA share converts into 0.668 new Lottomatica shares. CIRSA's majority owner Blackstone gets two board seats, taking the board from 11 to 13; antitrust approval is needed in Italy, Spain, Mexico and Morocco, and both shareholder meetings are expected by end of November.


Bottom line: The deal puts a land-based plus online group at the top of southern Europe and includes capital returns in the deal terms, showing large operators answer share-price pressure with mergers plus payouts. −EV for mid-sized European online operators and suppliers (purchasing and bargaining power concentrate in fewer buyers), +EV for private-equity holders looking to exit. Direct impact on Philippine and Brazilian brands is zero; the indirect signal is that Latin America is being used as a growth story.
