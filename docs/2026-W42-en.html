01 · LEAD Regulation −Negative Brazil · Congress and the top court

Most of Brazil's 95 amendments keep sports betting and ban online casino; Congress and the top court wait for Oct 25.

BNLData · iGaming Business Brasil · 10/13–10/17

The amendment window for Provisional Measure 1,394 (MPV 1394, which took every licensed Brazilian platform offline on Oct 6) closed on Oct 13. Lawmakers filed 99 amendments and withdrew 5; iGaming Business Brasil reports 95 pending (its arithmetic does not add up, so the pending count is used here). The broadest consensus is to ban online casino and slots outright, keep sports betting, and allow advertising for sports betting only. Amendment 64 would restore the old regime and add the shutdown period back onto licence terms; others would ban stadium naming rights and single-event bets such as corners and penalties. The joint committee has 25 full members but still no chair or rapporteur (relator). The measure locks the congressional agenda from Nov 9 and expires Nov 23, extendable by 60 days; Folha reports that with the recess the vote could slip to February 2027. Neither timeline is officially confirmed.

On the court side, Estadão reports that Justice Fux, who presides over the cases, plans to act only after the second round so as not to be seen favouring a candidate. Broadcasters' association ABERT, operator groups ANJL and IBJR, and clubs Cruzeiro, Flamengo and São Paulo have all asked to join the case; the Attorney General's Office (AGU) wants the ban upheld. Oct 25 is also the day federal authorisations lapse under the measure. In the first round Flávio Bolsonaro took 47.03% and Lula 45.16%, about 2.3 million votes apart. A Genial/Quaest poll shows 72% back the ban, but several polls find very few voters would switch over it. Flávio has said he backs keeping sports betting and blocking only online casino; Lula told a rally "I ended the bets".


Bottom line: Brazil's route back is clearer than a week ago: the mainstream proposal in Congress is "keep sports betting, ban online casino", and both the courts and Congress have pushed their timing past Oct 25. With 70% of voters behind the ban, no candidate will soften before the vote, so nothing is settled before November. +EV in the medium term for licensed operators built on sports betting, −EV for those built on online casino and slots; even if the market reopens, casino products may not come back. Next steps: keep handling Brazilian partner contracts and prepayments against the Dec 15 settlement deadline, and prepare only sports creative for any Brazilian relaunch. For the Philippines, Brazil is moving to regulate sports and casino separately, close to PAGCOR's split between online casino and sports betting, which is a useful reference in talks with the regulator.

SIGNAL 02 · Philippines · Regulator exits operations

PAGCOR plans to sell 35 Casino Filipino venues for PHP20–30B after a PHP7B loss this year, aiming to stop operating by late 2027.

PAGCOR (the Philippine Amusement and Gaming Corporation, the gambling regulator) chair Tengco said its land-based brand Casino Filipino will lose at least PHP7B (about NT$3.7B) in 2026, after about PHP6B in 2025. The sale covers 35 casinos: 12 run directly by PAGCOR and 23 run with private revenue-sharing partners (IAG says "nearly 40", a different count). The initial valuation is PHP20–30B (about NT$10.6–15.9B), below the roughly PHP80B cited in 2023 and the PHP30–50B cited in December 2025. The Governance Commission for GOCCs is expected to send its recommendation to the Palace this month, after which financial advisers will price the assets. Revenue-sharing partners get first right of purchase; directly run venues go to open tender, and large Entertainment City operators have shown interest. The target exit is Q3–Q4 2027. Severance of about PHP20B will come from a retirement trust fund. KABAN in Boracay is piloting a new model: the operator pays PAGCOR 35% of gross gaming revenue (GGR) and covers staff pay. Industry GGR for 2026 is forecast at about PHP350B, short of the PHP400B target, and PAGCOR's net income is expected below PHP5B, partly because the Supreme Court ordered it to remit 5% of gross revenue to the Sports Commission retroactively.


Bottom line: PAGCOR's revenue pressure has spread from online to land-based: online deposit rules are tighter and its own casinos keep losing money, so the regulator is selling them and moving towards a role limited to licensing and supervision. That reduces the conflict between regulator and operator. +EV for Entertainment City operators that want to buy land-based venues, since most of the 35 sites are in provincial capitals outside Manila and give quick access to local players; neutral to slightly +EV for licensed online operators, as PAGCOR will have no in-house competitor and will weigh policy by industry-wide revenue. Lower PAGCOR profit will push it to look for new income, so online enforcement and fee rates may be adjusted. Read original →

SIGNAL 03 · US · AI promotions

New York moves to ban AI-targeted promos, Massachusetts wants answers by Nov 3, and Louisiana renews DraftKings only on conditions.

On Oct 9 the New York State Gaming Commission voted unanimously to advance revised rules: unless the customer opts in, operators may not use AI or algorithms to profile players for personalised promotions or design promotions meant to raise spending, and they must identify possible problem gamblers and assign a staff contact. The same day the Massachusetts Gaming Commission wrote to every sportsbook asking how AI is used in marketing and promotions, whether losses or predicted lifetime value (LTV) trigger offers, and how much human oversight there is; written answers are due Nov 3. On Oct 15 the Louisiana Gaming Control Board extended DraftKings only conditionally, to Feb 19, 2027, with a full hearing on Feb 18; board members pressed executives on reports that it targeted losing bettors and on its VIP handling. DraftKings' co-founder denied targeting losers and said its AI is used to find players who need help. Maine and Maryland are also reviewing AI use, Sen. Blumenthal co-sponsors a federal bill to ban AI-targeted promotions, and the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) is reportedly in an early review of online sportsbook advertising (the FTC declined to comment and the source is unnamed). The trigger was September reporting by The New York Times and ProPublica.


Bottom line: US oversight is shifting from what an ad says to whom the system targets. Offers triggered automatically by LTV and wins or losses are standard in most operators' CRM today; if New York's rule is adopted, that has to become opt-in. −EV for US operators that rely on automated CRM and VIP management, +EV for responsible-gambling and compliance-tech vendors. The Philippines and Brazil have no similar clause yet, but other regulators tend to borrow these rules. Brands that send personalised promotions should document promotion trigger conditions as clear, explainable rules, keep records of human review, and never use "lose X, get Y" as a trigger. SIGNAL 04 · Philippines · Rebate cap

PAGCOR extends its 1.5% rebate cap to land-based slots from Oct 13, and rebates can no longer be deducted from GGR.

Approved by the PAGCOR board on Oct 8 and effective on posting on Oct 13, the new rule caps cash rebates on slots at licensed land-based casinos at 1.4% of turnover and non-cash reward commissions at 0.2%, with the two combined not to exceed 1.5%; commissions cannot be cashed out. It explicitly extends the rule that rebates may not be treated as gaming losses and deducted from GGR to electronic gaming machines. Table rules are unchanged: 1.35% of net turnover for non-junket VIP and 1.5% for junkets. Online caps were set separately in May: cash rebates of up to 1.5% on certain games including online slots, and cashback of up to 15%.


Bottom line: Online and land-based rebate caps now match at 1.5%. PAGCOR has closed the gap that let land-based venues out-rebate online, and made sure rebates cannot shrink the GGR base it shares in. Neutral for licensed online operators: their rules have not changed, but the land-based advantage is gone; −EV for land-based casinos that win slot players with high rebates. Brands writing creative need to keep three terms apart: rebate (capped at 1.5%), cashback (capped at 15%) and deposit bonus (no cap). A competitor ad this week reads "1.5% unlimited cashback + 2% slot return"; if the 2% is a rebate it breaches the cap (see the §5 competitor card). Read original →

SIGNAL 05 · US · NBA integrity

The NBA bans same-player under parlays and other risky props, but the rule does not reach prediction markets like Kalshi.

On Oct 16 the NBA told its authorised operators to stop taking parlays of multiple unders on the same player, and flagged other "heightened integrity risk" markets: player "first-of" props, the opening tip, players missing specific shots, and unders on players expected to play under 12 minutes. The WNBA follows from 2027. DraftKings and FanDuel back the rules. LSR notes the league's partner deals cover more than 99% of the US sportsbook market but not prediction markets. The league is also widening AI betting monitoring and opening a text hotline for threats. The backdrop is the Jontay Porter and Terry Rozier cases; prosecutors cite an $80K (about NT$2.56M) parlay on Porter unders that would have paid about $1.1M. Rozier's trial starts in February 2027. The new NBA season tips off Oct 20.


Bottom line: By restricting markets through partner contracts, the league is setting integrity rules itself rather than leaving them to regulators; the initial restrictions target the most popular player props. Prediction markets are not bound, so the same contracts still trade on Kalshi, widening the market gap between licensed sportsbooks and prediction markets. Short-term −EV for licensed sportsbooks (fewer high-margin same-game parlays), +EV for prediction markets. Brands making NBA content in the Philippines should avoid showing player-under parlays in season creative and push results and spreads instead. Read original →

Regulation○NeutralSpain · Payment checks

Spain makes payment methods match the account holder from Oct 28.

Yogonet · 10/16

Royal Decree 813/2026 amends Spain's anti-money-laundering rules so operators must verify that the payment methods used for deposits and withdrawals belong to the registered account holder. Existing customers get a six-month transition in which they can deposit and bet but cannot withdraw winnings until verified; accounts still unverified at the end are closed by the operator and balances returned. Electronic-ID provisions take effect 18 months after publication. Regulator DGOJ also reopened a public consultation on its algorithm for detecting high-risk players on Oct 13.


Bottom line: Matching the payment method to the account holder is the most direct way to stop proxy top-ups and mule accounts, which are also a problem with e-wallets and agent top-ups in the Philippines. −EV for operators that grow through agents or third-party top-ups. Brands running top-up promotions in the Philippines should avoid copy like "top up for a friend".

Bottom line: The Osaka IR is four years away, yet neighbouring markets are already counting how many Japanese visitors they will lose. −EV for Korean casinos. Japanese and Korean visitors to the Philippines will be affected too, but after 2030.
