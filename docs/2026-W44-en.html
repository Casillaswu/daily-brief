01 · LEAD Market −Negative Philippines · PAGCOR 9M

PAGCOR nine-month revenue fell 23.4% and eGames revenue fell 37%, as e-wallets stopped linking to gambling sites.

Inside Asian Gaming · BusinessWorld · GGRAsia · 10/29

The Philippine Amusement and Gaming Corporation (PAGCOR) reported on 29 October that total revenue for the first nine months of 2026 was PHP64.4 billion (about TWD32.2 billion), down 23.4% year on year. Most of the drop came from eGames (licensed online casino products): revenue fell from PHP44.0 billion to PHP27.6 billion, a 37.2% decline. Inside Asian Gaming attributes it to last year’s central bank order requiring payment providers to delink e-wallets from gambling sites, plus tighter regulation. Licensed land-based casino revenue fell only 3.22% to PHP23.1 billion; PAGCOR’s own Casino Filipino branches fell 8% to PHP7.28 billion. Net income dropped 84.9% to PHP2.17 billion, also because a Supreme Court ruling requires PAGCOR to remit 5% of gross revenue to the Philippine Sports Commission (PSC), roughly double the previous level. First-half revenue was down 26.6%, so the nine-month decline narrowed; that implies a third-quarter drop of about 16%. Despite lower revenue, PAGCOR still paid PHP45.3 billion in statutory and nation-building contributions, including PHP27.6 billion to the Treasury as the government’s 50% share, PHP3.01 billion to the PSC and PHP2.90 billion in franchise tax. Chairman Alejandro Tengco said PAGCOR will keep strengthening operations and oversight so that regulated gaming revenue continues to fund government programmes.

In the same week, Manila’s land-based casinos expanded their online businesses. Solaire parent Bloomberry launched celebrity-hosted livestream betting on its OnlyFun.PH platform on 29 October (see §3); Hong Kong-listed IEC said its LaVie casino will open a self-run online platform in mid-to-late November (see §5). The wider economy is also weakening: according to the Inquirer and BusinessWorld, the peso broke past 64 to the dollar for the first time on 27 October, and the central bank said October inflation could exceed 8%.


Bottom line: Online revenue fell 37% while land-based fell only 3%, so the decline is concentrated in online products whose deposit channels were cut, not in players gambling less overall. It follows that licensed online platforms with more deposit steps will keep converting poorly in the short term: −EV for pure-play licensed online operators, relatively +EV for operators that can route traffic from a casino floor and run their own live tables (Solaire, LaVie). With net income down to PHP2.17 billion, the regulator now depends more on an online recovery; whether policy moves toward opening compliant deposit channels or toward harder enforcement against unlicensed sites still needs to be verified. For Brazil-facing operators, this is a second way to cool a market besides a ban: leave the product legal, restrict payments, and online revenue still falls by nearly 40%. One step operators can take now: put compliant e-wallet deposit guides on landing pages and in creatives, and prepare “licensed and verifiable” creatives for the PAGCOR verification app due in December.

SIGNAL 02 · BRAZIL · SUPREME COURT HEARING

Brazil’s Supreme Court hears 28 bodies on the betting ban on 3 November, with licences lapsed and no reopening date set.

Lula won re-election on 25 October with 51.78% of valid votes. On the same day, the federal betting authorisations of 85 companies and 188 brands lapsed under Provisional Measure 1,394 (MPV 1394), with no refund of fees. Justice Luiz Fux published the hearing list on 28 October: 28 bodies, including clubs Flamengo, Fluminense, Botafogo, Cruzeiro, São Paulo and Corinthians; the football confederation CBF and the Olympic Committee COB; the Attorney General’s Office (AGU), the finance and sports ministries and the Prosecutor General (PGR); operator association ANJL, the responsible-gambling institute IBJR and broadcasters’ association Abert. The hearing starts at 10:00 on 3 November in the STF Second Panel room, with ten minutes per body, broadcast on TV and YouTube. It sits within ADI 8027, the constitutional challenge filed by ANJL seeking an immediate suspension of the measure. IBJR chief executive Carlos Lima told Poder360 on 30 October that the illegal market has grown an estimated 101% since the ban, with more than BRL28.1 billion (about TWD160 billion) wagered illegally a year, and that cloned versions of formerly licensed brands are making consumers believe operators have reopened. This is not the STF’s first betting hearing: in November 2024 it heard more than 50 experts on the 2023 betting law (Lei 14.790); this time the AGU asked for a new one, citing data gathered since the ban. The same measure is also challenged in ADIs 8024, 8028 and 8035, all assigned to Fux. Congress’s joint committee had still not met at our deadline.


Bottom line: A public hearing is a consultation, not a ruling; Fux decides only afterwards whether to suspend the measure, likely not before mid-November (inference). Clubs, the football confederation and the Olympic Committee make up much of the list, so the debate will centre on sponsorship and sports revenue, which favours a compromise that keeps sports betting and bans online casino. If that compromise takes shape, it is medium-term +EV for sports-led licensed operators in Brazil and −EV for casino-led ones. The growth of the illegal market and cloned brands is the industry’s strongest argument, and also a live risk for brands: monitor lookalike domains and state publicly that the brand is not currently operating. SIGNAL 03 · UNITED STATES · PREDICTION MARKETS

Utah files 14 criminal counts against Kalshi and its co-founders, a day after the CFTC sued the state over event contracts.

Utah Attorney General Derek Brown charged KalshiEX and co-founders Tarek Mansour and Luana Lopes Lara in state court in Salt Lake City on 28 October: one second-degree felony count of pattern of unlawful activity, 12 third-degree felony counts of online gambling promotion and one class A misdemeanour count of general gambling promotion. The case rests on 12 sports wagers placed on Kalshi by an undercover investigator. The day before, the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) sued Utah, arguing that federal jurisdiction over event contracts on registered exchanges is exclusive; it is the tenth state the CFTC has taken to court. Kalshi called the prosecution a “crazy overreach” and said it expects to win in court as it did in Arizona. Two other moves came the same week: Kalshi sued Louisiana officials in federal court to stop the state’s gaming regulators from acting against it, and Polymarket reached a court-approved agreement with the Michigan Gaming Control Board (MGCB) to stop offering new sports event contracts in Michigan and close all existing ones by 25 November, in exchange for no enforcement. Polymarket had sued Michigan in March, but its requests for a temporary restraining order and a preliminary injunction were both denied. Before the criminal case, Utah had filed a civil suit in mid-October accusing Kalshi of creating a public nuisance by skirting the state’s 130-year-old gambling ban; CFTC chairman Michael Selig said event contracts “also provide the public with information about the outcome of future events” and that Congress gave the CFTC sole oversight.


Bottom line: States have moved from civil injunctions to criminal charges against founders personally, and the CFTC has answered with federal suits; both sides are escalating, and the outcome will be set by federal appeals courts or the Supreme Court. Polymarket’s retreat in Michigan shows that prediction markets will drop sports contracts in states where they lose in court and keep their other categories. +EV for licensed US sportsbooks, as the substitute product is squeezed out of some states; −EV for Kalshi and Polymarket. More US states now treat sports event contracts as gambling, so Philippine operators assessing any tie-up with prediction-market products should handle compliance and licensing as they would for a betting product. SIGNAL 04 · UNITED STATES · NBA

The NBA signs a CFTC data-sharing MOU and names Kalshi and Polymarket as partners, gaining a say over which NBA markets they list.

On 26 October the NBA announced a memorandum of understanding (MOU) on information sharing with the CFTC and named Kalshi and Polymarket its preferred prediction market exchanges. The agreements let the league limit permissible markets, ban trading by league and team staff, and share information on investigations and suspicious trading. CFTC chairman Michael Selig said the NBA follows MLB and the NHL in signing an MOU. iGamingExpress says the NBA is the fourth North American league to partner with prediction markets; the NFL has signed neither and is asking the Supreme Court to let states apply their own laws. Kalshi’s head of sports partnerships Adam Barrick said millions of people trade on Kalshi with nationwide consumer protections; Polymarket’s president of sports business development Ari Borod said the deal lets users engage more deeply with basketball. Utah filed criminal charges against Kalshi in the same week (see Signal 03).


Bottom line: By signing with both the regulator and the platforms, the league gains control over which markets can be listed and access to trading data. That plugs prediction markets into the existing integrity-monitoring system and weakens the states’ argument that prediction markets lack integrity safeguards. +EV for Kalshi and Polymarket (league endorsement); −EV for licensed US sportsbooks that rely on NBA traffic, which now face a competitor with official backing. The NFL takes the opposite position, so the split between leagues will continue. Philippine sportsbook brands’ NBA creatives are not directly affected; when negotiating rights with leagues or data providers, the “market limits plus information sharing” terms are a useful template. SIGNAL 05 · UNITED KINGDOM · BUDGET

The UK Budget raises machine games duty from 20% to 25% on higher-stakes machines from April 2027, adding about £100m a year.

When Flutter announced job cuts last week, the industry feared machine games duty (MGD) would double to 40%. In the Autumn Budget on 28 October, Chancellor John Healey raised it from 20% to 25%, effective 1 April 2027, only for higher-stakes Category B machines (in casinos, betting shops and bingo halls; stakes over £1 or prizes over £100). Lower-stakes machines in pubs and seaside arcades are unchanged. The extra revenue funds business-rates relief for high streets: the relief threshold for retail and hospitality rises from £12,000 to £18,000, with adult gaming centres and vape shops excluded. The Office for Budget Responsibility (OBR) expects the change to raise about £100m a year (about TWD4.2 billion) from 2027-28; betting and gaming receipts are forecast at £5bn in 2026-27, up 31%, driven by remote gaming duty (RGD, on online casino and slots) rising from 21% to 40% in April and by online activity falling more slowly than expected. The Betting and Gaming Council called the move “deeply damaging”, and Entain confirmed higher costs for its Ladbrokes and Coral shops. Healey said he was raising gambling taxes “to support good growth on every high street”; before the Budget, Rank and Genting UK had warned that a rise would push some casinos into losses.


Bottom line: The additional tax falls on land-based machines; online tax rates are unchanged this time. The OBR also notes that online casino revenue fell less than expected after the tax rise, an official signal that online operators can carry more. −EV for Entain and evoke, whose shop estates are large (higher closure risk); neutral for pure-play online operators. The pairing “tax rate doubled, receipts still up 31%” will be quoted by finance ministries elsewhere; Philippine operators discussing rates with PAGCOR should bring local data on players moving to unlicensed sites rather than relying on the UK case. Regulation−NegativeAustralia · Star AML

A court orders Star to pay AU$260m in AML penalties over 19 months.

Inside Asian Gaming · 10/28–10/30

On 28 October Federal Court Justice Cameron Moore concluded the civil case brought by anti-money-laundering regulator AUSTRAC in 2022: Star must pay AU$260m (about TWD5.5 billion), AU$195m for The Star Sydney and AU$65m for The Star Gold Coast, in four equal instalments over 19 months. AUSTRAC sought AU$400m; Star argued anything over AU$100m would put its future in doubt. The judge rejected both figures and said Star had put “profit ahead of compliance”. Crown settled with AUSTRAC for AU$450m in 2023. The day before the ruling, Star reported its first quarter in more than two years with both EBITDA and cash flow positive, and new management admitted that “overzealous” compliance had driven VIPs away. After the ruling Star began talks with lender WhiteHawk because the penalty triggered a review event in its loan terms. According to iGamingExpress, major shareholder Bally’s said the penalty will not push Star into insolvency.


Bottom line: The court did not waive the penalty because of the operator’s finances; it only changed how it is paid. Star being fined AU$260m while admitting that over-compliance drove VIPs away shows that AML costs can only be saved through more efficient KYC (customer due diligence) processes, not lower standards. Neutral for licensed Australian casinos; −EV for VIP and junket business at Philippine integrated resorts, as PAGCOR and the Anti-Money Laundering Council (AMLC) will use the Australian ruling as a reference when checking VIP source of funds.

Regulation−NegativeSweepstakes · Oklahoma

Oklahoma’s sweepstakes ban starts on 1 November as over 20 platforms exit.

Gambling Insider · 10/29

Sweepstakes casinos run on two currencies: gold coins can be bought, while sweeps coins can only be obtained free and can be redeemed for cash. From mid-October ACE, Baba, Coin Wizard and Stake.us had announced exits from Oklahoma; in the final week more followed: B-Two’s McLuck, Hello Millions, SpinBlitz and PlayFame stop operating from 1 November; High 5 Casino ended access on 29 October; six A1 Development platforms including Funrize switched to gold-coin-only play; Blazesoft’s Fortune Wins, Zula and Sportzino excluded residents from 27 October. GiddyUp announced a temporary wind-down on 19 October, with redemptions open until 2 November. Wyoming’s Select Committee on Gaming reviewed draft 27LSO-0098 on 5–6 October, which would classify running online casinos and sweepstakes games as illegal professional gambling and cover payment processing, geolocation, hosting and advertising, with penalties of up to three years in prison or a US$3,000 fine; it is expected in the January 2027 session. Pennsylvania’s HB 2801 has not moved since 24 September and the session ends on 30 November. Florida’s attorney general gave Google, Meta and three other platforms until 23 October to explain how they block ads; as of 29 October neither side had said anything publicly.


Bottom line: Operators leave a state in one of two ways: a full exit, or a gold-coin-only mode. Keeping gold coins gives up revenue but keeps the user list while waiting for the law to change (inference). Wyoming’s draft also covers advertising and payment services, the same direction as Oklahoma; with Oklahoma in force, 10 states now ban the model explicitly, no other state has a set effective date yet, and the next milestone is Wyoming’s January session. +EV for licensed US online casinos; −EV for sweepstakes operators and the ad and payment providers that serve them.

Market＋PositiveUS · Nebraska ballot

Nebraska votes on online sports betting on 3 November, with 58% support in April.

Ballotpedia · SBC Americas · 10/31

Nebraska voters decide two linked measures on 3 November: Initiative 440 would legalise online sports betting only through vendors at the state’s six licensed racetracks; Initiative 441 would define online sports betting and require the Nebraska Racing and Gaming Commission to adopt rules by 1 June 2027. The campaign is led by local racetrack and casino operator WarHorse, with online sportsbook operators and local investors. A University of Nebraska-Lincoln poll in April found 58% in favour, 31% against and 11% undecided. Today bets can only be placed in person at licensed racetracks. Ballotpedia and SBC Americas describe the measures’ legal form differently (constitutional amendment versus statute), but both confirm that the two must pass together.


Bottom line: If they pass, online sportsbooks entering Nebraska will first have to strike deals with racetracks, which hold the keys to market access. +EV for operators with existing racetrack relationships there. The state has about 2 million people, but this is the only online gambling ballot measure in these midterms, so the result will be cited by campaigners elsewhere (inference). We will update after the count.

Alberta’s first regulated quarter drew C$2.89bn in online wagers, mostly casino.

Gaming Intelligence · 10/30

Alberta reported its first quarterly data since opening to private online operators: total wagers of C$2.89bn (about TWD66 billion), with online casino C$2.28bn or 79%, online sports betting C$325.8m and live casino C$284.1m. Market○NeutralUS · iGaming market share

US iGaming grew 12% in Q3, its slowest on record, as challengers took share.

NEXT.io (citing Citizens) · 10/27

Data from Citizens show US iGaming revenue grew 12% year on year in the third quarter, the slowest on record, after 16% in each of the first two quarters. FanDuel still leads but its share fell from 26.6% to 25.1%, declining every quarter this year; DraftKings fell from 24.3% to 22.2%, though up from 20.9% in the second quarter, its first sequential gain in two years; BetMGM fell from 20.7% to 18.6%, a sixth straight decline; Caesars fell from 7.1% to 6.4%. Gainers were Fanatics (3.9% to 5.2%), Rush Street (6.8% to 7.4%) and bet365 (0.4% to 1.7%, helped by launches in Michigan and West Virginia). In sports betting, the quarter’s hold was flat at 9.6%; DraftKings had 37.6% of handle and FanDuel 32.8%, with FanDuel’s margin of 10.4% above DraftKings’ 9.5%. Fanatics’ handle share fell from 11.2% to 9.9% while its revenue share rose from 7.3% to 8.8%; BetMGM’s margin rose from 9.6% to 10.2%.


Bottom line: Only a handful of states allow online casino, there are not enough new ones, and growth in mature states is slowing; the top three are losing share to Fanatics, RSI and bet365. +EV for challenger brands, −EV for the top three. Once a market saturates, challengers win share through product and cross-selling (Fanatics’ e-commerce members), not bigger bonuses.
