Regulation−NegativeBrazil · Congress deadline

Brazil’s Congress confirms the ban decree expires on 23 November; no committee yet.

Congresso Nacional, MPV 1394 page · read 11/08

What it means: Provisional Measure (MP) 1,394, a presidential decree that takes effect immediately but must be turned into law by Congress within a deadline, is still in force, so the ban keeps running; if Congress does not vote in time, the decree lapses. The MPV 1394 page on the Congress website lists the review period as 25 September to 23 November 2026, extendable once by 60 days under Article 62 of the Constitution; an urgency regime (regime de urgência) applies from 9 November; current status is “awaiting installation of the joint committee”. The page summary sets out what the decree does: it bans the operation, offer, brokering and advertising of fixed-odds betting, including sports betting and online games; ends existing authorisations within 30 days; requires operators to return player balances and winnings; bans related advertising, sponsorship and payments; authorises blocking of sites and apps; and creates an inter-agency enforcement committee. The Congress press office told Lance! that the clock stops during recess, so an extended deadline would run to about early March 2027. The government’s leader in Congress, Randolfe Rodrigues, said on 5 November he would push to install the committee next week and hold a vote this year; under the rotation, the Senate chairs it and the Chamber names the rapporteur. The page also lists 99 pending amendments, including a compensation clause that would refund only the fee for the unexpired licence term.
Next checkpoint: the week from 10 November · whether the committee is installed and who the rapporteur is.


Bottom line: The deadline, previously reported as either the 23rd or the 25th, is now confirmed as the 23rd; if no vote happens by then, the government will most likely extend it by 60 days and the ban runs into early next year (inference). For licensed Brazilian operators −EV: reopening has to wait for a congressional vote or a Supreme Court ruling. If the “refund for the unexpired term” amendment is adopted, it would amount to recognising that the licences have value, +EV for operators that paid the R$30 million fee. Brands can move their earliest assumption for a Brazil reopening to Q1 2027.

Market○NeutralUK · illegal-market data

UKGC data show about 4% of online gamblers used an unlicensed site within a year.

UK Gambling Commission · 11/05

What it means: for the first time, the regulator has turned “how many people use illegal platforms” into a tracked number, which government and industry will now cite in every debate over tax rises or new restrictions. On 5 November the Gambling Commission (GC) published initial findings from the Gambling Survey for Great Britain (GSGB) and launched an Illegal Gambling Data Dashboard: 94.4% of all respondents showed no sign of using an illegal site; 0.6% had used one in the past 4 weeks and a further 0.4% in the past 12 months. Among people who gambled online in the past 12 months, 2.5% had used one in the past 4 weeks and a further 1.5% within 12 months, about 4% in total. Use was higher among men than women; 18–24-year-olds were highest on the 12-month measure and 25–34-year-olds on the 4-week measure. GC Executive Director of Operations Sue Young said overall engagement is “low and stable”, but those who use illegal operators face higher risk of harm. In the same week the GC also analysed banking transaction data: in a non-representative sample, 91% of users with repeated unlicensed transactions also bet with licensed operators.
Next checkpoint: the next dashboard update · how figures move after remote gaming duty rose to 40% in April.


Bottom line: Online casino duty rose from 21% to 40% in April, yet the measured illegal-site usage is still in single digits, which weakens the industry argument that tax rises push players to the black market. For licensed UK operators −EV: resistance to further tax rises gets weaker. The 91% overlap means illegal and licensed players are mostly the same people, so licensed operators can still keep them with product. Regulators in Brazil and the Philippines lack exactly this kind of data when they debate bans or fee rates; operators that run their own player-flow surveys will be more persuasive than those quoting UK numbers. The GC asks whether people used a site that is “likely” unlicensed rather than asking them to judge legality themselves; Philippine operators running a similar survey can use the same wording to avoid under-reporting by players who do not know which platforms are licensed.

Regulation−NegativeNetherlands · supplier order

The Dutch Ksa orders supplier Endorphina to keep its games off unlicensed sites.

Kansspelautoriteit (Ksa) sanctions page · 11/02

What it means: the Dutch regulator is no longer chasing only unlicensed operators; it now holds game suppliers responsible for downstream sites, treating a supplier’s games appearing on unlicensed sites as promoting unlicensed gambling. On 2 November the Netherlands Gambling Authority (Ksa) published a binding instruction dated 19 October: Endorphina Limited (Malta) and Endorphina Ltd. (Cyprus) failed to take adequate measures to stop their games being offered on sites that are illegal in the Netherlands, in breach of Article 1(1)(b) of the Betting and Gaming Act; the two companies must put measures in place and report to the Ksa within two weeks of the decision, through their own technical measures and through contract terms with customers and distributors. According to Gambling.com, citing the decision, Ksa inspectors using Dutch IP addresses ran checks in January–February, June and late September and played games such as Hell Hot 100 on sites including starzino.com, amonbet.com and luckygem.com; two of the sites had already received penalty orders and the games were still playable. Endorphina disputed the findings throughout, saying it is a B2B-only supplier and that some supply runs through licensed intermediaries. The decision can be challenged; the Ksa also decided to publish the case as a warning to other suppliers and intermediaries.
Next checkpoint: whether Endorphina objects · whether the Ksa issues similar orders to other suppliers.


Bottom line: Regulatory pressure is moving up the supply chain: when unlicensed operators cannot all be shut down, game suppliers are told to cut supply. For suppliers serving both licensed and grey markets −EV; for suppliers serving only licensed markets +EV (rivals are forced to shrink their channels). PAGCOR is also cracking down on unlicensed sites; if it follows the Dutch approach, licensed operators’ supplier lists will be scrutinised, and operators renewing supplier contracts can ask for a commitment to supply only licensed operators plus a list of downstream clients.

01 · LEAD Regulation −Negative Brazil · reopening order overturned

TRF3’s president reversed CDA’s reopening on 6 Nov; Brazil’s Prosecutor General asked the STF to freeze similar suits.

Lance! · Correio Braziliense · CNN Brasil · 11/03–11/07

On 3 November, Judge Adriana Pileggi of the Federal Regional Court of the 3rd Region (TRF3, the federal appeals court covering São Paulo and neighbouring states) granted CDA Gaming interim relief, restoring the licences, domains and payment flows of its three brands, Casa de Apostas, Betsul and Jogo Online. Her reasoning: the company paid a R$30 million fee (about TWD170 million) for a licence valid to the end of 2029, and taking it back under the decree without a refund amounts to “true confiscation”. CDA reportedly resumed online operations on 4 November, the second operator after Stake to reopen through the courts. Three days later the ruling was reversed: TRF3 President Luís Antonio Johonsom di Salvo accepted the Attorney General’s Office (AGU) request on the night of 6 November and suspended the order, saying the public interest prevails when private interests collide with collective values, and that the case is “before the Supreme Court, so lower courts anticipating a view is inconvenient for public order”. That same night the Prosecutor General (PGR) asked the Supreme Court (STF), within constitutional challenge ADI 8027, to suspend similar cases nationwide, arguing that courts treating the decree differently creates “legal uncertainty and asymmetric application”.

The Supreme Court held its hearing on the ban this week: 3 November from 10:00 to 17:30, with 34 speakers; CNN counted at least 22 opposing the decree. Rapporteur Justice Fux said afterwards that he personally leans towards a ban, but also mentioned legal certainty, legitimate expectations and “modulation of effects” (modulação, which lets a ruling set a transition period or limit retroactive effect). STF President Fachin said this week he wants to join the cases on municipal lotteries, the gambling ban and betting for a single ruling, with no date. On the executive side, Finance Minister Durigan said the 2027 budget will drop betting tax revenue (see §6).

Positions at the hearing split in two. Football clubs and operators warned that the ban threatens sports funding and pushes players to unlicensed platforms; the Brazilian Institute for Responsible Gaming (IBJR) estimated on 30 October that the illegal market has grown 101% since the ban. The government side defended the ban on grounds of addiction, household debt and the drain on consumption, and the PGR and AGU also spoke. The CDA order applied only to this one company, and a first-instance court had rejected it; but it was the first case in which an appeals judge called the decree “confiscation”, and the industry expected other fee-paying operators to cite the same argument. The president’s suspension closes that route for now; CDA can still appeal within its own case, but its three brands cannot operate until a new ruling.


Bottom line: The president’s suspension plus the PGR’s request to freeze cases temporarily blocks individual operators from reopening through lower courts, leaving only two exits for Brazilian betting: a Supreme Court ruling or a congressional vote (Congress deadline in 🌍 Market Moves). Fux’s mention of modulation points to the more likely outcome being “keep the ban, give fee-paying operators compensation or a transition period” rather than a full reopening (inference). For licensed Brazilian operators still waiting to reopen −EV; for individual operators hoping to reopen first through litigation −EV. For Philippine operators: a longer Brazil ban may shift part of international brands’ Brazil budgets to Asia, which could raise Philippine traffic acquisition costs; this still needs to be verified. Keep the Brazil budget frozen and keep the “operations suspended” notice on websites and social channels; the next checkpoint is the 23 November congressional deadline.

SIGNAL 02 · PHILIPPINES · PAGCOR

Philippine Q3 GGR fell 11.4% to PHP83.7 billion; e-games fell 23% while licensed casinos slipped just 0.4%.

PAGCOR (the Philippine Amusement and Gaming Corporation) reported on 7 November that industry gross gaming revenue (GGR, total bets minus player winnings) for Q3 (July to September) was PHP83.72 billion (about TWD41.9 billion), down 11.41% year on year and narrower than the 20.33% fall in Q2. Electronic gaming (e-games, e-bingo and bingo grantees) brought in PHP35.28 billion, down 22.86%, about PHP10.45 billion less and 97% of the total decline; licensed casinos, including the Entertainment City integrated resorts, took PHP45.38 billion, down just 0.40%, or 54.2% of the industry, once again the largest source; PAGCOR’s own Casino Filipino made PHP3.03 billion, down 4.81%. By our deadline these quarterly figures had appeared only in Asia Gaming Brief citing BusinessMirror; PAGCOR’s website had not yet posted a release with the same numbers. Chairman Tengco blamed inflation and geopolitical uncertainty for weaker spending and said full-year GGR will be at least PHP350 billion, a “conservative” target. In June Tengco had warned that full-year GGR could fall by as much as 19%. The industry figures match PAGCOR’s own accounts published at the end of October: nine-month revenue down 23.4%, with income from e-gaming down 37%. PAGCOR is also weighing a sale of its loss-making Casino Filipino branches. In the same week, online brands owned by land-based casinos moved the other way: Okada Manila’s Okada Play passed PHP100 million in October GGR with more than 100,000 daily users (see §5), while DigiPlus, the largest pure online operator, saw Q3 revenue fall 32% (see §5).


Bottom line: E-games down a fifth while land-based is nearly flat continues the first-half pattern: after deposit channels were restricted, what fell was pure online operators’ old business, not overall demand. From this we infer that online platforms run by land-based casinos (Okada Play, OnlyFun.PH, LaVie) are taking over this space, because they can steer on-site members online. For pure online licensed operators −EV; for integrated resorts’ online brands +EV. Pure online operators can shift Q4 budget from acquisition to retention and deposit guidance, and show how to verify their PAGCOR licence in creative. Read original →

SIGNAL 03 · GLOBAL · FLUTTER

Flutter booked an $873 million Brazil impairment and a $1.15 billion Q3 net loss; FanDuel EBITDA was about $3 million.

Flutter reported Q3 on 4 November: revenue of $3.87 billion (about TWD124 billion), up 2%; adjusted EBITDA of about $360 million, down about 25%; a net loss of about $1.148 billion, driven by an $873 million non-cash Brazil impairment, mainly $541 million of goodwill and $296 million of long-lived assets. Flutter owns 56% of Flutter Brazil, so about $537 million of the post-tax charge is attributable to the group. The company said Brazil’s 25 September decree banned sports betting and online casino, and Flutter closed its Brazilian operations on 26 September; even before the ban, Brazil revenue was below expectations because of trading conditions and socio-economic measures. In the US, FanDuel’s Q3 adjusted EBITDA was about $3 million versus $51 million a year earlier, down 94%, which the company attributed to investment in its FanDuel Predicts prediction-market product and its July launch in Alberta, Canada; revenue was hit by customer-friendly results. FanDuel Predicts had 66,000 monthly active players in Q3, up 39% quarter on quarter, with $119 million of volume and immaterial revenue; the company expects prediction-market investment to stay at similar or higher levels in 2027. Full-year guidance was cut by $300 million on revenue and $160 million on EBITDA; the company paused share buybacks to prioritise reducing debt. CEO Dan Taylor mentioned the Nebraska ballot defeat on the earnings call (see Signal 05) but said there is still a “strong pipeline of further state openings”.


Bottom line: The world’s largest online gambling group writing off its Brazil assets in one go amounts to a public assessment that the ban will not be lifted soon, consistent with the court moves in Signal 01. For international operators still betting on a Brazil reopening −EV. FanDuel’s 94% profit drop was driven mainly by prediction-market spending, the same reason DraftKings gave for its guidance cut (see §4), so both US leaders are trading profit for prediction-market users this year. With buybacks paused and debt reduction first, Flutter may hold back on large acquisitions and sponsorships in new markets in 2027, −EV for regional operators looking for buyers or partners (inference). SIGNAL 04 · US · PREDICTION MARKETS

Kalshi lost three federal cases in a week, including the Ninth and Second Circuits and the first tribal injunction against it.

Around 3 November the Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals denied Kalshi’s petition for panel rehearing and rehearing en banc in the Nevada case; no judge asked for a vote, so the 28 August ruling that Nevada may enforce stands, and Kalshi has 90 days to petition the Supreme Court. On 3 November the Second Circuit denied injunctions pending appeal for Kalshi in New York and Connecticut and for Coinbase in Connecticut, so both states can enforce while the appeals continue. On 5 November, Judge Jacqueline Scott Corley of the Northern District of California, following the Ninth Circuit’s September ruling, barred Kalshi from offering sports contracts on the plaintiff tribes’ Indian lands (Blue Lake Rancheria, Chicken Ranch Rancheria), covering everything from football and basketball to esports, chess and combat sports; the tribes must provide boundary maps by 13 November. Attorney Daniel Wallach called it the first preliminary injunction a tribe has won against Kalshi. So far only the Third Circuit (the New Jersey case) has ruled for Kalshi, and New Jersey has asked the Supreme Court to take the case. The tribal case runs on a different law: not state gambling law but the federal Indian Gaming Regulatory Act (IGRA); the Ninth Circuit found in September that the tribes are “likely” to prove Kalshi’s sports contracts are unauthorised Class III gaming, and that Kalshi can violate it without having signed a tribal compact. In Utah’s criminal case, the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) and the Justice Department asked federal Judge Robert Shelby to halt the state prosecution; according to the federal docket summary, 6 November was only a roughly 32-minute status and scheduling conference that neither granted nor denied the injunction, Utah must respond by 11 November, and the injunction hearing is set for 20 November (docket text to be confirmed), so the state’s criminal and civil cases can proceed until then. In the same week the Arkansas Attorney General also sued Kalshi (see §7).


Bottom line: The courts’ reasoning has moved from “can state law apply” to “the rules of wherever the user is apply”: the California case holds that buying a contract on tribal land is gambling there, which undercuts prediction markets’ core claim that federal registration makes them valid nationwide. For Kalshi and Polymarket −EV; for state-licensed US sportsbooks and tribal casinos +EV. The final word depends on whether the Supreme Court takes the issue; Kalshi’s reply to New Jersey’s petition is due on 7 December. DraftKings is using prediction markets as a gateway into new states (see §4), and the legal risk on that route is rising. Read original →

SIGNAL 05 · US · BALLOT

Nebraska rejected online sports betting about 55% to 45%, despite DraftKings and FanDuel each putting in $10.4 million.

On 3 November Nebraska voted on two linked measures: Initiative 440 amending the state constitution and Initiative 441 setting the rules; both had to pass. Unofficial Secretary of State results show 440 failing 45.1% to 54.9%; 441 stood at 45.5% to 54.5% with 91% counted. The backers, Tax Relief Nebraska, raised nearly $22.5 million (about TWD720 million) through 29 October, with DraftKings and FanDuel giving $10.4 million each; State Treasurer Spellerberg said the opponents, Save Nebraska Sports, were outspent 20 to 1. Support was concentrated around Omaha, with Douglas and Sarpy counties at 52% to 48%; central counties opposed by 60% or more. Former Huskers football coach Tom Osborne led 45 current and former student athletes and coaches in a letter against the measures, citing game manipulation and harassment of athletes over losing bets. The measures would have let each of the state’s 6 racetrack casinos run two online brands, taxed at 20%, with 70% of tax revenue going to property tax relief. Online betting stays illegal; in-person sports betting at racetracks is unaffected; prediction markets still offer sports contracts there under federal oversight. The fight spilled into a legislative race: on 27 October the backers gave $750,000 to Tim Anderson, the Republican candidate in District 38, for ads in his name supporting online betting, and US Senator Ricketts, the state treasurer and the state auditor withdrew their endorsements. Ricketts argued that for a $250,000 home the property tax relief is about $1 a month, while Nebraskans would have to lose $800 million to generate that tax. Flutter CEO Dan Taylor said on the earnings call the next day that the company was disappointed and would learn from it.


Bottom line: The operators spent the most and still lost to the “protect athletes, too many ads” argument, which shows that in conservative states a tax-relief promise does not beat concern for sports integrity. For US operators planning to open new states by ballot −EV; for prediction markets already offering sports contracts there, short-term +EV (inference). When the Philippines debates online gambling limits, opponents also centre on protecting athletes and young people, so brands should assess this kind of reputational risk before sponsoring university sports such as the UAAP. Regulation−NegativeIreland · regulator study

An Irish study finds 54% of online gamblers open a second account.

GRAI · SBC News · Irish Examiner · 11/04

Ireland’s Gambling Regulatory Authority (GRAI, a new regulator set up under the 2024 gambling act and phasing in licensing) on 4 November published research it commissioned from the Economic and Social Research Institute (ESRI). In a sample of about 1,000 adult gamblers, 77% had bet online in the past year, 59% in person and 44% both. Among online account holders, 54% had opened a second account and about one in ten held four or more, citing good odds, easy sign-up and promotions. Nearly four in ten who opened an account for horse racing, sport or lotteries went on to online casino, slots and scratch cards. The study points to an “asymmetry”: opening accounts and depositing is easy, closing accounts and withdrawing is hard; 57% of online players ended their most recent session with less money than they started with. The GRAI said the research “confirms the need for new obligations” on licensed operators.


Bottom line: The regulator has written cross-product steering and hard-to-close accounts into its research findings, and upcoming licence conditions may limit push messages and offers that move sports customers into casino (inference). For operators in Ireland that acquire with sport and earn with casino −EV. Philippine licensed operators’ sports creative also often steers to casino games; checking disclosure and opt-out design for cross-product pushes now costs less than waiting for the regulator to name names. The GRAI is opening licence applications in phases, and the new obligations will be written into licence conditions, so the first licensees will be hit first.

Operator＋PositiveFrance · Banijay deal

France clears Betclic owner Banijay’s takeover of JOA’s 33 casinos without conditions.

Le Club des juristes · iGaming Expert · 11/04–11/05

France’s competition authority (Autorité de la concurrence) approved on 4 November Banijay Gaming’s acquisition of sole control of JOA Group, with no conditions. JOA runs 33 land-based casinos in France; Banijay Gaming owns the online sports betting and poker brand Betclic, and at the end of July also announced a deal for a majority stake in Germany’s Tipico. The authority reviewed online poker and land-based casinos and found no competition concern, given the parties’ modest shares, strong rivals and poker’s small share of JOA’s revenue. The deal was signed on 6 July and still needs Interior Ministry approval and staff consultation before closing. France allows only online sports betting, horse racing and poker; online casino remains illegal, and French industry commentators see the deal as leverage in pushing for online casino legalisation.


Bottom line: An online operator buying land-based casino licences is a way to claim a position while online casino is still illegal in France: if it opens, governments usually look first to existing casino operators. For Betclic +EV; for online operators without land-based licences −EV (inference). In the Philippines the same logic runs in reverse: integrated resorts are launching their own online platforms (see Signal 02). What both directions share is that one group holds land-based licences and online users at once, so whichever side the regulator tightens, it can steer customers to the other.

Arkansas sued VGW and Kalshi on the same day under consumer protection law.

Covers · SBC Americas · 11/04–11/05

Arkansas Attorney General Tim Griffin filed two civil suits on 4 November, both under the state’s Deceptive Trade Practices Act. The suit against sweepstakes casino operator VGW (Chumba Casino, LuckyLand and Global Poker, with ten affiliated companies named) was filed in White County Circuit Court and argues that users betting “Sweeps Coins” redeemable for cash are gambling, and the dual-currency model does not change that; VGW says it does not run a gambling business. Both suits seek restitution, penalties, taxes and injunctions. Sweepstakes casinos run on two currencies: entertainment coins can be bought, while sweeps coins can only be obtained free and can be redeemed for cash.


Bottom line: The Arkansas constitution allows gambling only at a few licensed casinos and racetracks, so suing under gambling law would first require proving that sweepstakes and event contracts are gambling; suing under consumer protection law instead sidesteps the “is it gambling” definition fight, and also puts sweepstakes casinos and prediction markets into the same framework. Arkansas has no sweepstakes ban statute and is getting a similar result through litigation; the number of states with an explicit ban is still 10, and the next legislative checkpoint is Wyoming’s January 2027 session. For sweepstakes operators −EV; for licensed US online casinos +EV. Stake.us belongs to the same group as Stake, and state findings that “dual currency is gambling” may be cited by regulators elsewhere.
