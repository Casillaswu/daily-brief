Regulation−NegativeBrazil · Congress deadline

MP 1394’s initial 60-day period ends 23 November; the ban stays in force.

Congresso Nacional, MPV 1394 page · read 11/08

MP 1,394 remains in force. Congress lists the initial 60-day period as 25 September–23 November 2026 and urgency from 9 November. If both Houses have not completed voting, a further 60-day period applies automatically, with the clock paused during congressional recess. November 23 is therefore neither automatic expiry of the ban nor permission to reopen. Lance! projects a possible outer deadline in early March 2027 after recess, but an official final date still needs to be tracked. The measure bans the operation, offering, brokering and advertising of fixed-odds betting, including sports betting and online games, terminates existing authorisations within 30 days, requires player refunds, restricts advertising, sponsorship and related payments, and permits site/app blocking and creates an inter-agency enforcement committee; payments needed for winding down and refunds are excepted. Congress records 99 amendments submitted, including amendment 18 proposing a proportional refund for the unexpired licence term, not an enacted refund entitlement. R7 reports that Randolfe Rodrigues said on 5 November he would seek committee installation the following week and voting this year. This is a government objective, not a confirmed timetable. The reported allocation of the chair to the Senate and rapporteur to the Chamber does not mean individuals have been appointed. Next checkpoint: committee installation and rapporteur arrangements in the week beginning 9 November.

Supporting sources: Congress: provisional-measure procedure


Bottom line: Brazilian licensees still face an adverse regulatory position, but the review timetable does not establish an earliest reopening date. The refund proposal remains under consideration; subsequent legislative, judicial and regulatory action will determine operators’ positions.

Market○NeutralUK · illegal-market data

UKGC estimates about 4% of online gamblers used an illegal site in the past year.

UK Gambling Commission · 11/05; banking study · 10/28

On 5 November the Gambling Commission (GC) published the first year of GSGB Illegal Gambling Index results and updated its dashboard. Fieldwork covered April 2025–April 2026. Among all respondents, 94.4% showed no indication of use; 0.6% were classified as users in the past four weeks and a further 0.4% in the past year but not the past four weeks. Another 4.6% showed limited indications, insufficient for classification in those use groups. Excluding National-Lottery-only players, the corresponding figures for online gamblers were 2.5% and 1.5%, about 4% combined. Engagement was higher among men; ages 18–24 were particularly prominent on the past-year measure, and ages 25–34 on the past-four-week measure. GC Executive Director for Operations Sue Young described population-level indicators as low and stable while highlighting greater harm risks among illegal-site users. A separate, non-nationally-representative banking study published on 28 October found that 91% of people with repeated detected illegal transactions also transacted with licensed operators. Next checkpoint: data covering periods after April 2026 and comparable methodology.


Bottom line: Remote Gaming Duty rose from 21% to 40% on 1 April 2026, but this survey cannot establish the effect on illegal-market migration, so it does not support an operator EV call on tax. The index combines seven self-reported behaviours with strength and recency weights, rather than asking one “likely unlicensed” question. The banking sample’s 91% is neither a market-wide estimate nor evidence that product-led retention works. Other markets would need local validation, with consumer protection as the focus.

Regulation−NegativeNetherlands · supplier order

Ksa orders supplier Endorphina to stop its games reaching unlicensed Dutch sites.

Kansspelautoriteit (Ksa) decisions · issued 10/19, published 11/02

On 2 November the Ksa published a binding direction issued on 19 October to Endorphina Limited (Malta) and Endorphina Ltd. (Cyprus). It found inadequate safeguards against use of their games on Netherlands-unlicensed sites, contrary to article 1(1)(b) of the gambling law, and required measures and a report within two weeks of the decision. Technical controls and client/distributor contracts must prevent Netherlands players from accessing the games on sites without a Ksa licence; this is not a worldwide supply ban. The decision documents Dutch-IP checks in January–February, June and late September, including starzino.com, amonbet.com, luckygem.com and games such as Hell Hot 100. The operators of qbet.com and luckygem.com had already received cease-and-desist orders backed by non-compliance penalties, but Endorphina games remained playable. Endorphina disputed responsibility and the technical evidence, citing its B2B role and licensed intermediaries. An objection is possible but does not automatically suspend the direction. The publication decision also warns other suppliers and intermediaries. Next checkpoint: compliance and objection outcomes, and further supplier directions.


Bottom line: This case extends Netherlands compliance pressure into game supply and distribution. Suppliers serving Netherlands players should review onward distribution, technical restrictions and contractual duties. Competitive gains for other suppliers, or adoption of similar rules in the Philippines, remain unproven. Renewal reviews can address territorial licences, onward-distribution duties and audit rights under local law.

01 · LEAD Regulation −Negative Brazil · reopening order suspended

CDA’s interim reopening order is suspended.

TRF3 · STF · Lance! · CNN Brasil · 3–7 Nov

TRF3, the federal appeals court for São Paulo and Mato Grosso do Sul, issued interim relief signed by Judge Adriana Pileggi on 3 November for CDA Gaming, covering Casa de Apostas, Betsul and Jogo Online. The order records a R$30 million fee (about TWD179 million) and an authorisation originally valid through 31 December 2029. The judge raised confiscation and legal-certainty concerns about ending it without refund or compensation; this was reasoning in a company-specific interim order. On 6 November, TRF3 President Luís Antonio Johonsom di Salvo granted the application submitted by the Attorney General’s Office (AGU) on behalf of the federal government to suspend its effects on public-order and uniform-policy grounds until final judgment in the original action or a further presidency decision. PGR also applied in STF case ADI 8027 for a nationwide stay of similar proceedings. The application itself is not a nationwide stay.

The STF held its hearing on 3 November. The advance list contained 34 invited entities; CNN identified at least 22 that had publicly opposed the ban before the hearing, not a tally of speeches or votes. Fux expressed his personal opposition to gambling and discussed legal certainty, legitimate expectations and possible modulation of effects, without delivering a merits ruling for the Court. On 4 November, Fachin indicated that related municipal-lottery, gambling and betting cases could be considered together; no date was set. Finance Minister Durigan separately said betting-revenue assumptions in the 2027 budget would be revised (see §6).

Sports and betting representatives raised risks to sponsorship and migration underground, while government representatives defended the ban on health, household-finance and enforcement grounds. A late-October IBJR scenario modeled roughly 101% higher annual illegal-market GGR if 70% of previously licensed activity migrated underground. It is not measured market growth. CDA’s initial request had been rejected at first instance; the subsequent interim appellate relief applied only to CDA and its effects are now suspended.

Supporting sources: CNN: PGR request · STF: morning hearing account · STF: afternoon hearing account


Bottom line: CDA’s interim basis for reopening has been suspended, but PGR’s application cannot be described as a nationwide stay or proof that every route to individual relief is closed. Fux’s personal remarks do not establish whether the Court will uphold the ban, award compensation or allow reopening. The position remains adverse for Brazilian licensees. Operational decisions and public notices should follow the orders and regulatory instructions applicable to each company. Watch urgency from 9 November, committee progress and new judicial decisions; 23 November is the first 60-day checkpoint only.

SIGNAL 02 · PHILIPPINES · PAGCOR

Philippine Q3 GGR falls 11.41%.

PAGCOR (the Philippine Amusement and Gaming Corporation) reported on 6 November that industry gross gaming revenue (GGR, stakes less payouts to players) for Q3 (July to September) was PHP83.72 billion (about TWD43.5 billion), down 11.41% year on year and narrower than the 20.33% fall in Q2. Electronic gaming (e-games, e-bingo and bingo grantees) brought in PHP35.28 billion, down 22.86%, about PHP10.45 billion less and 97% of the total decline; licensed casinos, including the Entertainment City integrated resorts, took PHP45.38 billion, down just 0.40%, or 54.2% of the industry, once again the largest source; PAGCOR’s own Casino Filipino made PHP3.03 billion, down 4.81%. These totals and components come from PAGCOR’s official release. The three categories sum to PHP83.69 billion, PHP0.03 billion below the reported total; the original figures are retained and the difference remains unreconciled. Chairman Tengco blamed inflation and geopolitical uncertainty for weaker spending and was reported by BusinessMirror as expecting full-year GGR of at least PHP350 billion, a “conservative” target. In June Tengco had warned that full-year GGR could fall by as much as 19%. Separately, PAGCOR’s own accounts released on 29 October (not industry GGR) showed nine-month revenue down 23.4%, with income from e-gaming down 37%. A 14 October report also said PAGCOR was weighing a sale of its loss-making Casino Filipino branches, subject to presidential approval. In the same week, online brands owned by land-based casinos moved the other way: Okada Manila’s Okada Play passed PHP100 million in October GGR with more than 100,000 daily users (see §5), while DigiPlus, a major online gaming operator, saw Q3 revenue fall 32% (see §5).

Supporting sources: AGB / BusinessMirror: statements and background


Bottom line: The decline was concentrated in electronic gaming while licensed casinos were relatively stable. This does not rule out weaker overall demand or establish migration to resort-owned online brands. Okada Play’s October growth is not directly comparable with the industry’s Q3 year-on-year decline; comparable OnlyFun.PH results have not been disclosed, and LaVie’s self-operated Phase Two platform is planned for mid-to-late November. Resorts may benefit from their brands and existing members (inference), but a sustained advantage requires comparable retention, deposit and acquisition-cost data. Read original →

SIGNAL 03 · GLOBAL · FLUTTER

Flutter books an $873 million non-cash Brazil impairment.

Flutter reported results for the quarter ended September 30 on November 4: revenue of $3.87 billion (about TWD127 billion), up 2%; adjusted EBITDA of about $360 million, down about 25%; and a consolidated net loss of about $1.148 billion, driven by an $873 million non-cash Brazil impairment. Its main components included $541 million of goodwill and $296 million of long-lived assets; these were not the full breakdown. Flutter owns 56% of Flutter Brazil, and the company reported a $537 million post-tax impairment attributable to the group. Brazil’s September 25 decree banned sports betting and online casino, and Flutter closed local operations on September 26. Even before the ban, Brazil revenue was below expectations because of trading conditions and socio-economic measures. The US segment reported adjusted EBITDA of $3 million versus $51 million a year earlier, down about 94%. The company cited FanDuel Predicts, its July Alberta launch and World Cup marketing, alongside higher promotional and payment-processing costs; it did not quantify prediction-market spending as the largest cause. The US Q3 sportsbook margin decline mainly reflected higher promotions; the material sports-results headwind was in International and early Q4 trading. FanDuel Predicts averaged 66,000 monthly players in Q3, up 39% quarter on quarter, with $119 million of volume; revenue remained immaterial in the quarter. The company expects prediction-market investment at similar or higher levels in 2027. Full-year revenue and adjusted EBITDA guidance midpoints were cut by $300 million and $160 million, respectively. Buybacks were paused, with organic investment and debt reduction prioritised. In the earnings-call transcript (secondary), CEO Dan Taylor mentioned the Nebraska ballot defeat (see Signal 05) but said the pipeline of further state openings remained strong, without predicting individual states or timing.


Bottom line: International gambling group Flutter’s Brazil impairment reflects weaker trading, the ban and regulatory uncertainty; it does not establish a reopening timetable or mean every Brazil asset was written down to zero. Near-term financial measures are under pressure (−EV), while reopening still depends on courts and policy. Flutter and DraftKings are both investing in prediction markets, but their earnings measures and causes of decline differ; Flutter’s US decline cannot be attributed entirely to that investment. The buyback pause places greater emphasis on organic investment and deleveraging. Any reduction in 2027 acquisitions or sponsorship remains unconfirmed. Read original →

SIGNAL 04 · US · PREDICTION MARKETS

Kalshi faces adverse interim court developments.

The Ninth Circuit denied panel rehearing and rehearing en banc in the Nevada case on 4 November. DeFi Rate’s account of the order says the panel was unanimous and no judge requested an en-banc vote. The 28 August ruling removing preliminary protection remains in place, leaving Nevada enforcement unblocked by that injunction; it does not finally resolve every merits issue. Absent an extension, the ordinary 90-day period to petition for Supreme Court review ends on 2 February 2027 (Supreme Court Rule 13). The Second Circuit’s 3 November order denied injunctions pending appeal in three cases: Kalshi’s New York and Connecticut appeals and Coinbase’s Connecticut appeal. The requested injunctions do not block the states’ enforcement while the merits appeals continue. Judge Jacqueline Scott Corley of the Northern District of California issued a preliminary injunction on 4 November; reports followed on 5 November. It bars Kalshi from offering sports-event contracts on the plaintiff tribes’ Indian lands, not throughout California, and lists sports including football, basketball, esports, chess and combat sports. The tribes must supply boundary maps by 13 November; Kalshi must report compliance within 21 days of the order, by 25 November. The injunction was not first issued on the map deadline. DeFi Rate reports that attorney Daniel Wallach described it as the first preliminary injunction obtained by a tribe against Kalshi; this brief did not independently survey every case nationwide. Within the state-enforcement circuit split discussed here, the Third Circuit’s New Jersey ruling favours Kalshi; New Jersey has petitioned the Supreme Court for review. The tribal case applies the federal Indian Gaming Regulatory Act (IGRA). Under the preliminary-injunction likelihood-of-success test, the Ninth Circuit’s 16 September opinion found the tribes likely to establish that sports-contract trading on their lands constitutes unauthorised Class III gaming, even though Kalshi is not a party to the tribal compact. That is not a final merits finding. In Utah, the United States and CFTC asked federal Judge Robert J. Shelby for a temporary restraining order and preliminary injunction against the state prosecution. The 6 November ECF 17 docket entry records a 32-minute conference, from 1:00 to 1:32 pm. The entry neither grants nor denies an injunction and does not itself halt state enforcement. Utah’s response is due 11 November, the plaintiffs’ reply on 16 November and a limited surreply on 18 November; the hearing is set for 20 November at 1:30 pm. A hearing is not a guaranteed decision date. Arkansas also filed a separate suit against Kalshi on 4 November (see §7).


Bottom line: The tribal case shows that sports-contract trading on tribal land can constitute gaming there under IGRA; it does not establish a universal rule for every location or contract. These orders directly create legal risk for Kalshi. Competitive effects on Polymarket, other prediction markets, state-licensed sportsbooks and tribal casinos remain an inference. Supreme Court review has not been granted; Kalshi’s response to New Jersey’s certiorari petition is due 7 December. DraftKings’ strategy of entering new states through prediction markets (see §4) requires case-by-case assessment of orders and geographic scope, rather than treating federal registration as a nationwide guarantee of legality. SIGNAL 05 · US · BALLOT

Nebraska unofficial results reject online sports betting.

Nebraska voted on two linked measures on 3 November: Initiative 440 would amend the constitution and 441 would set the rules; both were needed for legalisation. The Secretary of State’s unofficial results as of 6 November, 4:59 pm Central time show all 1,322 precincts fully reported, without certifying the final result. Initiative 440 had 335,163 votes for (45.1%) and 408,460 against (54.9%); 441 had 338,237 for (45.5%) and 405,326 against (54.5%). This supersedes the early 91%-counted snapshot. Nebraska Examiner’s review of filings puts Tax Relief Nebraska’s fundraising through 29 October at nearly $22.5 million (about TWD737 million), including about $10.4 million each from DraftKings, through DK Crown, and FanDuel. Fundraising is not spending. Spellerberg estimated that opponents Save Nebraska Sports were outspent roughly 20 to 1; this brief did not independently retrieve the individual filings. In LSR’s 4 November early-results report, Initiative 441 stood at about 52% for and 48% against in Douglas and Sarpy counties, with about 64% opposition in Buffalo County and 60% in Hall County. These are examples, not every central county; updated county returns were not independently checked. WOWT reports that 45 current or former players and coaches in total, including former Huskers coach Tom Osborne, signed an opposition letter citing sporting integrity and player-harassment risks. Those arguments do not establish specific match manipulation or every voter’s motive. The official measure pamphlet would have allowed each authorised operator up to two online wagering platforms. Tax would be 20% of gross gaming revenue, with 70% of gaming-tax receipts going to property-tax relief, not 70% of wagers. The vote did not legalise online sports betting or change the existing retail framework. Prediction markets continue to offer contracts, but federal registration is not a final finding of legality under state law. Local reporting says the campaign gave $750,000 on 27 October to Tim Anderson, the Republican candidate in District 38, for advertising supporting online betting; Ricketts, Spellerberg and Foley withdrew their endorsements. Ricketts estimated about $1 a month in relief for a $250,000 home against $800 million in aggregate losses; this brief has not independently verified his model or time period. LSR reports that Flutter’s Dan Taylor expressed disappointment and said it would learn from the result on the 4 November earnings call.


Bottom line: Heavy fundraising did not secure passage. Athlete protection and advertising concerns were opposition arguments, but the result alone does not establish why voters rejected the measures or a rule for all conservative states. This is a setback for operators seeking entry through this ballot; there are no outcome data showing a benefit to prediction markets. Brands sponsoring university sport can include athlete protection and gambling advertising in reputational-risk assessments without treating the US vote as evidence of Philippine public opinion. Regulation−NegativeIreland · regulator study

54% in Irish study opened another gambling account.

GRAI · ESRI full research report · 11/04

On 4 November Ireland’s Gambling Regulatory Authority (GRAI), established under the 2024 Act and implementing licensing in phases, published research it commissioned from ESRI. The sample comprised 1,000 Irish adults who spent money gambling in the past year, excluding people whose only gambling was buying lottery tickets or scratchcards in person. In this sample, 77% gambled online, 59% in person and 44% both. Of those who had ever opened an online account, 54% later opened another; around one in ten online gamblers held four or more. Odds, easy registration and offers were common reasons. Among horse- or sports-betting account starters, 38% later tried more immediate forms such as online casino, slots, bingo or scratchcards; this percentage does not include lottery starters. Opening accounts and depositing were generally easier than closing accounts and withdrawing, with some users reporting difficulties. Some 57% reported losing money in their most recent online session. GRAI said the findings supported stronger obligations on accounts, spending limits and advertising.


Bottom line: The research flags consent and opt-outs for notifications, cross-product activity, account closure and withdrawals for regulatory attention; it is not a new set of licence conditions or an implementation timetable. Operators relying on cross-product promotion should monitor potential compliance changes (inference). Track formal rules, commencement dates and the licence categories affected. Philippine operators can review consent, cross-product disclosure, opt-outs, account closure and withdrawals without assuming Irish rules apply locally.

Operator＋PositiveFrance · Banijay deal

France clears Banijay’s acquisition of JOA.

French competition authority · 11/04; Banijay releases · 07/06 and 04/23

On 4 November France’s competition authority unconditionally cleared Banijay Gaming’s acquisition of exclusive control of JOA, which operates 33 French casinos. Banijay owns Betclic and completed its Tipico acquisition on 23 April 2026. The JOA acquisition commitment was announced under a put-option agreement on 6 July. At that time, completion was conditional on employee-representative consultation and regulatory approvals; this decision confirms competition clearance. The authority assessed the combination of online poker and land-based casinos and found no likely harm given market shares, significant competitors and poker’s limited contribution to JOA’s business. France’s competitive online licensing covers sports betting, horse-race betting and poker; FDJ also offers online lotteries under monopoly rights. Online casinos remain prohibited, and the authority explicitly considered legalisation too hypothetical to include in its assessment.


Bottom line: Removing the competition hurdle is positive for Banijay’s transaction. Combining online and land-based operations offers cross-channel opportunities, subject to product licences, marketing consent and consumer-protection rules. The deal does not establish imminent online-casino legalisation or priority for existing casinos in future licensing. Effects on other online operators depend on actual rules and execution.
