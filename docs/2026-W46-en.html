Regulation−NegativeBrazil · Congress deadline (Trigger 4)

Brazil’s Congress lists MP 1394 deliberation deadline as 4 March 2027.

Congresso Nacional — MPV 1394 page · checked 12 Nov, rechecked 15 Nov

Brazil’s Congress currently lists 25 September 2026–4 March 2027 as the deliberation period (Deliberação) for Provisional Measure 1.394 (MP 1394), marked “Prorrogado” (extended). The current measure prohibits fixed-odds betting and related advertising and sponsorship. The 4 March date is the current congressional deadline, not a guarantee against legislative or judicial changes before then, and not a reopening date. Last week’s 23 November date was only the first 60-day period. Poder360 reported a 60-day extension on 11 November and explained the March date by the year-end recess pausing the clock. The urgency procedure applies from 9 November, while the joint committee is still listed as awaiting installation. The primary extension act, its number and date were not read in this round.

Next checkpoint: committee installation, rapporteur and voting arrangements; Congress returns in February 2027. Track the STF litigation separately.


Bottom line: The current restrictions remain negative for licensed operators in Brazil, but neither 4 March nor the end of Q1 is a guaranteed minimum duration for the ban. Flamengo and Betano’s suspension talks (Signal 01) show pressure on existing contracts. Track Congress and the STF separately and reassess advertising, sponsorship and influencer plans when the legal position changes. Expiry of the MP should not itself be treated as automatic restoration of old authorisations.

Regulation○NeutralBrazil · refund mechanism (Trigger 3)

SPA opens R$5m reserves for refunds.

Ministry of Finance (Fazenda), Prizes and Betting Secretariat notice · 11 Nov

What it means: betting operators shut down by MP 1394 that do not have enough cash to return player balances can apply to use the R$5 million (about NT$29.85 million) financial reserve each of them must hold, with state bank Caixa paying the refunds on their behalf. The basis is Normative Instruction 32 (IN SPA/MF nº 32) from the Prizes and Betting Secretariat (SPA, the betting regulator inside the Finance Ministry), signed on 6 November; the reserve obligation comes from Ordinance 615 of 2024. The process is: the operator applies to the SPA → the SPA approves → the money moves to Caixa → Caixa refunds players. The money may only be used to return player balances, not for salaries, taxes or wind-down costs; only after refunds are complete and no payments are outstanding can the operator ask for the remainder back. The notice sets no refund deadline and names no operator that has applied. The ministry’s 11 November explanation states that IN 32 was published on 10 November; 6 November is the instruction’s date. These dates serve different purposes. The full Official Gazette (DOU) text was not read this round.

Next checkpoint: the first operator to apply publicly to use its reserve · date not set.


Bottom line: The government is laying out a wind-down process for after the ban, which signals it expects some operators to be unable to repay player balances. For players this limits the damage; for operators it is bad news, because the reserve is locked for refunds and the money only comes back after the wind-down. Each reserve is only R$5 million, and the notice does not say how any shortfall is covered if a large platform owes players more than that. For brands that hope to return to Brazil, how they handle player balances now will directly affect trust when the market reopens.

01 · LEAD Market −Negative Brazil · shirt sponsorship

Flamengo and Betano discuss suspending a deal worth up to R$268.5m a year.

UOL (via Sportbuzz) · 13 Nov; Diário do Pará · 14 Nov

Flamengo, Brazil’s most popular club, is negotiating a “suspension” of its contract with front-of-shirt sponsor Betano. UOL reported on 13 November, as relayed by Sportbuzz, that the deal pays a fixed R$220 million (about NT$1.31 billion) per season and, with commercial bonuses and other investment, up to about R$268.5 million (about NT$1.60 billion) a year, for a total of roughly R$900 million running to the end of 2028. The report says the suspension is a given; what remains open is compensation. Flamengo has already received the full 2026 season payment in advance, so the unfulfilled period must be repaid in cash or offset with services; October alone was worth about R$20 million. No suspension has been announced and there is no replacement sponsor.

Why now: The current MP 1394 bans advertising and sponsorship for fixed-odds betting. Congress lists a deliberation deadline of 4 March 2027 (see 🌍), which does not guarantee the ban will last until then. The judicial track is separate. On 9 November STF Justice Fux also suspended ordinary-court cases contesting the MP’s constitutionality, and Casa de Apostas, the last brand trading on a court order, shut that day, leaving Brazil with no regulated platform in operation (see §6). The sponsor is paying for exposure it cannot legally get, and the club risks breaking the law by displaying it, so both sides need to freeze the contract first. Betano had already used the main shirt space to print “Proibir não é proteger” (banning is not protecting) in protest against the ban.

This is the first time the ban has hit the biggest single commercial contract in Brazilian football. Most Série A clubs have betting brands as main sponsors, so how Flamengo settles this (the refund share, and whether the shirt space is sold to another industry) will become the reference price for other clubs and betting brands. Fans on X are already asking whether the shirts on Adidas’s website have dropped the Betano logo (see §9); we did not verify the site.

The wind-down side of the same week: CDA Gaming, owner of Casa de Apostas, told users on the morning of 9 November that its platforms had stopped operating and promised to return their money, while the Finance Ministry published the reserve refund rules (see 🌍). Brazilian betting brands are now handling three things at once: refunding player balances, freezing sponsorship and media contracts, and waiting for the STF and Congress. Refunds and contract compensation create cash-flow pressure while operations generate no new revenue; the congressional deadline cannot establish how long any operator can endure.


Bottom line: For betting brands in Brazil this is negative: the current ban restricts sports sponsorship, and the return of exposure depends on changes in the legal position, and money already paid must now be negotiated back. For Brazilian clubs it is a revenue gap, since replacement sponsors from outside gambling are expected to pay far less. For operators considering Brazil long term, the task now is to audit suspension and refund clauses in existing sponsorship, influencer and media contracts rather than sign new ones; if the STF joint ruling or Congress lets the ban lapse, whether exposure rights come back depends on how each contract is written.

SIGNAL 02 · US · LOUISIANA

Louisiana wins a TRO against 31 prediction-market entities, and the CFTC sues the state a day later.

Louisiana Attorney General Liz Murrill sued 31 entities behind 13 brands, including Kalshi, Polymarket, Robinhood and Coinbase, in state court on 12 November. The same day, Judge Jeffrey Johnson of the 21st Judicial District Court in Tangipahoa Parish signed a temporary restraining order (TRO) without hearing the defendants: it bars event contracts tied to sports, elections and awards for anyone in the state, requires geofencing of all of Louisiana, and forbids telling Louisiana users the products are lawful. No bond was required; the TRO expires on 22 November and can be extended in 10-day increments, and a preliminary-injunction hearing is set for 9 a.m. on 30 November. On 13 November the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC, the federal derivatives regulator) and the Justice Department sued the state in the Eastern District of Louisiana, asking the court to declare state gambling law pre-empted for CFTC-regulated markets and to bar the state from enforcing or extending the TRO. CFTC Chairman Selig called the state suit “naked lawfare”; Murrill shot back that he is Kalshi’s “obsequious standard bearer”. After Utah’s criminal case against Kalshi, this is another state naming the whole industry as defendants at once. The defendants had prepared: Polymarket’s US entity QCX sued Louisiana officials in federal court on 27 October on pre-emption and Commerce Clause grounds, and Kalshi sued Murrill in federal court in October and, after the TRO, asked that court for its own restraining order.


Bottom line: For licensed US sportsbooks this leans positive: the state has labelled prediction markets unlicensed gambling outright and won a statewide ban. For Robinhood, Coinbase, Underdog and others that already offer prediction products it is negative: one state judge can force several platforms offline in a day, and whether federal law prevails depends on the federal court. Whether the TRO is extended before 22 November is the nearest checkpoint. Read original →

SIGNAL 03 · EUROPE · SUPPLIERS

Allwyn pays €50m for a perpetual Kambi sportsbook licence.

European lottery and gaming group Allwyn signed with sportsbook technology supplier Kambi on 10 November: Kambi grants Allwyn a global, perpetual licence to its sportsbook platform source code for €50 million (about NT$1.83 billion), reportedly paid over three years. Kambi will also provide implementation, AI trading and risk services, and will build a team that transfers to Allwyn once implementation is complete. Allwyn plans to move brands including OPAP in Greece, win2day in Austria and Sazka Bet in the Czech Republic onto one platform. Kambi has always sold a white-label product for a revenue share, so selling source code is a first for it; Kambi estimates the deal adds about €45 million to €50 million of operating profit over 2027–2031, and its shares closed 17.4% higher that day. Allwyn also announced the same day that it is buying the remaining 30% of instant-games supplier IWG (see §2). Kambi profit estimate.


Bottom line: Large operators have started to work out that buying the code and running their own team is cheaper than paying a revenue share for years. For suppliers this is neutral to negative: one-off licence income replaces long-term revenue share. For smaller operators, white label is still the fastest way to launch, but a source-code licence can be used as leverage in price talks, provided they can afford their own tech team. Read original →

SIGNAL 04 · EUROPE · BULGARIA

Bulgaria backs a draft ad ban and affiliate phase-out.

Bulgaria’s cabinet approved amendments to the Gambling Act on 11 November and sent them to parliament. The draft bans advertising for gambling games, operators and their trademarks in every medium and location, including light boxes in casino and gaming-hall windows; the only exceptions left are sponsorship of sports clubs and associations, and support for culture, healthcare and education. Affiliate marketing (sending traffic to operators for commission) is treated as part of promotion, and every affiliate licence would be cancelled from 1 January 2027, reversing a licensing regime created earlier this year. Foreign online operators would need a local place of business, an online betting licence would cost €400,000 (about NT$14.64 million) up front, and the land-based levy would be 20% of stakes minus winnings. Parliament’s budget committee backed the bill unanimously, and MPs will form a working group to revise three related bills before the first and second readings. Prime Minister Radev said the aim is to keep aggressive marketing away from children and young people. Parliament rejected a similar ad ban and tax rise in July, so final passage is still uncertain.


Bottom line: For operators and affiliates that rely on affiliate traffic in small EU markets this is negative: if the draft passes as written, advertising and affiliate routes would be restricted together, leaving owned channels and limited sports sponsorship. The combination of an ad ban, an end to affiliates and a local-presence rule could become a template other regulators look at (inference). If it passes, affiliate revenue in Bulgaria should be modelled as zero from the end of 2026. Read original →

SIGNAL 05 · PHILIPPINES · PAGCOR

PAGCOR warns of a possible restricted-persons data leak.

PAGCOR (the Philippine gaming regulator) issued a public advisory responding to an online post that allegedly contains data from its National Database of Restricted Persons (NDRP), iGaming Expert reported on 12 November. The NDRP lists people barred from casinos and gambling, including government officials who may not gamble by law, people who asked for self-exclusion, and people blacklisted by licensees. PAGCOR said its Computer Emergency Response Team (PAGCOR-CERT) is verifying the data’s authenticity, source and extent, and checking whether it relates to an earlier incident; at present there is no verified evidence linking the post to a new unauthorised intrusion into PAGCOR’s systems. PAGCOR urged the public to watch for phishing and identity theft and not to open or share the files. In September 2025 hackers claimed an NDRP leak of about 560,000 names, mostly officials, and PAGCOR said then that the data probably came from a licensee with access.


Bottom line: For Philippine licensees this is a compliance risk: it is the second reported NDRP leak in just over a year. Licensees access the NDRP to screen players, so if the source turns out to be an operator, the regulator is likely to tighten access and audits. The list includes self-excluded players, who could be targeted by unlicensed sites if the data is out; compliance teams should check their own NDRP access logs and permissions, and customer service should be ready for “has my data leaked?” questions. Regulation−NegativeIreland · Social Impact Fund

Ireland plans a 1% levy from April 2027.

SBC News · 13 Nov; X · @iGamingExpress · 13 Nov

Ireland has notified the European Commission of draft rules for charging licensees for the Social Impact Fund; the Commission received the notification on 12 November and the standstill runs to 15 February 2027. Under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024, the fund is set up by the regulator (GRAI) to pay for research, education and training, treatment and intervention on gambling harm. The draft sets an annual rate of 1%: ordinary commercial licensees pay on turnover in the financial year before the contribution year, while B2B licensees pay on gross profit from the relevant products or services under the Act; charitable or philanthropic licences are exempt. The rules would take effect on 1 April 2027, with payment due by 1 July each year. In a parliamentary reply on 3 November the minister still said the draft would go to Brussels once finalised; it has now been notified. The government is not funding the scheme.


Bottom line: One percent looks low, but charging on turnover rather than gross profit weighs more heavily on low-margin sportsbooks. Operators in Ireland should budget for the cost from 2027. Elsewhere, this is the model of setting up the fund in law and fixing the rate by regulation later, and it is likely to be cited when the Philippines or Brazil discuss funding for responsible gambling (inference).

Regulation○NeutralAustria · online opening

Allwyn calls Austria’s timeline too tight.

X · @iGamingExpress · 10 Nov; European Gaming · 10 Nov

Austria is replacing its online casino monopoly with multiple licences. The Finance Ministry notified the draft to the EU on 4 August and the EU standstill runs to 7 December, after which parliament can pass it. The draft allows an unlimited number of online casino licences, initially for five years with a possible ten-year extension, at a licence fee of €70,000 (about NT$2.56 million); lotteries stay a monopoly, and the ministry expects about 20 first-round applicants. Allwyn, parent of current monopoly holders Casinos Austria and win2day, argued in a submission to the EU dated 27 October that only about nine months would remain between passage and the 1 October 2027 launch, that the independent regulator has not been set up, and that the federal tax office (Finanzamt Österreich) stays in charge meanwhile, so it doubts supervision will be ready in time. A trade association says an October 2027 launch is feasible.


Bottom line: An incumbent arguing that “supervision is not ready” to slow down opening is a common way to protect a market. For operators hoping to enter Austria it is a negative delay risk, and the launch date could slip. For readers in Brazil and the Philippines it is a reference case of how incumbents stall the move from monopoly to licensing, and it is the same company as in Signal 03.

Bottom line: Bringing non-gambling gaming venues under gambling supervision is usually justified as stopping them from becoming disguised casinos or money-laundering points, but a wider definition adds cost for legitimate esports and café operators. For esports and café businesses in Asia this is an example of the regulatory line moving towards games; it is worth watching whether the text draws the line at money prizes (inference).

Regulation○NeutralGibraltar · prediction markets

Gibraltar regulates two event markets.

European Gaming · SBC News · 10–11 Nov

Gibraltar’s Minister for Justice, Trade and Industry Nigel Feetham opened Europe’s prediction-markets summit in Gibraltar on 10 November, promoting the territory’s regime. The Prediction Market Regulations 2026 took effect on 13 July, carving prediction markets out of the Gambling Act 2025 and requiring every event contract to be approved by the gambling regulator. Two operators are in: ADI Predictstreet holds a betting intermediary licence, and WagerWire’s Wire Markets is “approved in principle” according to SBC News; some reports call it licensed, and this card uses the more cautious wording. More applications are pending. Most European regulators are currently blocking prediction markets, and Gibraltar is so far the only jurisdiction that licenses them. We saw no report of Kalshi or Polymarket applying.


Bottom line: For operators that want to run prediction markets in Europe, Gibraltar offers a legal base, but it is an offshore licence; entering each country still depends on whether local law treats the product as gambling. The state-federal fight in the US (Signal 02) is being opened up in Europe by a small jurisdiction first; for readers in Asia and Brazil it is a case study in regulating prediction markets as a separate category.

Product−NegativeUS · micro betting

Underdog launches live NFL drive markets.

Legal Sports Report · 12 Nov

US fantasy sports and prediction-market operator Underdog launched live NFL drive-by-drive prediction markets, claiming an industry first: users trade on the outcome of a single possession during a game. The timing is sensitive. A few weeks ago the CFTC paused Novig’s micro contracts over manipulation risk, and lawmakers in New Jersey and Pennsylvania are pushing to ban micro betting (very short-window bets on a single play or possession) altogether. LSR notes that prediction markets accept users from 18 while most state-licensed sportsbooks require 21, so consumer protections differ; critics including Representative Ro Khanna call prediction markets disguised betting that evades state rules, and bills in Congress would classify them as illegal gambling.


Bottom line: Micro betting is sports betting’s main route to higher bet frequency, and it is also what regulators worry about most: high manipulation risk and fast, impulsive staking. Launching right after the CFTC acted amounts to testing the federal line; if Underdog is stopped, it strengthens the case for state bans. Product teams in Asia and Brazil planning high-frequency in-play markets should allow for a scenario in which they are banned on their own.

Regulation−NegativeUS · Utah

Utah seeks an injunction to shut Kalshi, with a federal hearing on 20 Nov.

X · @WALLACHLEGAL · 14–15 Nov; SCCG · 9 Nov; Covers · 21 Oct

Utah’s attorney general sued Kalshi and two Susquehanna market-making entities in civil court in Salt Lake County’s Third District on 20 October, alleging unlawful gambling, public nuisance, unjust enrichment and conspiracy, and asked for a preliminary injunction shutting sports, election, politics and other event contracts. According to gaming lawyer Daniel Wallach’s summary of the state’s brief on X, Utah argues that Susquehanna takes opposing positions and profits from sports contracts, so it participates directly and cannot claim to be an “independent market maker”; it also argues that Kalshi’s earlier federal loss bars it from relitigating the same issue, rejecting federal pre-emption. Susquehanna says market making is not gambling. On a separate track, the CFTC and the Justice Department sued Utah to stop state enforcement; federal Judge Shelby holds a hearing on 20 November, with remaining briefs due 16–19 November. Shelby ruled for Utah in Kalshi’s own case in August, and the Tenth Circuit refused an injunction pending appeal in September. Kalshi and its two co-founders also face 14 criminal counts in Utah.


Bottom line: Naming the market maker extends the risk from the platform to the financial firms that supply liquidity; if the state court agrees, market makers may pull back from in-state event contracts and platforms’ odds depth will suffer. That is relatively positive for licensed sportsbooks. The 20 November hearing runs alongside Louisiana (Signal 02), and the two outcomes will shape whether other states follow.

SBC News · 9 Nov; X · @iGamingExpress · 9 Nov

The International Federation of Horseracing Authorities’ (IFHA) anti-illegal betting council analysed 301 sites (74 licensed, 227 illegal) and found that in the first seven months of 2026, UK users made 19.4 visits to illegal sites for every 100 visits to licensed betting sites, against about one in late 2021. The IFHA says success should be measured by sustained falls in illegal traffic, not by the number of URLs taken down.


Bottom line: These figures use a different measure from last week’s Gambling Commission survey (about 4% of online gamblers using illegal sites): a traffic ratio versus a share of people, so they cannot be compared directly.
