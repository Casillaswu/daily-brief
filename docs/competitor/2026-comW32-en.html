▲Executive Briefing

This Week

Three things this week: the president's annual address to Congress (SONA) didn't mention online gambling — the government hasn't decided whether to regulate; PAGCOR's H1 official figures land, revenue -26.6%, net income -85%, eGames -41.9%; integrated resorts enter online, squeezing DigiPlus margins.

1. The president's annual address to Congress didn't mention online gambling — two years running.
SONA (State of the Nation Address) is the Philippine president's yearly report to Congress, where he sets the national agenda and often announces major policy. The 7/27 address (Marcos's fifth) covered the flood-control scandal, tax and power reforms — not a word on online gambling, same as last year. Lawmakers who pushed him to act on it came up empty. The government's line is still "under review, weighing the social impact, no blanket ban for now," with a multi-sectoral discussion being convened. In short: the government still hasn't decided whether or how to regulate — status quo holds. See §04 Card 1.(Sources: Philstar 7/28 · iGamingToday)

2. PAGCOR's H1 official figures land: revenue -26.6% YoY, net income -85%, eGames / eBingo / bingo -41.9%.
The "month-end" figures came out 7/30. H1 total revenue ₱43.3B (~US$705M) versus a record ₱59.1B a year earlier; core gaming-operations revenue ₱38.9B, -27.1%; net income -85% (higher PSC remittances also ate in). The heaviest drag is online — eGames / eBingo / bingo down 41.9% combined to ₱18.6B, tracking directly to last year's BSP order stripping gambling top-up links from e-wallets. This confirms the pool is shrinking structurally, not as a one-off. See §04 Card 2.(Sources: IAG 7/30 · GGRAsia)

3. Online reshuffle: integrated resorts go online (owned / outsourced), squeezing DigiPlus margins.
Abacus Securities' 7/28-7/29 note: Bloomberry (Solaire's parent) leads with Solaire Online and relaunched FunaloMAX; Okada Manila, Newport World Resorts, Hann and NUSTAR use PhilWeb to run online platforms for them. The near-term hit is on margins, not revenue dominance — DigiPlus still holds ~41% share, but its moat is diluted by IR (integrated resort) entry, forcing sustained ad / promo spend to hold share. See §04 Card 3.(Sources: IAG 7/29 · AGB 7/28)

PolicyNo ban, no green light — status quo. The government is still studying whether to regulate; a sudden full ban is unlikely near-term (it just reported a revenue plunge, won't cut its own tax base the same month); on the same track, PAGCOR pushed the B2B accreditation deadline from 7/31 to 9/30 — the regulator keeps easing short-term, not hardening. Keep spending and compliance as normal, but don't read "not mentioned" as a green light.
MarketThe shrinking pool is now official (-26.6% / eGames -41.9%); hold the first-year target at W27's -15~25% cut, its basis now an official half-year figure rather than an estimate.
CompetitionIR entry is the new variable — more owned platforms split the existing pool; while DigiPlus defends share, entrants offset into the "explanation / trust" gap rather than colliding head-on. The sports vacuum is still open — hold it with low-cost content.

§02Strategic Read · 5Q

Strategic Read · 5Q (neutral)

Q1
Market space

The direction is now official: PAGCOR H1 total revenue ₱43.3B (~US$705M), -26.6% YoY; core gaming-operations revenue ₱38.9B, -27.1%; net income -85%. The heaviest drag is online — eGames / eBingo / bingo down 41.9% combined to ₱18.6B, tracking last year's BSP order stripping gambling top-up links from e-wallets. Licensed-casino revenue fell only 3.85% and self-run casinos 8.67% — the gap makes clear the problem is online, not land-based. Brokers cut the full-year GGR estimate another ~7% in the same window.

The recent rate cut (online → 25%, land-based → 30%) still stands as real relief; but next to the -26.6% half-year figure, take is down, compliance spend is up and the pool is still shrinking — net effect for smaller operators is even less certain.

Sources: IAG 7/30 · BusinessWorld 7/30
Q2
What players care about

Player pain points are consistent with recent weeks; the AML advisory + e-wallet break sharpen them:

Cash-out stuck on GCash verification. No real-name / failed verification means limited or blocked withdrawals. Deposits going sideways. After the BSP unbundled GCash / Maya from gambling last August, players route via Lazada vouchers, Viber, crypto apps — more steps, more scam exposure.

The social tone is regret, wanting out, fear of scams. Reddit r/CasualPH: "never again", "addiction"; top searches "how to withdraw", "is it a scam", "how to quit". On X the "fast cash-out / legit" position is occupied by affiliate messaging.

Read: the official half-year figure puts "online shrinking, money harder to move" on the table. Players fear stuck money and scams; the regulator forces everyone to run real-name + source-of-funds checks — whoever frames that as "we verify clearly, so you get paid" converts compliance cost into a trust selling point. "Fast cash-out" is dead; shift to "how real-name works, how to prove source of funds, how long payout takes".

Sources: Reddit r/CasualPH (recent evidence) + PAGCOR H1 · IAG 7/30
Q3
The empty gaps

The four old gaps are still open: nobody turns the ad swap into player-friendly, shareable content; nobody publishes a deposit / alt-payment guide; nobody does the math on what promo terms actually pay out; nobody turns KYC / real-name onboarding into a player-readable guide. All are trust / explanation gaps, not budget gaps.

IR entry surfaces a fifth: several IR platforms will grab existing players at once — whoever first makes "verifiable compliance + clear payout" plain wins trust in the scrum. See §06 Opportunities.

Sources: 7 brands with no new offers + IR entry + AML spillover
Q4
Pricing structure

DigiPlus runs event offers, everyone else runs rebates — no change. 1.5% rebate is table stakes; OKBet, Bet88 and PlayTime share the same number and terms; CasinoPlus's 1.5% expired 7/31 with no successor, pivoting to the Color Game cumulative jackpot crossing ₱100M as a marketing hook — from pricing to IP / jackpot narrative.

The pricing recession continues: the World Cup ended 7/19 (OKBet's FIFA pool and Bet88's World Cup pool run out, no successors), CasinoPlus's 1.5% expired 7/31. Next week watch CasinoPlus's renewal, whether independents fill the sports gap, and whether IR platform launches bring new offers.

Sources: offers read as-is + World Cup 7/19 close + CasinoPlus milestone 7/30
Q5
Differentiation

Differentiation gains a structural line this week: DigiPlus's lopsided dominance starts loosening under IR entry. DigiPlus still has owned sports IP, a physical venue, ₱20B cash plus buyback ammunition and a VIP Club pushing upmarket that nobody matches; now Bloomberry (Solaire Online) + Okada / Newport / Hann / NUSTAR (via PhilWeb) bring their own brands and land-based crowds online — Abacus expects them to slowly eat DigiPlus share and force sustained A&P. On supply, PhilWeb's Pragmatic Play exclusive is still pending approval; once cleared, it again gates PP content.

Conclusion: no new pricing blow-up this week — the structure is moving, and harder than last week: the official half-year figure confirms the shrinking pool (-26.6%), IR entry confirms the leader's turf being split, the president's silence confirms policy isn't settled. All three point one way: the market restructuring, the leader's moat loosening, regulatory direction undecided but the tightening sequence unbroken.

Suggestion: fighting BingoPlus on pools or the IRs on land-based crowds is wrong — each has ammunition you don't. Fight where nobody's yet planted: explain real-name / cash-out / deposit / source-of-funds clearly to players, and while DigiPlus defends against IRs and the IRs rush to launch, offset into the "verifiable compliance + clear payout" trust gap. The government hasn't decided either way — keep compliance steady, don't bet on a reversal. A recommendation, not a conclusion.

Sources: window synthesis · IR entry + official H1 figure + SONA outcome
