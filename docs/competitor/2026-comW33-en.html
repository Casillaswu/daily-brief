▲Executive Briefing

This Week

Three items this week: DigiPlus reported Q2 results — domestic revenue -37% YoY, while users rose QoQ; the leader shifted its growth focus offshore, filing for a New Zealand license, its third international market after Brazil; promotions all carried over unchanged, no new offers this week.

1. DigiPlus reported Q2 results: domestic revenue -37% YoY, users up QoQ.
The Q2 results, previously flagged for around mid-August, were released 8/4-8/5. Group net income rose 124% QoQ to ₱6.98B, but chiefly on a paper fair-value gain from its investment in International Entertainment Corp (IEC) convertible notes; excluding that one-off, core net income rose just 8% QoQ to ₱2.35B, gross gaming revenue (GGR) fell 9% QoQ to ₱15.61B, and revenue was still -37% YoY — the same regulatory-driven structural decline as PAGCOR's official H1 (-26.6%). On the other side, monthly active users (MAU) reached 5.75M and monthly average bettors / depositors (MABD) rose 26% QoQ to 4.68M, the first sign of a domestic user rebound. See §04 Card 1. (Sources: BusinessWorld 8/5 · GGRAsia)

2. The leader shifted growth focus offshore: the board approved a New Zealand online license filing, its third international market after Brazil.
In the same report, DigiPlus's board approved filing an expression of interest for a New Zealand online gaming license. New Zealand opened a competitive online-casino licensing process in July, awarding up to 15 licenses; DigiPlus thereby opens a third international front after Brazil. Read alongside the -37% YoY domestic revenue, the move reflects a more conservative view of PH domestic growth and a shift of growth momentum offshore. See §04 Card 2. (Sources: Manila Bulletin 8/4 · Context.ph 8/4)

3. Promotions all carried over unchanged; this week's change is structural, not promotional.
All 9 scored operators posted no new offers this week: the World Cup has ended, CasinoPlus's 1.5% rebate expired with no successor announced, and independents added no sports promotions. The regulatory front advances but has not landed: PAGCOR's "decoupling" recommendation from the GCG is expected to reach the Palace in August, with the executive order (EO) likely taking shape by year-end, and chairman Tengco pins an H2 rebound on online / e-games; the government's post-SONA "multi-sectoral discussion" still shows no concrete progress. (Source: decoupling · IAG 7/19)

PolicyStatus quo after SONA; the "decoupling" EO won't take shape until year-end, so no hard constraint short-term. Continue spend and compliance as normal, but do not read policy silence as a green light.
MarketThe leader's -37% YoY domestic revenue confirms the structural decline shown in PAGCOR's H1; yet the QoQ user rebound (MABD +26%) is the first counter-signal on whether the domestic market has bottomed. We read this as single-quarter data, insufficient to adjust the first-year cut — note it and wait for Q3 confirmation.
CompetitionThe leader shifting growth focus offshore disperses its domestic management resources, so defensive intensity may ease, leaving room to differentiate; the IR-entry pressure noted in W32 nonetheless persists. We suggest using this window to enter the still-unclaimed "verifiable compliance, clear payout" positioning.

§02Strategic Read · 5Q

Strategic Read · 5Q (neutral)

Q1
Market space

Direction unchanged, with the leader's own results adding confirmation. PAGCOR's official H1 (total revenue -26.6% / eGames -41.9% / net income -85%) already established the structural decline; this week DigiPlus's Q2 provides downstream confirmation — domestic revenue -37% YoY, GGR -9% QoQ, the leader's books likewise bearing the regulatory squeeze. The +124% QoQ net income looks strong on the surface but stems from a one-off paper gain; core operations did not turn (see §04 Card 1).

The counter-signal worth noting is the user rebound: MABD +26% QoQ, MAU 5.75M; chairman Tengco likewise pins an H2 recovery on online / e-games. Read: this is the first counter-signal on whether the domestic market has bottomed, but it is single-quarter and accompanied by a QoQ GGR decline (falling ARPU), insufficient to reverse the call. We hold the first-year cut at -15~25% and will adjust once Q3 users and GGR turn together.

Sources: DigiPlus Q2 · GGRAsia · PAGCOR H1 · IAG 7/30
Q2
What players care about

Player pain points are consistent with recent weeks (not live-scrapable in the sandbox this issue, reusing recent evidence): cash-out blocked on GCash verification; deposits rerouted through alternatives such as Lazada vouchers, Viber and crypto apps, which are cumbersome and prone to impersonation scams. Social sentiment centers on regret, quitting and fraud fear (Reddit r/CasualPH "never again", "addiction"; top searches on withdrawal, scam-checking and quitting); the "fast cash-out / legit" position on X is largely occupied by affiliate accounts.

Read: the leader's users rose QoQ while GGR fell, indicating growth from low-barrier acquisition rather than stronger betting intent. This actually widens the trust gap — the new players it acquires first meet deposit / withdrawal friction and fraud concerns. Whoever clearly explains "how real-name verification passes, how to prove source of funds, how long payout takes" is best placed to capture the inflow the leader's acquisition generates. A simple "fast cash-out" claim no longer works.

Sources: Reddit r/CasualPH (recent evidence) + DigiPlus MABD +26% · GGRAsia
Q3
The unmet gaps

The four existing gaps are unchanged from last week and remain unclaimed: no one turns compliant ad-swap material into player-friendly, shareable content; no one publishes a deposit / alt-payment guide; no one computes the actual payout of promo terms; no one turns KYC / real-name onboarding into a player-readable guide. All are trust and explanation gaps, not budget gaps.

One more layer this week: the leader is occupied with acquisition and overseas expansion and several IRs with launching, so no one focuses on making "verifiable compliance, clear payout" plain. Whoever establishes trust first is best placed to capture the players spilling over from the leader's acquisition. See §06 Opportunities.

Sources: 9 brands with no new offers + DigiPlus acquisition + AML spillover to players
Q4
Pricing structure

DigiPlus runs event offers, the rest run rebates — unchanged, and quieter this week: all 9 scored operators posted no new offers. The 1.5% rebate remains an entry threshold rather than a selling point; OKBet, Bet88 and PlayTime share the same number and terms; CasinoPlus's 1.5%, expired 7/31, still has no successor, continuing the Color Game cumulative-jackpot narrative (W32 milestone, no new progress).

Promotional intensity has eased since W32 and bottoms this week: the World Cup ended (OKBet's FIFA pool and Bet88's World Cup pool expired, no successors), CasinoPlus's 1.5% expired with nothing following. Next week: watch CasinoPlus's renewal, whether independents add sports promotions, and whether DigiPlus's user rebound drives new promo intensity.

Sources: offers read as-is + World Cup 7/19 close + CasinoPlus 1.5% expiry
Q5
Differentiation

This week's new variable is the leader's strategic pivot: DigiPlus shifts its growth focus offshore (a New Zealand front after Brazil), effectively acknowledging that PH domestic growth room is approaching saturation. Its existing advantages — owned sports IP, a physical venue, cash and treasury-share reserves, and an upmarket VIP Club — remain hard to match short-term; but with some resources and management attention now offshore, domestic defensive intensity should ease in theory. At the same time, the W32 IR-entry pressure persists (Bloomberry, Okada, Newport, Hann, NUSTAR, owned or via PhilWeb), and the leader has not truly let go domestically — MABD +26% is evidence it keeps investing in domestic acquisition.

Conclusion: this week's change is at the leader level, not in pricing. Three facts read together — Q2 domestic revenue -37% YoY (structural decline confirmed), a QoQ user rebound (possible domestic bottoming, though single-quarter and uncertain), and the New Zealand license filing (growth momentum moving offshore). The leader simultaneously acknowledges domestic saturation and expands abroad while still investing in domestic acquisition to hold share; this "strategic contraction rather than withdrawal" leaves more room than when it defended domestic at full force.

Suggestion: competing with DigiPlus head-on on jackpots or acquisition budget is not advisable given its resource advantage. We suggest using the window — the leader distracted offshore, new players meeting deposit / withdrawal friction, several IRs busy launching — to enter the unclaimed positioning: explain real-name verification, deposits/withdrawals and payout timing clearly, and focus on capturing the inflow spilling over from the leader's acquisition. Policy remains unsettled; keep compliance on track and do not bet on a policy reversal. A recommendation, not a conclusion.

Sources: window synthesis · DigiPlus Q2 + NZ license + pricing slowdown
