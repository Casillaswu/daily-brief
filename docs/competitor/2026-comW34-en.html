▲Executive Briefing

This Week

Land-based casinos stopped falling while online dropped another 37.2%; the split inside the industry is now confirmed;
Two integrated-resort filings show online is already real revenue, and Solaire Online lands within weeks;
PAGCOR certified 16 Casino Filipino sites to ISO 9001:2015, an asset tidy-up ahead of the sale.

1. PAGCOR released Q2 industry data: land-based turned positive, online fell another 37.2%.
On 10 August PAGCOR (the Philippine gaming regulator) reported Q2 industry gross gaming revenue of ₱88.13 billion, down 20.3% year on year. Two segments inside that number moved in opposite directions: licensed land-based casinos took ₱45.4 billion, up 2.9% YoY and 1.9% QoQ, or 51.5% of the industry; electronic gaming (e-Games / e-Bingo / bingo / poker) took ₱39.85 billion, down 37.2%, or 45.2%. Chairman and CEO Alejandro Tengco attributed the overall decline to inflation and Middle East tensions weighing on discretionary spending. This is the first quarter since the regulatory tightening in which online clearly trails land-based in a like-for-like comparison. See §04 Card 1. (IAG 8/10 · BusinessWorld 8/10)

2. Two integrated-resort filings confirm online is already a real revenue source; Solaire Online lands within weeks.
Travellers, parent of Newport World Resorts, reported on 14 August first-half attributable net income of ₱568 million, more than double a year earlier, with non-VIP gross gaming revenue up 6% to ₱9.6 billion, which the company attributed to "the growing contribution of its digital channels". Bloomberry, parent of Solaire, reported on 15 August that its Q2 loss narrowed to ₱345.3 million from ₱1.4 billion, with consolidated GGR up 15% to ₱16.4 billion, and said its own platform FUNaloMax is now commercially live with Solaire Online joining the same platform "in the coming weeks". The brokerage call flagged in W32 — that integrated resorts moving online would squeeze the leader's margin — became evidence in the operators' own filings this week. See §04 Card 2. (IAG 8/14 · IAG 8/15)

3. PAGCOR certified 16 Casino Filipino sites to ISO 9001:2015, an asset tidy-up ahead of the sale.
On 15 August PAGCOR announced that 16 Casino Filipino sites and branches had obtained ISO 9001:2015 quality-management certification, valid to 22 March 2029, received by Tengco in person. It lands at the point where the GCG recommendation on "decoupling" is due at the Palace within August and Casino Filipino is slated for sale at ₱30-50 billion. See §04 Card 3. (IAG 8/15)

MarketQ2 splits "the market is shrinking" into two separate facts: land-based has stopped falling, online has not. The online segment alone fell 37.2%, 17 points worse than the industry's -20.3%. Read: the earlier first-year haircut of -15~25%, set on an industry-wide basis, understates the pressure on a pure-online business and should be recut on the segment basis.
CompetitionIntegrated-resort online is no longer a forecast. Newport has written digital channels into its earnings attribution and Bloomberry has a launch window for Solaire Online. Read: the number of competitors in local online will rise materially over the next quarter, and the newcomers arrive with land-based customer bases and existing brand trust, a different acquisition path from pure-online operators.
PolicyThe ISO certification and the decoupling timetable line up. Read: if the Casino Filipino sale advances, PAGCOR becomes a pure regulator sooner, and regulatory intensity typically rises rather than falls at that point. A week without new rules should not be read as easing.

§02Strategic Read · 5Q

Strategic Read · 5Q (neutral)

Q1
Market space

Q2 splits the previous single conclusion in two. Industry-wide GGR was ₱88.13 billion, down 20.3%; but licensed land-based casinos took ₱45.4 billion, up 2.9% YoY and 1.9% QoQ, with the four Entertainment City properties (City of Dreams Manila, Newport, Okada Manila, Solaire) up 2.7%. Over the same period electronic gaming took ₱39.85 billion, down 37.2%.

Read: looking at online and land-based together understates the online decline and overstates the risk to the market as a whole. The online segment fell 37.2% in one quarter, 17 points worse than the industry. The first-year haircut of -15~25%, set on an industry basis, is optimistic when applied to a pure-online business and should be recut on the segment basis. The DigiPlus user recovery noted in W33 (MABD +26% QoQ) reads, alongside this week's segment-level -37.2%, as one operator's acquisition result rather than a segment rebound.

Sources: PAGCOR 2Q26 industry data · IAG 8/10 · GMA News
Q2
What players care about

Player-side pain points are unchanged from recent weeks (no live capture this period; carried over from recent evidence): withdrawals stall on e-wallet identity verification; deposits route through prepaid vouchers, messaging apps and crypto apps, which is cumbersome and exposed to impersonation scams. Community sentiment runs to regret, quitting and scam-avoidance.

One new layer this week: land-based footfall recovered while online kept falling, at the same time. Read: this is not only trading down. Some players may have moved back to venues where cash goes in and out directly after hitting friction online. If so, the players lost from online are not the weakest spenders but the ones able to travel to a venue, which matters more to pure-online operators than the revenue line alone suggests. This is an inference; it needs venue footfall against online actives to verify, and is on next issue's watchlist.

Sources: recent community evidence + land-based +2.9% vs online -37.2% · IAG 8/10
Q3
Unmet gaps

Four existing gaps are unchanged and still unworked: compliance-driven creative refreshes have not been turned into content players understand and share; deposits and alternative payment routes have no usage guide; nobody has worked out what promotional terms actually pay out; identity verification has not been written up in plain language.

A fifth is added this week. Integrated resorts arrive online carrying a land-based brand, and their credibility rests on a venue you can walk into. Pure-online operators have no such card; the substitute available to them is a checkable licence and a verifiable withdrawal record. Nobody is explaining that clearly. Read: the window on this gap equals the time Solaire Online and the other integrated-resort platforms need to finish launching, roughly one quarter.

Sources: zero new offers across the nine this window + integrated-resort online evidence
Q4
Offer structure

DigiPlus runs event-style offers and the rest run rebates; the pattern holds. All nine published no new offer this week, the third straight quiet week: sports offers expired when the World Cup ended on 7/19 with no successor, and the CasinoPlus 1.5% rebate that expired on 7/31 still has none. The 1.5% rebate remains a cost of entry rather than a selling point, with OKBet, Bet88 and PlayTime on identical figures and terms.

Read: three weeks with nobody raising the stakes, against a segment down 37.2% YoY, points to acquisition cost and payback being out of proportion rather than a simple calendar gap. If so, the next raise waits for a clear sporting or seasonal hook. Watch next week for anyone pre-positioning sports offers ahead of the September season start.

Sources: offers carried over from W33 baseline (no on-site re-verification this period) + World Cup ended 7/19
Q5
Differentiation

This week's new variable is not among the nine operators but in the composition of the competitor set. Newport posted first-half attributable net income of ₱568 million, more than double a year earlier, with non-VIP GGR up 6% to ₱9.6 billion attributed to "the growing contribution of its digital channels"; Bloomberry narrowed its Q2 loss to ₱345.3 million from ₱1.4 billion, has FUNaloMax commercially live and Solaire Online joining the same platform within weeks. Those two sets of numbers turn the W32 brokerage call into something that has already happened.

Conclusion: the local online market is shrinking and getting more crowded at the same time. The segment is down 37.2% while the number of participants rises, and the newcomers arrive with land-based customer bases, existing brands and verifiable operating records. For a pure-online entrant, competing with integrated resorts on brand credibility is not realistic, and competing with DigiPlus on jackpot size is not realistic either.

Proposal: put the differentiation on the one thing neither the integrated resorts nor the leader will spend time on — writing licence verification, identity checks, money in and out, and settlement times into something a player can read and check for themselves. Integrated resorts solve trust with a building; a pure-online operator can only solve it with information that can be verified. This has to register before Solaire Online and the rest finish launching, roughly a one-quarter window. This is a proposal, not a conclusion.

Sources: Newport 1H26 · IAG 8/14 · Bloomberry 2Q26 · IAG 8/15

Q2 industry data: land-based +2.9% YoY, online segment -37.2% YoY

On 10 August PAGCOR reported Q2 industry gross gaming revenue of ₱88.13 billion (about US$1.45 billion), down 20.3% from ₱110.63 billion a year earlier. Two segments moved in opposite directions: licensed land-based casinos took ₱45.4 billion (about US$474 million), up 2.9% YoY and 1.9% QoQ, or 51.5% of the industry, with the four Entertainment City properties (City of Dreams Manila, Newport World Resorts, Okada Manila, Solaire Resort) up 2.7% YoY; electronic gaming (e-Games / e-Bingo / bingo / poker) took ₱39.85 billion (about US$675 million), 45.2% of the total, down 37.2% from ₱63.4 billion. PAGCOR-operated casinos took ₱2.90 billion, or 3.3%. Tengco attributed the overall decline to "inflation and the geopolitical crisis in the Middle East, which weighed on consumer spending, particularly on discretionary activities".
Read: the value in this release is the split, not the total. Land-based has turned positive while online is still accelerating downwards, 17 points apart, in the same consumer environment. That weakens "inflation suppressing spending" as the main explanation for the online decline and points to segment-specific friction (e-wallet delinking, tighter identity verification, advertising rework). For operators: the first-year haircut set on an industry-wide basis (around -20%) is optimistic, and a pure-online business should recut acquisition and revenue assumptions on the -37% segment basis. Separately, the land-based recovery says players are still spending and the channel has changed; part of the online loss is blocked access rather than lost demand, and that determines whether creative should explain how to get money in and out or why the product is worth playing.

The practical significance is timing: if the sale advances, PAGCOR becomes a pure regulator sooner, and a regulator that has lost its operating income typically closes the budget gap with higher licence fees, tighter compliance requirements and more active inspection. A week without new rules should not be read as regulatory easing.

§06Implications · Threats / Opportunities

Implications (Threats / Opportunities)

Entry parameters (all internal estimates)
  • Online segment basis, rewritten: Electronic gaming fell 37.2% YoY in Q2 against -20.3% for the industry, a 17-point gap. A pure-online first-year model should be recut on the segment basis; the earlier -15~25% haircut was set on the industry basis and is optimistic for online. This is the same basis as §04 Card 1 and is not repeated elsewhere.
  • Land-based stopped falling, online did not: Licensed land-based casinos +2.9% YoY, four Entertainment City properties +2.7%. Player demand is intact; online loss is more likely blocked access than lost demand.
  • Integrated-resort online is now proven: Newport counts digital channels in its earnings attribution; Bloomberry's Solaire Online lands within weeks. Competitor count in local online rises materially next quarter.
  • PAGCOR may become a pure regulator sooner: The ISO certification lines up with the decoupling timetable; a regulator that loses operating income typically raises licence fees and compliance requirements.
  • AML compliance: Tighter review of customer due diligence and suspicious-transaction wording is the 6-12 month direction; add a legal review line to payment and identity copy budgets.
  • Monthly guaranteed fee (MGF): At current monthly GGR below ₱30M the monthly shortfall is ₱9M; it rises to ₱10.5M per month from 10/1. Model from 10/1 onward.
  • B2B accreditation moved to 9/30 (PAGCOR 7/20 memo, originally 7/31): Non-compliant providers go dark from 10/1; direction unchanged, get it done.
Threats
  • Online fell 37.2% in one quarter, far worse than the industry.The split against land-based +2.9% points to segment-specific friction (e-wallet delinking, tighter identity verification, advertising rework). Cost of inaction (internal estimate): Setting first-year targets on the industry basis (-20%) overstates online revenue by roughly 17 points and distorts the payback assessment.
  • Integrated resorts bring land-based brands online; the credibility gap cannot be closed with promotions.Newport's digital channels already earn revenue and Solaire Online lands within weeks. Cost of inaction (internal estimate): Building trust-oriented creative only after they have launched costs 2-3x more to persuade, and a pure-online operator will then lack a comparable credibility proof.
  • The online increment is transferred share, not market growth.Integrated-resort online growth happened while the segment fell 37.2%, meaning share moves out of incumbent online operators. Cost of inaction (internal estimate): Budgeting acquisition on a market-expansion assumption against what is actually a zero-sum fight can put CPA 20-40% above estimate.
  • Regulatory intensity may rise as PAGCOR transitions.Decoupling is advancing and Casino Filipino is being prepared for sale; a regulator without operating income closes the gap with fees and inspection. Cost of inaction (internal estimate): Reading a week without new rules as easing and scaling spend leaves both media and creative stranded when the next tightening forces takedowns.
  • Three straight weeks with nobody raising offers makes rivals' intent hard to read.All nine are quiet, which does not distinguish strategic retreat from waiting for a hook. Cost of inaction (internal estimate): Raising alone during a collective silence means carrying the inefficient acquisition of the gap alone; doing nothing forfeits the pre-September positioning window.
