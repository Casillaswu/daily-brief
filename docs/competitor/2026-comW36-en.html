1. PAGCOR fined CasinoPlus ₱1m because the raffle went unfiled.
PAGCOR chairman and CEO Alejandro Tengco told the House Committee on Appropriations on 24 August, during the agency's 2027 budget hearing, that a licensed online gambling company had been fined ₱1 million for sponsoring an influencer giveaway of 100 handsets without clearing the promotion first. The fine has been paid, the promotion was ordered stopped, and a repeat offence could mean suspension or licence cancellation. Tengco did not name the company at the hearing. Several outlets pointed to CasinoPlus through the endorsement relationship: the platform signed Ivana Alawi (Filipino actress and content creator, more than 50 million followers across platforms) in May as its "responsible gaming" brand advocate, and her July giveaway of 100 iPhone 17 units — media put the handsets above ₱4.28 million — required entrants to follow a gambling platform's page. At the same hearing, Representative Elijah San Fernando (Kamanggagawa party-list) called for influencers to carry the same liability as operators. See §04 card 1. (GMA News 8/25 · Inquirer 8/24)

2. PAGCOR ships a checker app because 50% of sites are unlicensed.
At the same hearing Tengco said PAGCOR will release an official app before the end of 2026 letting players check whether a site is licensed, with a formal dispute mechanism for licensed operators built in. He put the share of unlicensed online gambling sites accessed in the Philippines at roughly 50%. The app builds on the PAGCOR Guarantee verification page launched in 2025, and the agency is working with the DICT (Department of Information and Communications Technology), the NTC (National Telecommunications Commission) and the CICC (Cybercrime Investigation and Coordination Center) to trace unlicensed operators and their payment rails. See §04 card 2. (IAG 8/25 · BusinessWorld 8/25)

3. PAGCOR cut licensees from 74 to 48 pending consolidation.
Speaking on the sidelines of the appropriations hearing on 26 August, Tengco said he sees no need to lift the freeze on new online gambling licences imposed in March 2024. The licensee count is down from 74 to 48, and PAGCOR wants further consolidation before considering new issuance. Fee rates hold at 30% for e-games and 25% for integrated resorts. The agency also guided 2026 total income down about 18% year on year, with net income falling from ₱17.47 billion in 2025 to ₱1.66 billion. See §04 card 3. (Philstar 8/27 · GGRAsia)

§02Strategic Read · 5Q

Strategic read · five questions (neutral)

Q1
Market space

The supply side got closed off in two directions this week. First, the count: the freeze on new online gambling licences has run since March 2024, Tengco said on 26 August it stays, and the licensee count is down from 74 to 48, with PAGCOR waiting on further consolidation before it revisits issuance. Second, the rates: 30% for e-games, 25% for integrated resorts, unchanged. Demand was marked down officially in the same week, with PAGCOR guiding 2026 total income down about 18% and net income from ₱17.47 billion to ₱1.66 billion, largely on last year's central bank order unlinking e-wallets from gambling platforms, which Tengco said cut activity by around 40% at one point.

Read: a cap on the count, unchanged rates and a marked-down forecast add up to a filter. PAGCOR did not cut rates to prop up the market and is not issuing licences to fill the gap, which tells the 48 incumbents to hold on or leave. For anyone entering, the only workable route this year is acquisition, not application.

Sources: Licence freeze and rates · Philstar 8/27 · 2026 forecast · GGRAsia
Q2
What players care about

Withdrawals stuck at e-wallet identity checks, deposits moving to vouchers and messaging apps, and community sentiment running to scam avoidance and quitting: all three are unchanged from recent weeks and are not restated in detail here, with no live capture available this period.

One layer is new. Tengco said on the record that roughly 50% of the online gambling sites accessed in the Philippines are unlicensed, and the app PAGCOR is building exists so players can check for themselves. Read: that turns "is the site I play on legal" from vague anxiety into a checkable question. If an operator's answer is still one line of licence text in the page footer, then once the app ships, players will treat the app's result as the answer, with the site's own claim a distant second.

Sources: Roughly 50% unlicensed · iGaming Business
Q3
Unmet gaps

Plain-language compliance creative, a usable guide to alternative payment rails, a working calculation of what promotional terms pay, and identity verification written out for players: these four gaps are unchanged from recent weeks and still unworked.

A fifth gap appeared this week, tied directly to the verification app. None of the nine has turned "how to check that we are licensed" into something a player can follow; licence information mostly sits as a footer line. Once the app ships, the first brand to do this properly effectively gets a free slot with official backing behind it.

One more constraint has been in force since June and has not been unpacked here before, and it limits the creative itself: ASC (Ad Standards Council) Circular 2026-008, effective 24 June 2026, bars advertising material in any medium (broadcast, digital, print, out-of-home) from showing or mentioning casino games and equipment, including roulette, baccarat, blackjack, slot machines, cards, chips and dice; breaches draw a cease-and-desist order copied to PAGCOR. Read: this limits the visuals, not the copy, and it hits the creative pipeline. Copy and media budget stay outside its scope. Offer figures can still be stated; tables and chips cannot appear, which leaves fewer usable visual elements than most people assume.

Sources: ASC Circular 2026-008 · MARKETECH APAC
Q4
Offer structure

The split between the two offer types held this week, with DigiPlus on event-led offers and everyone else on rebates. Five straight weeks with no new tiers among the nine: the sports offer expired when the World Cup ended on 7/19 and nobody picked it up, and CasinoPlus's 1.5% expired on 7/31 with no successor announced. The 1.5% rebate is table stakes by now and carries no selling power, and OKBet, Bet88 and PlayTime carry identical figures and terms.

The only substantive change on the offer side came from the fine. The CasinoPlus 100-handset raffle was ordered stopped, and follow-the-page giveaways of exactly that kind have been standard practice across the nine for two years. Read: the cost structure of giveaway offers gained a line item this week, which is the lead time for the filing. What used to run from concept to live inside a week now has to fit a PAGCOR filing in front of it, so slots move earlier. Brands that cannot absorb that will run fewer giveaways and fall back on plain rebates, which makes an already homogeneous offer set more homogeneous.

Sources: Promotion ordered stopped · Inquirer 8/24
Q5
Differentiation

Three threads this week point at one structure: enforcement is landing ahead of legislation, the regulator is stepping directly into how players choose a site, and the only entry route left is acquisition. The first fine over an endorsement-linked promotion has been issued and paid (GMA 8/25, Inquirer 8/24); the official verification app ships this year and the chairman puts roughly 50% of sites outside the licensing regime (IAG 8/25, BusinessWorld 8/25); licensees are down from 74 to 48, the freeze holds and rates are unchanged (Philstar 8/27, Focus Gaming News).

Conclusion: the basis of competition in the Philippines is shifting from who can buy attention to who holds up under scrutiny. Read separately these are three regulatory stories; stacked, they are one thing — compliance status itself is becoming public information that players, media and prosecutors can check directly, no longer a document an operator keeps internally.

Proposal: treat compliance assets as marketing assets. Three concrete items. First, reconcile brand name, domains and registered entity against PAGCOR's records character by character, so the listing is findable and correctly spelled when the app ships. Second, make the filing receipt a precondition for putting any giveaway, raffle or follow-to-enter mechanic live, and keep it. Third, build a plain-language "how to verify our licence" page for the site and the app. None of the three falls inside SB 2347's prohibitions. Proposal, not conclusion.

Sources: GMA 8/25 · IAG 8/25 · Focus Gaming News

Card 1 · PAGCOR / enforcement8/24-8/27
High

PAGCOR stopped a 100-phone CasinoPlus raffle over no filing.

Tengco told the House Committee on Appropriations on 24 August, during deliberations on PAGCOR's 2027 budget, that the agency had fined a licensed online gambling company ₱1 million for sponsoring an influencer giveaway of 100 handsets without filing the promotion with PAGCOR. He said the fine has been paid and the promotion was ordered stopped, with a stern warning attached: a repeat draws a heavier fine, and a further violation could lead to suspension and ultimately cancellation of the licence. He did not name the company at the hearing.
The case goes back to a contested July giveaway. Ivana Alawi (Filipino actress and content creator; more than 45 million followers on Facebook, 23.4 million on TikTok, 20.6 million YouTube subscribers) posted a giveaway of 100 iPhone 17 units, valued by media above ₱4.28 million, with entry conditions that included sharing the video, commenting and following a gambling platform's page. Several Philippine outlets identified the fined party as CasinoPlus through the endorsement relationship: the platform signed Alawi in May 2026 as its "responsible gaming" brand advocate. She was previously Bet88's endorser from 2022 until early 2026.
At the same hearing, Representative San Fernando singled out the requirement to like the page and asked PAGCOR to hold influencers equally liable with operators for promotional content that breaches advertising rules. Two related signals landed in the same window: on 26 August, on the sidelines of the appropriations committee, Tengco disclosed that the CICC has 12 to 15 cases open against gambling endorsers and influencers, naming the individuals, with platforms off the charge sheet; and the CICP (Creator and Influencer Council of the Philippines), an industry self-regulatory body, went on record about the giveaway, saying gambling-related endorsements deserve a heightened degree of caution and responsibility, and noting that the trust creators build can normalise gambling before viewers have had a chance to work out whether a platform is licensed.
Read: the weight of this card sits in three things being true at once. ₱1 million is a small sum for a licensee. First, enforcement is faster than legislation — SB 2347 is still a filed bill, and PAGCOR has already fined and collected using an existing unregistered-promotion clause. Second, the penalised activity was run by a "responsible gaming" ambassador, which means a compliance narrative is not itself a defence. Third, the CICC cases name the individual, which moves the agency-side risk from "my client got fined and I may not get paid" to "I am the defendant". Three practical consequences: make the PAGCOR filing receipt a precondition for putting any raffle, giveaway or follow-to-enter mechanic live, and keep it; write individual legal liability into influencer contracts as a clause, with nothing left verbal; and price the filing lead time into the schedule, because the one-week concept-to-live cycle no longer works from this quarter.
⚠️ On identifying the fined party: Tengco did not name the company at the hearing (GMA 8/25, read directly). The CasinoPlus attribution comes from media identifying it through Ivana Alawi's endorsement relationship, including GMA's own article URL and the CICP interview naming Casino Plus as the platform the post drove to (MARKETECH APAC). PAGCOR has not published a named sanction notice, so this card says "identified by media" and draws no conclusion of its own.
💬 Social signal: the giveaway post is itself the social-side event (a Facebook video post with share, comment and follow-to-enter conditions) and it drew a public CICP response; player-side discussion could not be captured live this period and remains unquantified.

Card 2 · PAGCOR / regulation8/24-8/25
High

PAGCOR and 3 agencies trace payments, 50% of sites unlicensed.

At the same budget hearing on 24 August, Tengco said PAGCOR will release an official app before the end of 2026 that lets players check whether an online gambling site is licensed. He put the share of unlicensed online gambling sites accessed in the Philippines at roughly 50%. The app builds on the PAGCOR Guarantee verification page launched in 2025 and, beyond licence lookups, will carry a formal dispute-resolution mechanism for dealing with licensed operators.
The approach is interagency. PAGCOR is working with the DICT (Department of Information and Communications Technology), the NTC (National Telecommunications Commission) and the CICC, and beyond identifying unlicensed operators the effort targets the financial mechanisms behind illegal gambling, particularly the payment channels unauthorised platforms use.
Read: PAGCOR is shifting its method towards routing players before they deposit. An ad ban governs what an operator may say; a verification app governs what a player sees, and the second has the more direct effect. One extra check before a player deposits structurally trims traffic to anything outside the whitelist, and none of it requires a bill to pass. Net positive for compliant operators, provided the listing is findable. The concrete risk is registration consistency: if brand name, domains and registered entity are spelled inconsistently in PAGCOR's source data, or subdomains are not covered, a player who checks and gets "not found" has, in practice, been shown a site flagged as illegal. PAGCOR has not published the app's whitelist source or its registration and appeal mechanism, which is the single most important thing to chase this period.
⚠️ Single-source note: industry coverage is multi-source (IAG 8/25, BusinessWorld 8/25, iGaming Business, Focus Gaming News, Marketing-Interactive). The launch date, data source and appeal mechanism have not been set out in a formal PAGCOR notice; "this year" is Tengco speaking, not a written timetable. Player-side reaction could not be captured live this period.
Sources: PAGCOR to launch new app allowing players to identify licensed and unlicensed online gaming sites · IAG 8/25 · PAGCOR developing app to curb access to unregistered platforms · BusinessWorld 8/25 · Pagcor launching app to help identify licensed online casinos · iGaming Business · PAGCOR says new app will help players identify legal platforms · Focus Gaming News · PAGCOR to launch app to steer players towards licensed platforms · Marketing-Interactive
Proposal (for Client X): before entry, walk the PAGCOR listing as a technical check — main domain, every subdomain and app name, registered entity name. If the entry route is acquiring a licensee, put the target's listing status and spelling consistency into due diligence: the cost is near zero and the cost of missing it is being checked as "illegal" after launch. Separately, build the "how to verify our licence" page for the site and the app now, so it can point at the official app when that ships.

Card 3 · PAGCOR / structure8/24-8/27
High

PAGCOR guides 2026 income down 18% on the e-wallet unlinking.

Speaking on the sidelines of the House appropriations committee on 26 August, Tengco said he sees no need to lift the freeze on new online gambling licences. The freeze has run since a board resolution in March 2024 and bars PAGCOR from accepting new applications. The current count is 48, down from a peak of 74. He said the regulator wants further consolidation before revisiting issuance, and that PAGCOR has identified licensees that are not actually operating and are holding approvals to sell, and is moving to clear them out. The fee structure holds: 30% of gross gaming revenue for e-games operators, 25% for integrated resorts.
The demand-side official numbers landed in the same week. PAGCOR guided 2026 total income down about 18%, with two figures on record: GGRAsia reports nearly ₱86.95 billion (about US$1.41 billion) and GMA reports ₱86.498 billion, both against ₱106.031 billion in 2025. Net income is guided from ₱17.47 billion in 2025 to ₱1.66 billion, which Philstar headlined as a 91% drop. Tengco attributed the weakness mainly to last year's central bank order unlinking e-wallets from online gambling platforms, saying activity fell around 40% at one point, and also cited Middle East conflict squeezing lower-income spending. He added that online activity recovered about 10% in July and held at that level in August, and pointed to the coming peak season. PAGCOR also makes monthly payments against arrears owed to the Philippine Sports Commission. At the same hearing Tengco put 2026 industry-wide GGR at around ₱370 billion, below the ₱396.14 billion recorded in 2025 (Philstar 8/27).
Read: these three come from the same set of budget arithmetic. Revenue comes from the existing 48, the gap is not filled by new licences, and consolidation is handled by the minimum guaranteed fee and by compliance attrition. For anyone entering, that is direct: the application route stays closed this year, acquisition is what is left, and pricing an acquisition now turns on compliance history, unpaid fees and listing status as much as on revenue. For anyone selling marketing services, a cap of 48 means the client universe will not grow and can only shrink, so the emphasis should shift from new business to going deeper with existing accounts; at the same time, the share released by exits gets absorbed by the larger operators that remain, so media and influencer rates may rise in the second half. The 10% July recovery is Tengco speaking with no third-party data behind it, so it should not be treated as an established recovery when planning Q4.

Both trace to the same hearing briefing, and the gap may be rounding or a range. Cite the source and do not merge them into one number. "About 10% recovery" is a verbal statement with no published dataset behind it.

Card 4 · DigiPlus / product8/23-8/25
Medium

DigiPlus added 100 free games, mainly to reach a wider audience.

DigiPlus Interactive (parent of BingoPlus, ArenaPlus and GameZone) launched ArenaSocial Arcade in late August, a new game suite inside its free-to-play social platform ArenaSocial, adding more than 100 free games spanning skill-based challenges, reflex titles, strategy games and high-score competitions, built for short, low-friction sessions. ArenaSocial itself launched earlier in 2026 as a free-to-play, non-wagering environment built around sports and esports challenges, casual play, community participation and rewards. DigiPlus president Ping Chen said the move reflects the company's intent to reach broader audiences and create more ways for people to engage with its digital ecosystem.
Read: the product itself does not change the competitive map; the regulatory positioning is what to follow. With the ad ban bill unresolved and endorsement enforcement already issuing fines, a free, non-wagering category gives DigiPlus a brand-exposure channel outside gambling advertising rules while keeping the traffic pool in-house, which is a direct answer to the roughly 40% drop in funnel conversion after the e-wallet unlinking. What to watch over the next two weeks is how the creative runs: which platforms it goes on, and whether it carries 21+ or responsible-gaming markings. If it can run at volume on mainstream platforms without those markings, the category is in practice being treated as non-gambling content, and that is a route an independent operator could copy.

§06Implications · threats / opportunities

Implications (threats / opportunities)

Entry parameters (all internal estimates)
  • Filing lead time for endorsement and giveaway slots: an unregistered raffle has now produced a ₱1m fine, a stop order and a suspension warning. Any slot with a raffle, giveaway or follow-to-enter mechanic needs PAGCOR filing built into the plan; allow two to three weeks per slot. Shares a source with §04 card 1; not repeated elsewhere.
  • Personal legal exposure for endorsers: the CICC has 12-15 cases open against gambling endorsers and influencers, naming the individuals, with platforms off the charge sheet. Influencer contracts must set out personal liability. The list is not public, so use "has previously promoted unlicensed platforms" as a proxy when picking talent.
  • Listing consistency: the official verification app ships this year. Brand name, domains, subdomains and registered entity must be reconciled character by character against PAGCOR's records; in practice, an inconsistency means players see the site flagged as illegal.
  • Entry limited to acquisition: the freeze on new online gambling licences has run since March 2024 and Tengco said on 26 August it stays, with 48 licensees capped. The application route does not open this year.
  • Licence fixed costs rising: the e-casino class MGF is currently ₱9m a month (against a ₱30m revenue threshold), rising to ₱10.5m a month (against ₱35m) on 2027/01/01. Model on the 2027 tier.
  • Creative-level visual limits: ASC Circular 2026-008, in force since 2026/06/24, bars advertising material from showing or mentioning roulette, baccarat, blackjack, slot machines, cards, chips, dice and other casino games and equipment. Re-audit the usable visual elements accordingly.
  • Demand-side base: PAGCOR guides 2026 total income down about 18% and net income down about 91%. Build revenue assumptions on the official 2Q26 actual (industry-wide GGR ₱88.13bn, down 20.3%) rather than Tengco's verbal 10% July recovery.
Threats
  • Enforcement on endorsement-linked promotions has landed, and the first fine hit a watchlist operator. An unregistered raffle is itself grounds for sanction, and a repeat can mean suspension or cancellation. Cost of inaction (internal estimate): if existing slots go live without back-filing, the sanction itself is small but a stop order means the slot breaks mid-flight, with an estimated 40-70% of the slot's creative and media spend unrecoverable.
  • The CICC cases name the individual endorsers, and influencer rates face a wholesale reprice. Twelve to 15 cases, list not public. Cost of inaction (internal estimate): if the first referral to prosecutors happens, top-tier endorser rates for gambling work are estimated to rise 30-60%, or the talent exits the category outright; finding replacements then costs an estimated four to eight weeks.
  • The verification app turns listing errors into something players can see. If brand name, domains and registered entity do not line up, players get "not found". Cost of inaction (internal estimate): a single mis-flagging is estimated to cost 20-40% of organic-traffic registration conversion for the period, and the cost of repairing trust far exceeds the near-zero cost of correcting the listing.
  • The licence freeze leaves acquisition as the only entry, making compliance history a pricing variable. Forty-eight capped, and PAGCOR is clearing out licensees that are not actually operating. Cost of inaction (internal estimate): if due diligence misses unpaid fees and prior sanctions, back-payment and remediation after the deal are estimated at 10-25% of the purchase price, and may trigger a licence renewal review.
  • The same ad ban lands unevenly on pure online operators versus groups with physical assets. DigiPlus is consolidating a physical resort and has opened a free non-wagering category, and neither channel sits inside the online gambling advertising prohibitions. Cost of inaction (internal estimate): if the competitive assumption is still "everyone gets banned together", the actual outcome is an estimated 1.5-2x larger drop in your own share of voice; directional estimate, to be validated after the bill passes.
Opportunities
  • Turn licence verification into a page players can follow (internal estimate ~₱150K-₱400K / 3-4 weeks) The official app ships this year, and licence information at the nine is mostly a footer line, with nobody treating it as readable content. Doing it first is effectively a free slot with official backing behind it. Note: do not imply PAGCOR endorses the product; state the verification method and the facts only.
  • Build the filing process into an SOP and a slot template (internal estimate ~₱100K-₱250K / one-off) The fine has proved this is a real operational gate, and most operators and agencies have not built it into their workflow. Templated, it becomes a service capability worth selling. Note: the template needs review by counsel familiar with PAGCOR promotion filing; do not self-assess what is exempt.
  • Feasibility study on the free non-wagering category (internal estimate ~₱100K-₱300K / one-off) DigiPlus is already generating evidence, and its media placement over the next two weeks shows the compliance treatment directly. Note: evaluate only until the position is clear, do not build; the routing design between the category and the main brand is the key variable.
  • Hold the September sports window with instructional content (internal estimate ~₱100K-₱300K / one-off) Rivals have had no sports offer since 7/19, seven weeks now, and instructional or match-analysis content touches neither promotional-reward exposure nor the casino equipment visuals barred by the ASC circular. Note: avoid "guaranteed" or "sure win" framing; legal review first.
