This week

HB 10982 would ban influencer ads over a 50.77% share.
DigiPlus takes 97% of GGR on a rented third-party licence.
PAGCOR has 8 days left on the 9/15 helpline ad swap.

1. HB 10982 would ban influencer ads over a 50.77% share.
Cebu Third District Rep. Karen Hope Garcia filed House Bill 10982, the Online Gambling Advertising Prohibition Act of 2026, on 26 August. It first surfaced in the press on 1 September and spread to national and trade outlets between 3 and 5 September. It is pending first reading. The ban covers TV, radio, print, billboards, social media, websites and mobile apps, and it names influencers, content creators, affiliate marketers, celebrities and vloggers as covered parties. This version also bans promotional mechanics themselves, including bonuses, free bets and referral codes, plus cross-promotion of land-based products carrying the same brand. Penalties: operators face PHP 500,000 to PHP 10 million, licence suspension or revocation, and up to three years in prison; public figures must return endorsement fees already received and are barred from gambling endorsements for three to five years; platforms, media firms, adtech companies and ISPs face PHP 50,000 per day. See §04 card 1.（Sunstar 9/1 · Manila Times 9/5）

2. DigiPlus takes 97% of GGR on a rented third-party licence.
Hong Kong-listed International Entertainment Corporation (IEC, owner of LaVie Resort & Casino Manila) told the exchange on 28 August, alongside its interim results, that subsidiary New Coast Leisure Inc (NCLI) now holds every PAGCOR licence it needs. The online platform it runs jointly with DigiPlus subsidiary Total Gamezone Xtreme Inc (TGXI) is slated for late August or September, opening with electronic casino games only and no live dealer. The revenue split, made public on 1 September, gives NCLI 3% and TGXI 97% of gross gaming revenue. With new online licences frozen and the licensee count down from 74 to 48, this is the first workable lateral expansion route this quarter. See §04 card 2.（IAG 8/30 · Manila Bulletin 9/1）

3. PAGCOR has 8 days left on the 9/15 helpline ad swap.
Dr Janice Saraza, officer-in-charge of PAGCOR's Health Services and Wellness Department, spoke on 4 September at HWONFEX 2026 on behalf of chairman and CEO Alejandro Tengco. She covered how regulated gaming revenue funds tourism, healthcare and education, and highlighted the 24/7 National Problem Gambling Helpline built with Seagulls Flock Organization. Two deadlines land in the same month: the retention period for responsible-gaming ads carrying the helpline expires 15 September, and the B2B supplier accreditation deadline falls 30 September, with unaccredited systems decommissioned from 1 October. See §04 card 3.（IAG 9/6 · GGRAsia）

EndorsementsBoth chambers now have an ad-ban bill running, and the House version names influencers and affiliates as covered parties, with clawback penalties attached. Read: endorsement contracts have changed character. The worst case used to be that the brand gets fined and you do not get paid. The worst case now is that the talent has to return fees already collected and cannot take similar work for three to five years. A clause saying the contract is unaffected by future regulation does not remove that. Any long-dated endorsement deal from Q4 onward needs its rate and its termination clause renegotiated together.
OffersThe House version bans bonuses, free bets and referral codes as well. Read: if this text passes as written, what is banned is not just advertising but the whole channel for talking about offers. Operators can still give the same value, they just have no way to tell players. That pushes up the value of the existing customer database, in-product messaging and the opt-in list, because those touchpoints sit outside the ban. Moving traffic into owned lists now is a cheap hedge.
Market structureDigiPlus opened a new online platform through a third-party licence holder and takes a 97% share. That is a new brand slot created without a new licence being issued. Read: the licence freeze binds less tightly than the announcement suggests. A party with capital and technology can rent a licence and go live, giving up a single-digit share. Once this is copied, the number of brands in the market will not converge with the number of licensees; a layer of white-label brands can sit under the 48.

§02Strategic Read · 5Q

Strategic read · five questions (neutral)

Q1
Market space

Supply gained a route this week that the existing structure did not account for. IEC subsidiary NCLI holds all the PAGCOR licences it needs and has handed online operation to DigiPlus subsidiary TGXI, keeping 3% and giving TGXI 97%. The freeze on new online licences since March 2024 and the drop from 74 to 48 licensees are both unchanged, but a licence holder can lease out the operating right.

On the demand side, two September deadlines are now inside two weeks: the retention period for responsible-gaming ads expires 15 September, and B2B supplier accreditation closes 30 September, with unaccredited systems shut off from 1 October.

Read: the freeze caps the number of licence holders, not the number of brands. A licensed shell can host a third-party operation, so the market can show more than 48 brands. For an entrant, that adds a much cheaper route than acquisition, namely finding a licensee that holds paper but has no operating capability. DigiPlus has now put a price on the table, and 3% is what a licence holder gets when the operator has the leverage.

Sources: 3%／97% split · Manila Bulletin 9/1 · B2B deadline 9/30 · GGRAsia
Q2
Player focus

Three familiar player-side problems showed no verifiable change this week: withdrawals stuck behind e-wallet identity checks, deposits routed through vouchers and messaging apps, and community talk dominated by scams and self-exclusion. On-site sampling was unavailable, so none of it is expanded here.

A new layer arrived from the legislature. The House bill cites PAGCOR's 2025 figures: electronic and online gaming GGR of PHP 201.12 billion, or 50.77% of the industry's PHP 396.14 billion, overtaking land-based casinos for the first time. The stated rationale is that advertising and influencer posts bring gambling into the home. Read: the argument has moved from "some people get addicted" to "this sector is now large enough to require control", and it runs on the regulator's own published numbers. Answering that with "we are licensed" misses the point, because the licensed half is exactly what is being questioned.

Sources: PHP 201.12bn at 50.77% · Manila Times 9/5
Q3
Unserved gaps

Five long-standing gaps again went untouched: plain-language compliance material, alternative payment guidance, offer-term calculators, identity-verification walkthroughs and licence-verification explainers.

A sixth arrives from how the House text treats promotional mechanics. Beyond advertising, it bans bonuses, free bets and referral codes. None of the nine currently treats non-promotional retention content as an asset; in-product messaging, email and opt-in lists are used almost entirely as delivery pipes for promo codes. Read: if the ban becomes law, the codes go and the pipes stay. Building content those touchpoints can actually carry is what leaves you able to speak on the day the ban takes effect.

One hard constraint sits at the visual layer. Since Ad Standards Council (ASC) circular 2026-008 took effect on 24 June 2026, advertising material in any medium may not show or mention roulette, baccarat, blackjack, slot machines, cards, chips or dice; ASC issues a cease-and-desist order and copies PAGCOR.

Sources: Ban on promotional mechanics · Focus Gaming News
Q4
Offer structure

Two offer types, unchanged: DigiPlus runs campaigns, everyone else runs rebates, and nobody switched this week. Six weeks in, not one of the nine has launched anything new; the sports slot that expired 7/19 and CasinoPlus's 1.5% that expired 7/31 both sit empty.

The reason 1.5% looks identical everywhere is that it is the ceiling. Under current PAGCOR rules, cash rebates measured on player turnover or deposit are capped at 1.50% for electronic games (excluding casino table and arcade-type games), and cashback measured on net losses is capped at 15%. Read: there is no differentiation left in the rebate line, because the ceiling is set by the regulator. What can still separate brands is how the cashback is calculated, how fast it lands, and whether the terms are written so a player can follow them. None of those is capped, and none falls inside the literal wording of the House ban.

Sources: 1.5% rebate / 15% cashback caps · GGRAsia
Q5
Differentiation

This week's three threads sit on different layers. Legislation produced a House ad-ban bill naming influencers and promotional mechanics (Sunstar 9/1, Manila Times 9/5). Structure produced a licensed shell leasing out its operating right at a 3% to 97% split (IAG 8/30, Manila Bulletin 9/1). Execution has two September deadlines inside two weeks (IAG 9/6, GGRAsia). The nine operators themselves did nothing.

Conclusion: none of the three lands on pricing. Offers already sit at a regulator-set ceiling and have not moved in six weeks. What decides the next year is whether you can keep talking to players once the advertising channels narrow, and what it costs to obtain a licence slot you can go live on.

Proposal: three things. First, inventory the owned touchpoints as assets, covering the opt-in list, in-product messaging, messaging-app communities and support conversations, confirm each remains usable after an ad ban, and start moving traffic into them. Second, add two clauses to every multi-year endorsement and affiliate contract: termination on regulatory change, and clawback liability assigned to the brand. Third, if the entry route is still open, add "negotiate an operating share with a licence holder" as the second option next to acquisition, using the 3% to 97% split as a reference point. These are recommendations, not conclusions.

Sources: Sunstar 9/1 · IAG 8/30 · IAG 9/6

Card 1 · House / Legislation8/26-9/05
High impact

HB 10982 sets fines to PHP 10M over ads in homes.

Cebu Third District Rep. Karen Hope Garcia filed House Bill 10982, the Online Gambling Advertising Prohibition Act of 2026, on 26 August. It is pending first reading. Her stated rationale is that online gambling reaches households through livestream advertising, social-media bonus codes and influencer promotion, touching children and financially vulnerable families; she describes smartphones as 24-hour casinos people carry with them. The bill cites PAGCOR's 2025 figures: electronic and online gaming GGR of PHP 201.12 billion, or 50.77% of the industry's PHP 396.14 billion, overtaking licensed land-based casinos as the largest contributor for the first time. It also cites the World Health Organization's December 2024 fact sheet, which classes gambling disorder as a health-harming addictive behaviour and recommends prohibiting gambling advertising, promotion and sponsorship.
The ban covers TV, radio, print, billboards, social media, websites and mobile apps, plus search advertising, targeted digital campaigns, affiliate marketing, and promotional communication by email and SMS. It is a separate bill from Senator Francis Escudero's SB 2347, filed in August, and differs in three places: it names influencers, content creators, affiliate marketers, celebrities and vloggers as covered parties; it bans promotional mechanics themselves, including bonuses, free bets and referral codes; and it bars cross-promotion of land-based products carrying the same brand, while requiring stronger age and identity verification on online gambling accounts.
Penalties run in three tiers. Operators: PHP 500,000 to PHP 10 million, licence suspension or revocation, and up to three years in prison. Public figures: return of endorsement fees already received, plus a three- to five-year bar on gambling endorsements. Social platforms, media companies, adtech firms and internet service providers: PHP 50,000 per day for non-compliance, with possible licence revocation. Exemptions cover advertising for licensed land-based casinos, PCSO lotteries and sweepstakes, horse racing and licensed cockpits, still subject to responsible-gaming advisories; news reporting, academic discussion and government communication are also outside the ban.
Read: the point is not that another ad-ban bill exists, it is three specific provisions. First, the clawback. Returning fees already collected plus a three- to five-year bar means a deal signed today has to be valued against future regulatory risk rather than against the campaign rate. In practice that turns a long-dated endorsement into a liability with a tail. Second, promotional mechanics are inside the ban. If bonuses, free bets and referral codes cannot be communicated, what is banned is the entire acquisition script, leaving only players already on the list. Third, intermediaries are liable. PHP 50,000 a day for platforms, media and adtech means the buy side will start self-censoring before the bill passes, so the real tightening arrives ahead of any effective date. Three practical consequences: add regulatory-change termination clauses to Q4-onward endorsement and affiliate contracts with clawback liability assigned to the brand; start moving advertising traffic into owned lists, which sit outside the ban; and track closely whether this bill is consolidated with SB 2347, because whose text survives decides whether promotional mechanics are banned at all.
⚠️ Timing basis: the filing date is 26 August (Manila Times 9/5 and alienwp on bill status), which falls in the previous data window; first public coverage was Sunstar on 1 September, spreading to trade and national outlets between 3 and 5 September. This card states filing 8/26 and reporting from 9/1 side by side and does not count the filing date as an in-window event. The bill is pending first reading, not yet referred to committee, with no hearing scheduled; the text can change in committee.
⚠️ Single-source note: Philippine mainstream and trade coverage is multi-source (Sunstar 9/1, Manila Times 9/5, Focus Gaming News, iGamingToday, Allin News 9/3); the full bill text is not published online, so all provision detail is second-hand and the original must be obtained before citing section numbers. Player-side and creator-side reaction could not be sampled this period.

Proposal (for Client X): build the endorsement and affiliate budget in two versions. In version one the bill passes as written, endorsements, affiliate marketing and promo-code communication are all unusable, and acquisition runs on owned lists and in-product touchpoints only; this version has to pull list-building cost forward into the first quarter after entry. In version two the consolidated text narrows to advertising only, leaving promotional mechanics usable while endorsements stay banned. The gap between the two versions is the price of this legislative risk, and it is worth computing now rather than waiting for a floor vote.

Card 2 · DigiPlus / IEC8/28-9/01
High impact

TGXI takes 97% of GGR on a rented NCLI licence.

IEC told the Hong Kong exchange on 28 August, alongside its interim results for FY2025/26, that wholly owned subsidiary New Coast Leisure Inc (NCLI) holds every PAGCOR licence it needs, and that the online platform it runs with DigiPlus's wholly owned subsidiary Total Gamezone Xtreme Inc (TGXI) is slated for late August or September 2026. The cooperation agreement was signed on 10-11 June and covers integration, aggregation, provision, technical support and operation of approved online games, subject to PAGCOR approval and applicable regulation. The product line as filed lists electronic casino games, electronic bingo, online poker and specialty games; the launch will carry electronic casino games only, with no live dealer. IEC also states the arrangement is entirely separate from LaVie's land-based casino business and does not currently involve taking integrated-resort games online.
The split was first disclosed in reporting on 1 September: on gross gaming revenue, NCLI takes 3% and TGXI takes 97%.
The operating figures in the same filing: group interim revenue HK$852.5 million, up 50.6%; gaming revenue HK$819.4 million, up 60.7%, driven mainly by the LaVie expansion; loss for the period HK$486.4 million (about US$62 million), driven by non-cash fair value movement on the HK$1.6 billion convertible notes subscribed by DigiPlus. Excluding that item, the loss narrowed 78.2% year on year and gross profit rose 66.4% to HK$454.6 million. The notes are unconverted; full conversion would give DigiPlus 53.89% of IEC.
Read: the value here is not the revenue contribution, it is that the deal has measured how tightly the licence freeze actually binds. PAGCOR has issued no new online licences since March 2024 and the count has fallen from 74 to 48, officially pending industry consolidation. But a licence holder can outsource operation to a third party and collect a share, so a new platform can appear while the licensee count stays flat. The 3% to 97% ratio is worth remembering: the licence holder takes 3%, which says that under current supply and demand the paper itself has far less bargaining power than operating capability and traffic. For anyone selling marketing services, this means the new clients that appear over the next year will not come only from the existing 48 but from white-label platforms of this kind, and those will subsidise hard to buy volume at launch, pushing Q4 media costs up. What to watch is when the brand name and domain surface, and how large the welcome offer is, because that number will set the floor price for the quarter.

The formal interim loss is HK$486.4 million (about US$62 million), which differs from the "could widen to about US$64 million" estimate in the 21 August profit warning; the formal figure governs. The 53.89% is a post-conversion estimate and conversion has not occurred. The 3% to 97% ratio applies to gross gaming revenue and is not a net profit split.

Card 3 · PAGCOR / Deadlines9/04-9/06
Mid impact

PAGCOR has 23 days left on B2B accreditation.

Dr Janice Saraza, officer-in-charge of PAGCOR's Health Services and Wellness Department, spoke on 4 September at the opening of HWONFEX 2026 on behalf of chairman and CEO Alejandro Tengco. The speech had two parts: how regulated gaming revenue funds tourism, healthcare, education and social development, including partnerships with licensed integrated resorts to build hospitals and supply medical equipment; and mental and emotional wellbeing, centred on the 24/7 National Problem Gambling Helpline set up with Seagulls Flock Organization, which provides confidential counselling and professional referrals. IAG relayed PAGCOR's release on 6 September.
Two deadlines moved inside two weeks in the same period. First, PAGCOR's June memorandum required all licensees to replace responsible-gaming advertising with the 24/7 helpline version by 15 July and keep it in place until 15 September. Second, the B2B supplier accreditation deadline, pushed from 31 July to 30 September, means gaming systems, platforms, software and related equipment from unaccredited suppliers stop operating from 1 October.
Read: the speech itself is a public-relations move; what matters is when it appeared. Both chambers have an ad-ban bill running, PAGCOR's consistent position is tighter control rather than a total ban, and the most persuasive material for that position is evidence that player protection is already being done. The helpline has been live for a little over three months, and this was the week it got pushed onto a health and wellness stage. Two consequences for operators. Responsible gaming is no longer just a line of text on creative; it is becoming the regulator's evidence for its own position, and how well operators cooperate is being noted in that context. And once 15 September passes, someone has to decide which version of the advisory the current creative carries, because PAGCOR has not published the follow-on requirement, which is the item to confirm with the regulator this period. The 30 September accreditation deadline hits content supply directly: if a supplier drops out, its games disappear from the site on 1 October, so any Q4 campaign tied to a specific supplier's titles needs a fallback now.
⚠️ Single-source note: the speech content comes from a PAGCOR release relayed by IAG 9/6, Context.ph 9/5 and The Financial District, which is multi-outlet republication of an official statement rather than independent reporting. Both deadlines are expiry dates on existing rules, not new rules this week. PAGCOR has not published usage data for the helpline.

Ask PAGCOR to confirm in writing what responsible-gaming advisory is required after 15 September and lock the creative template to that answer, so Q4 material does not have to be remade. And ask each game supplier for evidence of accreditation progress, pull any title from a supplier without accreditation by 30 September out of the Q4 campaign plan, and line up replacement content. Both cost almost nothing; the cost of missing them is content going dark after a campaign is live.

Card 4 · DigiPlus / Brand9/04
Mid impact

DigiPlus won 3 awards on responsible gaming.

DigiPlus Interactive, parent of BingoPlus, ArenaPlus and GameZone, took three Awards of Excellence at the 22nd Philippine Quill Awards on 4 September, in the audio/visual, publications and social media categories. The winning programmes covered three subjects: business growth storytelling, integrated financial and sustainability reporting, and responsible-gaming advocacy. The Philippine Quill Awards are run by the International Association of Business Communicators' Philippine chapter and judge the strategy and execution of corporate communication programmes.
Read: the awards do not shift the competitive picture; what is worth noting is the mix of subjects. In a year when both chambers are moving ad-ban bills and endorsement enforcement has already produced a fine, the market leader has put its communication resource into business narrative, sustainability reporting and responsible gaming rather than product and offers. Those three share one property: none is constrained by gambling advertising rules, and all three can run in mainstream media and corporate communication venues. When a ban does land, this is among the few things still publishable, and building it takes quarters rather than starting the day the ban arrives. For an independent operator the barrier here is subject choice rather than budget: a small team can turn "how we handle player protection" into publishable material, provided it starts accumulating the evidence and data now.

§06Implications · threats / opportunities

Implications (threats / opportunities)

Entry parameters (all internal estimates)
  • The two ad-ban bills have to be costed separately: Senate SB 2347 bans advertising, endorsements, product placement, merchandise and sponsorship; House HB 10982 adds a ban on bonuses, free bets and referral codes, names influencers and affiliates as covered parties, and attaches clawback penalties. The two are not consolidated; plan a contingency against the stricter House version. Basis is §04 card 1; not repeated elsewhere.
  • Endorsement contracts now carry a tail liability: the House version requires public figures to return fees received and bars them for three to five years. Multi-year endorsement and affiliate contracts need a regulatory-change termination clause with clawback liability assigned to the brand.
  • Second route to a licence slot: a licence holder can outsource online operation; DigiPlus's TGXI and IEC's NCLI split gross gaming revenue 97% to 3%. This is a PAGCOR-approved precedent and a usable reference point; the licence holder's sanction record travels with the licence and is not severed by outsourcing.
  • Two September deadlines: the responsible-gaming ad retention period expires 9/15 with no published follow-on requirement; B2B supplier accreditation closes 9/30, with unaccredited systems stopped from 10/1. Q4 campaigns tied to a specific supplier need a fallback.
  • The rebate ceiling is set by the regulator: current caps are 1.5% cash rebate on turnover or deposit (excluding casino table and arcade-type games) and 15% cashback on net losses. Differentiation is limited to calculation basis, settlement speed and term readability.
  • Licence fixed costs rise: the minimum guaranteed fee for e-casino operators is PHP 9 million a month (against a PHP 30 million revenue threshold), rising to PHP 10.5 million (against PHP 35 million) from 2027/01/01. Model on the 2027 band.
  • Creative-level restriction: ASC circular 2026-008, in force from 2026/06/24, bars advertising material from showing or referring to roulette, baccarat, blackjack, slot machines, cards, chips and dice.
  • Demand-side baseline: PAGCOR sees 2026 total income down about 18%. Use the 2Q26 official figure (industry GGR PHP 88.13bn, down 20.3%) as the conservative revenue assumption.
ThreatsThreats
  • Ad-ban pressure has gone from one Senate bill to a version in each chamber, and the House text is stricter.Influencers and affiliates are named parties, promotional mechanics are banned, and clawback penalties apply. Cost of inaction (internal estimate): signing multi-year endorsement deals on current terms through Q4 would force a one-off unwind and renegotiation if the bill passes, turning an estimated 15-30% of that contract value into sunk cost, plus a 4-8 week gap while replacements are found.
  • If promotional mechanics go too, the whole acquisition script fails, not just the creative.Bonuses, free bets and referral codes are the shared entry pitch across all nine. Cost of inaction (internal estimate): starting list-building only at that point takes an estimated 6-9 months to reach usable scale, with new-customer acquisition cost up an estimated 40-80% in the interim.
  • If leasing out a licensed shell gets copied, brand count keeps rising underneath the 48 licensees.DigiPlus and NCLI have done it once, with regulatory approval. Cost of inaction (internal estimate): planning on "licensee count capped means rival count capped" would understate Q4 media cost by an estimated 20-40%, as launch subsidies from new platforms push acquisition prices up across the board.
  • Content supply may break after the 30 September accreditation deadline, and campaigns are usually tied to specific suppliers.Unaccredited systems stop operating from 1 October. Cost of inaction (internal estimate): a Q4 lead campaign tied to a dropped supplier's titles needs a mid-flight content change, with rework and buy adjustment costing an estimated 20-40% of that campaign's budget.
  • DigiPlus now holds four channels outside gambling advertising rules; independent operators hold none.A free-to-play category, a land-based resort position, award-winning corporate communication, and as of this week a new platform on a third-party licence. Cost of inaction (internal estimate): budgeting on "the ban hits everyone equally" understates the share-of-voice gap against DigiPlus, and this week's white-label platform widens it another notch; directional, to be recalibrated once a bill passes.
