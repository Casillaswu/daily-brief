1. BSP gives 12 months to comply, because gambling must go direct.
The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP, the central bank, which oversees banks, payments and e-wallets) posted a draft circular, Regulations to Strengthen Integrity Controls in Payment Transactions, on 7 September for public comment, with a 2 October deadline. The draft amends the payments manual and names six merchant categories (casinos and online gambling operators; gaming system administrators, platform operators and their affiliates; lawful adult content; virtual asset service providers; money service businesses; and anything BSP later adds). Those six may only be acquired under a direct merchant arrangement, meaning the acquirer contracts with the merchant directly and no third party may collect or settle in between. Existing institutions get six months to inventory merchant relationships and a further six to fix or terminate non-compliant layered arrangements. The draft also freezes new payment system operator registrations for 12 months. See §04 card 1. (BSP draft PDF · BusinessWorld 9/7)

2. PhilWeb puts P4.23B into JKS, because it needs digital rails.
PhilWeb and its wholly owned PhilWeb Capital subscribed to 10.73 million JKS Tech Solutions Common B shares at P394 each, P4.23 billion in total, for a 30% stake in JKS. In the reverse leg, JKS took 81.38 million PhilWeb treasury shares (shares the company had bought back) at P16.50, P1.34 billion in total, about 4.85% of PhilWeb. The Philippine Stock Exchange classified the deal a substantial acquisition, suspended trading at 9am on 8 September and lifted it on 10 September once the required disclosure was filed. The transaction still needs approval from JKS shareholders and the Philippine Securities and Exchange Commission. JKS describes itself as a B2B platform and digital infrastructure provider serving mid-market licensed operators. See §04 card 2. (Inquirer 9/8 · BusinessWorld 9/10)

3. Moody's rates DigiPlus B1, because 2026 EBITDA falls 20%.
Moody's assigned DigiPlus Interactive (parent of BingoPlus, ArenaPlus and GameZone) a first-time B1 corporate family rating with a stable outlook on 10 September. B1 sits below investment grade. The same opinion puts 2026 EBITDA at about P11.4 billion, down 20.3% from roughly P14.2 billion in 2025, recovering to P14-15 billion across 2027-28. Moody's attributes the drop to the central bank's August 2025 order requiring e-wallets to unlink in-app access to online gambling platforms, plus fuel prices and inflation squeezing discretionary spend. See §04 card 3. (IAG 9/11 · GGRAsia)

PaymentsAll three storylines sit on the same layer. The central bank wants gambling merchants acquired directly, and Moody's has become the first outside party to name the August 2025 e-wallet unlinking as the reason earnings fell. Read: over the next year the gap between operators will show up in payment approval rates before it shows up in promo numbers. Operators with a direct bank relationship keep costs under control; those running through aggregators have 12 months to rebuild, and get cut off if they cannot. The first question to ask a prospective client shifts from budget size to whether their acquiring is direct or layered.
CapitalPhilWeb spent P4.23 billion for 30% of an infrastructure provider in the same week Moody's said consolidation favours incumbents. Read: both point the same way. The market is contracting, cash-rich players are buying capability upstream, and smaller operators and white labels get absorbed or exit. For pricing work, the practical effect is that the list of clients able to pay reliably in Q4 gets shorter, last year's rates are hard to hold, and performance-based terms become easier to negotiate.
EndorsementManny Pacquiao, brand ambassador for DigiPlus properties ArenaPlus and GameZone, was sworn in on 8 September as lead convenor of the National Anti-Poverty Commission at cabinet secretary rank. His own payment platform MannyPay is already integrated with the payment flows of BingoPlus, ArenaPlus and GameZone. Read: endorser risk no longer stops at the endorser. Neither side has commented on the contract since the appointment, but the first time a reporter or senator puts the two roles side by side, an operator's endorsement assets can become unusable overnight.

§02Strategic Read · 5Q

Strategic read · five questions (neutral)

Q1
Market space

Everything on the supply side this week happened in payments and capital. The central bank draft requires gambling merchants to be acquired under a direct arrangement with no third party collecting or settling in between, with six months to inventory and six more to fix. In the same week PhilWeb paid P4.23 billion for 30% of a digital infrastructure provider, and Moody's judged that consolidation in Philippine online gaming favours incumbents.

The underlying structure is unchanged: no new online licences since March 2024, licensee count down from 74 to 48.

Read: the shape of the entry barrier has changed. It used to be licence plus advertising budget; now acquiring architecture comes first. An operator with a licence and players but layered acquiring has 12 months from effectivity to move to direct, or lose its payment rails. For an entrant, payment architecture moves from an annex of due diligence into the top three items.

Sources: direct merchant arrangement text · BSP draft PDF · consolidation favours incumbents · IAG 9/11
Q2
Player focus

The familiar player complaints got a number attached this week. Moody's listed the August 2025 order unlinking in-app e-wallet access as one reason industry-wide online GGR fell, and cut DigiPlus 2026 EBITDA 20.3% on that basis. It is the first time a third party outside the regulator and the operators has converted deposit friction into a figure.

If the new draft takes effect as written, deposit routes tighten again. Read: "transfers are a hassle" is a structural problem, not a support problem, and it gets tighter before it gets easier. Two things remain in an operator's control: state clearly which payment methods work, and tell players the day a route changes rather than after three failed attempts.

Sources: EBITDA down 20.3% and attribution · GGRAsia
Q3
Unmet gaps

Five long-standing gaps remain unaddressed: plain-language compliance creative, alternative payment guides, promo-terms calculators, identity-verification walkthroughs, and licence-check explainers. The sixth added last period, retention content that is not a promo code, also went untouched.

A seventh appears this week, from the draft's remediation timeline. None of the nine has published what happens when a payment route is switched off: when players are told, through which channel, and what happens to unsettled balances. Read: a player communications plan for payment disruption is an open flag. The draft grants 12 months. Whoever writes and publishes that process first will see an order of magnitude fewer complaints and less churn at the next disruption.

The creative constraint still runs. Since ASC (the Philippine Ad Standards Council) circular 2026-008 took effect on 24 June, advertising in any medium may not show or mention roulette, baccarat, blackjack, slot machines, cards, chips or dice.

Sources: 6+6 month remediation window · Inquirer 9/7
Q4
Price structure

Two templates as before: DigiPlus runs events, everyone else runs rebates, and nobody changed anything this week. Seven weeks in, none of the nine has launched a new tier; the sports offer that expired 7/19 and the CasinoPlus 1.5% that expired 7/31 both sit empty.

The 1.5% is a regulatory ceiling. Under current PAGCOR rules, e-games operators may give cash rebates of at most 1.5% on turnover or deposits (excluding casino table and arcade-type games), and cash back of at most 15% on net losses. Read: this column holds no surprises this year. The cap is fixed, demand is shrinking, and the cost side now carries the bill for rebuilding acquiring. What can still create distance is how cash back is calculated, how fast it lands, and whether the terms are readable, none of which is capped.

Sources: 1.5% rebate / 15% cash back caps · GGRAsia
Q5
Differentiation

The three storylines sit on separate layers: regulation, with the central bank narrowing gambling acquiring to direct relationships (Philstar 9/7, BusinessWorld 9/7); capital, with PhilWeb buying upstream infrastructure and a two-day PSE suspension (Inquirer 9/8, BusinessWorld 9/10); and external assessment, with Moody's putting a number on the earnings impact of e-wallet unlinking (IAG 9/11, GGRAsia). The nine operators themselves did nothing, and price tiers are quiet for a seventh week.

Conclusion: the decisive line this year has moved one layer below advertising, down to payments. The ad bans are still at bill stage with no timetable; the payment draft is already in consultation with dates on paper. One decides whether you can be heard, the other decides whether money can come in, and the second arrives first.

Proposal, three items. First, put acquiring architecture in the top three of due diligence for every partner and acquisition target: direct bank relationship or payment aggregator, and what the 12-month migration plan looks like. Second, start writing the player communications plan for payment disruption, covering notice timing, channel and unsettled balances; none of the nine has one. Third, decide before 2 October whether to file a named comment on the draft, particularly on how far the term "affiliates" reaches. Proposals, not conclusions.

Sources: Philstar 9/7 · BusinessWorld 9/10 · IAG 9/11

Card 1 · BSP / payment rules9/07-9/14
High impact

BSP gives 12 months to comply, because gambling must go direct.

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas posted its draft circular, Regulations to Strengthen Integrity Controls in Payment Transactions, on the policy exposure drafts page on 7 September. It amends MORPS, the manual of regulations for payment systems, with comments due 2 October at ppdd@bsp.gov.ph. Section 1(l) lists six merchant categories: casinos and other operators of gambling, betting, wagering or games of chance; gaming system administrators, gaming platform operators and their affiliates; lawful adult content providers; virtual asset service providers; money service businesses; and any others BSP may add. These six may be accepted only under a direct merchant arrangement, paired with enhanced due diligence, enhanced monitoring, and transaction and settlement limits.
A direct merchant arrangement is defined as an authorised supervised institution holding a direct relationship with the merchant, with no intermediary accepting, processing, collecting, receiving, allocating or settling payments on its behalf. The layered structure in which an operator reaches an acquiring bank through a payment service provider or aggregator is not permitted under this draft.
Three supporting provisions. Section 5 establishes a centralised national QR code merchant database. Section 7(a) gives existing supervised institutions six months to inventory current merchant relationships and a further six to remediate or terminate non-compliant layered arrangements, with enforcement under MORPS Part 15 after 12 months. Section 7(c) suspends acceptance of new payment system operator registrations for 12 months from effectivity while BSP reviews its licensing and taxonomy frameworks. Section 8 states the circular takes effect 15 days after publication, but the signature block is blank, so the calendar date cannot yet be worked out.
Read: this draft has a harder edge and a nearer timetable than either ad ban bill. Those are still awaiting first reading; this is in consultation with a deadline and drafted text. Three things matter. First, the text names gaming system administrators, platform operators and affiliates, so the outsourced-operations and white-label layer is caught directly, not by implication. Second, the direct requirement is effectively a capital threshold: contracting straight with an acquiring bank, passing enhanced due diligence and absorbing transaction limits favours operators with scale and a compliance team, which leaves smaller operators and white labels to merge or exit. That is the same thing Moody's said in the same week about consolidation favouring incumbents. Third, 12 months looks generous, but renegotiating banks and rebuilding risk controls and reconciliation eats most of it. For anyone taking on marketing work, the immediate move is to establish a partner's acquiring architecture, because payment approval rates set real acquisition cost ahead of any promo figure.

PhilWeb and wholly owned PhilWeb Capital subscribed to 10.73 million JKS Tech Solutions Common B shares at P394 each, P4.23 billion in total (about US$67.4-67.5 million), for a 30% stake in JKS. Trading resumed on 10 September once PhilWeb filed the required disclosure; follow-up coverage on 14 September reported the shares rose afterwards. The transaction still needs approval from JKS shareholders and the Philippine Securities and Exchange Commission, along with other regulatory requirements and closing conditions, and the JKS shares will be issued through an increase in authorised capital stock. The PSE also noted that its determination is a disclosure requirement and does not classify the deal as a reverse takeover. JKS describes itself as a B2B platform and digital infrastructure provider serving mid-market licensed operators. PhilWeb is chaired by Lance Gokongwei, who joined the board in July and was elected chairman on 28 August.
Read: put the money alongside the central bank draft from the same week and the direction is clear. PhilWeb runs online operations for several integrated resorts, which places it squarely inside the draft's list of gaming system administrators and platform operators. Buying digital infrastructure upstream reads most plainly as acquiring in-house payment and compliant acquiring capability, matching what DigiPlus already has. Seen from the other side, P4.23 billion of cash going out in one transaction says this company's resources this year go to infrastructure rather than marketing. For anyone taking on marketing work, that means white-label clients in this group will not expand media spend soon and last year's rates will not hold. What to watch is what JKS actually covers technically, and whether PhilWeb brands disclose their acquiring architecture after the deal, which would be the first verifiable signal.

Card 3 · Moody's / DigiPlus9/10-9/12
Mid impact

Moody's rates DigiPlus B1, because 2026 EBITDA falls 20%.

Moody's assigned DigiPlus Interactive (parent of BingoPlus, ArenaPlus and GameZone) a first-time B1 corporate family rating with a stable outlook on 10 September. B1 is speculative grade, below investment grade. The same opinion puts 2026 EBITDA at about P11.4 billion (roughly US$181.8 million), down 20.3% from about P14.2 billion in 2025, recovering to P14-15 billion across 2027 and 2028 on organic growth, the consolidation of International Entertainment and overseas investments.
Moody's gives three reasons for the decline: the central bank's August 2025 directive requiring e-wallets and payment providers to unlink in-app access to online gambling platforms, which lowered industry-wide online GGR; weaker consumer sentiment with fuel prices high; and inflation compressing discretionary entertainment spend. Two further judgments: consolidation in Philippine online gaming favours incumbents, and the company retains headroom to take on debt for growth.
Read: the value here is not the rating, it is where the attribution comes from. Until now the August 2025 e-wallet unlinking was something PAGCOR and the operators said about themselves. This is the first time an outside rating agency has written it into a formal document and converted it into money. That pulls the 7 September central bank draft out of the "future risk" column and into "already priced". Same regulatory logic: the last round cost the market leader a fifth of annual earnings, and this round goes after the acquiring architecture itself. Also worth holding on to is the line about consolidation favouring incumbents. It and the PhilWeb acquisition in the same week are two faces of one script, and what disappears is the budget of smaller operators and white labels, which is where independent operators have found most of their clients for years. From Q4 onward, rank prospects by ability to pay before rate card.

DigiPlus endorser joins the Cabinet, because he now leads NAPC.

Manny Pacquiao, the former world champion boxer and former senator, was sworn in on 8 September as lead convenor of the National Anti-Poverty Commission, replacing Lope Santos III. The post carries cabinet secretary rank and, under Republic Act 8425, covers day-to-day operations of the commission and liaison with its 14 basic sectors.
The same person has been brand ambassador for DigiPlus properties ArenaPlus and GameZone since 25 March 2026. That arrangement has two layers: ten co-branded games (Pacquiao Fortune, Manny Punch, Super Ace Pacquiao and others), and the integration of payment flows for BingoPlus, ArenaPlus and GameZone into MannyPay, the payment platform he chairs, with DigiPlus as MannyPay's first official gaming partner.
As of this issue no termination or suspension of the endorsement has been announced, and neither side has commented on the contract's status since the appointment. None of the 8 and 9 September appointment coverage mentions the gambling connection.
Read: this is not something to act on this week, it is a position to note. One person now sits at both ends: in cabinet as the official responsible for anti-poverty policy, which is precisely the constituency both ad ban bills invoke to argue gambling causes harm, and as the largest operator's brand ambassador and payment channel. HB 10982 names public figures as subjects of the ban, requiring return of endorsement fees and a three to five year bar; CICC has 12 to 15 cases open against gambling endorsers and influencers, targeting the individuals themselves. A reporter or senator will eventually put those three facts side by side. What moves is an implicit assumption in how work is priced: that endorser risk stays with the endorser. In the Philippines that no longer holds, and an operator's endorsement assets can go to zero overnight. Multi-year endorsement contracts need two clauses: a takedown procedure and deadline if the endorser's status changes, and a split of payment for unaired flights.

§06Implications · threats / opportunities

Implications (threats / opportunities)

Entry parameters (all internal estimates)
  • Payment architecture becomes an entry condition: the central bank draft limits gambling operators, gaming system administrators, platform operators and affiliates to direct merchant arrangements, with six months to inventory existing layered structures and six more to remediate. Due diligence on every partner and acquisition target must cover acquiring architecture and a migration plan. Basis in §04 card 1, not repeated elsewhere.
  • New payment operator registrations frozen for 12 months: from effectivity, no new payment system operator registrations are accepted. If the entry plan includes building a payment entity, that route closes for a year from effectivity and has to shift to partnering with an existing licensed provider.
  • The two ad ban bills need separate modelling: Senate SB 2347 bans advertising, endorsement, product placement, merchandise and sponsorship; House HB 10982 adds a ban on bonuses, free bets and referral codes, names influencers and affiliates as subjects, and carries retroactive penalties. The two have not been consolidated, so plan a contingency against the stricter House version.
  • Endorser status risk: beyond the bills' retroactive penalties, a new category appeared this week, an endorser taking public office. Multi-year endorsement contracts need a takedown deadline on status change and a payment split for unaired flights.
  • Two demand-side baselines: PAGCOR sees 2026 total revenue down about 18%; Moody's sees the market leader's 2026 EBITDA down 20.3%, back to P14-15 billion in 2027-28. The two measure different things (regulator revenue versus one operator's earnings) and are not interchangeable; list both as a conservative baseline.
  • The rebate ceiling is set by the regulator: current caps are 1.5% cash rebate on turnover or deposits (excluding casino table and arcade-type games) and 15% cash back on net losses. Differentiation is limited to calculation basis, settlement speed and readability of terms.
  • Fixed licence cost rises: the minimum guaranteed fee for e-casino operators is currently P9 million a month (against a P30 million revenue threshold), rising to P10.5 million (against P35 million) from 2027/01/01. Financial models should use the 2027 band.
  • Creative-level limits: ASC circular 2026-008 has applied since 2026/06/24; advertising may not show or mention roulette, baccarat, blackjack, slot machines, cards, chips or dice.
  • Two September deadlines: the responsible gaming ad retention period ends 9/15 with no successor version published; the B2B supplier accreditation deadline is 9/30, with unaccredited systems ceasing to operate from 10/1. Q4 campaigns tied to specific suppliers' games need a fallback.
ThreatsThreats
  • Regulatory pressure has moved from the advertising layer down to payments, and the payments timetable is nearer. The ad bills await first reading; the payment draft is in consultation with comments due 10/2. Cost of inaction (internal estimate): if a partner runs layered acquiring and does not migrate within the 12-month window, a cut-off stalls campaigns for an estimated 4-8 weeks, with recovery on already-committed spend estimated below 30%.
  • Direct merchant arrangements are a capital threshold that pushes smaller operators and white labels toward merger or exit. Moody's judged in the same week that consolidation favours incumbents, and PhilWeb spent P4.23 billion on upstream capability. Cost of inaction (internal estimate): if the client list stays weighted to smaller operators and white labels, expect an estimated 30-50% of it to fall away between Q4 and H1 next year, concentrated at the weakest end for ability to pay.
  • Upstream technology and acquiring capability are being bought up by the leaders, and rental terms worsen year on year. PhilWeb swapped a 30% stake for JKS's infrastructure; DigiPlus already builds its own. Cost of inaction (internal estimate): if the entry plan still assumes back-end technology can be rented at today's rates, three-year total cost of ownership is understated by an estimated 20-40%.
  • Endorsement assets can be voided overnight by a change in the endorser's status, and contracts usually do not cover it. The ArenaPlus and GameZone endorser has joined the cabinet, and CICC has 12 to 15 cases open against endorsers and influencers. Cost of inaction (internal estimate): with creative already in heavy rotation and no takedown clause, a single endorser going out of use costs an estimated 15-30% of a season's budget in reshoots and media adjustments.
  • Content may go dark after the 30 September accreditation deadline, now 16 days out with no sign of a further extension. Unaccredited systems cease to operate from 1 October. Cost of inaction (internal estimate): if a Q4 headline campaign is tied to a lagging supplier's games, swapping content mid-flight costs an estimated 20-40% of that campaign's budget in remakes and media changes.
OpportunitiesOpportunities
  • Make acquiring architecture a standard item in due diligence and contracting (internal estimate ~P150K-P350K in legal and technical advice / 4-6 weeks) The draft text is public and the definition of a direct merchant arrangement is clear, so it can be turned into a reusable checklist and contract clause now. Note: clauses should be settled by counsel familiar with Philippine payment regulation, not lifted from another jurisdiction's template.
  • Write the player communications plan for payment disruption (internal estimate ~P100K-P250K / one-off plus a rehearsal) Notice timing, notice channel, unsettled balance handling and support scripts. None of the nine has published such a process, so the flag is open. Note: the content must not carry promotional rewards or referral codes, or it may be treated as advertising.
  • File a named comment on the draft before 2 October to gain room on scope (internal estimate ~P80K-P200K / 2-3 weeks) The focus is the boundary of "affiliates" and whether traffic-referral arrangements fall inside it. Note: filing discloses identity and business model, so confirm the current structure will bear scrutiny before submitting.
  • Turn endorser risk into contract template language (internal estimate ~P60K-P150K / one-off) Takedown deadline on status change, payment split for unaired flights, termination right when a regulator opens a case. Note: terms have to be negotiated on equal footing with the endorser's representatives; loading risk onto one side raises the fee or kills the deal.
