This week in brief

S&P rated DigiPlus B+ on a 40-50% share forecast.
PAGCOR's split still awaits an order as GCG has not finished.
BSP shut 8,000+ merchant accounts over fake shopfront cash-in.

1. S&P rated DigiPlus B+ on a 40-50% share forecast.
S&P Global Ratings assigned DigiPlus Interactive (parent of BingoPlus, ArenaPlus and GameZone) a first-time B+ long-term issuer credit rating with a stable outlook on 17-18 September. B+ sits in speculative grade. The same report expects the company to hold 40% to 50% of the Philippine online gaming market over the next two years, puts the second-largest operator at 15% to 20%, and records that the company's share fell from 47% in 2024 to 41% in 2025. S&P credits product quality and user engagement for the lead, and names regulatory change and execution on new projects as the main risks, while judging that tighter regulation and consolidation work in the company's favour. See §04 card 1. (GGRAsia 9/18 · IAG 9/17)

2. PAGCOR's split still awaits an order as GCG has not finished.
PAGCOR chairman and chief executive Alejandro Tengco told the IAG Academy Summit 2026 keynote at Newport World Resorts, Manila on 15 September that the proposal to separate the agency's regulatory functions from its casino-operating functions remains under review by the Governance Commission for GOCCs (GCG), with a decision expected soon. The route is: GCG completes its review and endorses the proposal to the Office of the President; if the President finds merit, an executive order implements the separation. His stated reason is that a regulator should not regulate its own operations. In separate remarks on 18 September he said the regulatory framework will keep evolving as conditions change and experience accumulates. See §04 card 2. (IAG 9/15 · GGRAsia 9/15)

3. BSP shut 8,000+ merchant accounts over fake shopfront cash-in.
The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP, the central bank, which supervises banks, payments and e-wallets) closed more than 8,000 merchant accounts tied to unlicensed online casinos. The accounts were registered as bakeries, salons and small shops but were used to process betting funds; the detection signal was large volumes of very small late-night payments. PAGCOR said it was aware of these innocuous-sounding fronts but has no jurisdiction over the payment side and is working with the central bank. A second figure disclosed at the same event: channelisation to licensed sites now runs at roughly 50%, against roughly 75% before the e-wallet delinking. See §04 card 3. (IAG 9/17 · Focus Gaming News)

Competitive shapeS&P put the gap between the leader and the runner-up into numbers: 40-50% against 15-20%. Read: this changes the baseline language for any pitch. The habitual description of the Philippine online market is three or four head brands plus a tail of smaller ones; S&P's version is one dominant operator, one distant second, and everyone else splitting the remaining third. Two practical consequences. Work for the leader comes with budgets and approval layers an order of magnitude above the rest. Work for anyone else is not a volume purchase but a differentiation purchase, and a proposal built on reach alone will be sent back.
Regulatory structureOnce GCG finishes, the file goes to the Office of the President and the last step is an executive order. Read: this has been discussed for four years and this is the first time there are concrete procedural nodes. The point is not the timeline but the post-split finances. Without operating income, the regulator's revenue is fees and fines only. Fees are already rising, with the second minimum guarantee fee step effective 1 January 2027. What is left to move is enforcement intensity. Anyone handling compliant creative should plan next year on the assumption of denser audits and faster penalties, not on this year's rhythm.
Payments8,000 front accounts closed, channelisation down from roughly 75% to roughly 50%. Read: the two numbers describe one thing. Half the handle sits in the black market, and the central bank is using the payment rail to squeeze it back. For licensees this is a medium-term positive, since the displaced handle has to go somewhere. For creative teams it is a short-term problem, because once bank-side suspicious transaction detection tightens, legitimate deposits get caught too. §02Strategic Read · 5Q

Strategic read · five questions (neutral statement)

Q1
Market space

For the first time an outside institution has put this market's share structure into numbers. S&P expects the leader to hold 40% to 50% over the next two years, the second operator 15% to 20%, and the remaining licensees to split roughly the last third. The same report records the leader's share falling from 47% in 2024 to 41% in 2025, while judging that tighter regulation and consolidation will stop the slide.

The underlying condition is unchanged: new online licences have been frozen since March 2024 and the licensee count has fallen from 74 to 48.

Read: the shape of this market is one dominant operator plus a distant second, not the three-or-four head brands it is usually described as. For an entrant the practical consequence is that meaningful share does not come from organic growth but from absorbing the players and licences of those who leave. S&P says as much: consolidation favours incumbents. The first question in an entry assessment therefore shifts from "how do we reach 5%" to "which licensees exit in the next 12 months, and where do their players go".

Q2
Player focus

The channelisation figure became public this week: roughly 50% of handle goes to licensed sites, against roughly 75% before the e-wallet delinking. Half the handle sits on unlicensed sites.

In the same period the central bank closed more than 8,000 merchant accounts registered as bakeries, salons and small shops and used to process betting funds.

Read: players are not avoiding licensed sites by preference; depositing at a licensed site is simply more trouble than at a black-market one. What lets the black market hold half the handle is exactly the easy cash-in those disguised fronts provide. Once that route is blocked, those players will compare again, and the first thing they compare is whether the deposit goes through. The most useful thing an operator can do in this period is not to add promotions but to state clearly which payment methods work and how long funds take to land.

Q3
Unmet gaps

Six standing gaps remain unaddressed: plain-language compliance creative, alternative payment guidance, promotional terms calculators, identity verification walkthroughs, licence verification explainers, and turning non-promotional retention content into an asset. The player communication plan for payment disruptions, added last issue, has also gone untouched.

An eighth gap arrives with the channelisation figure. None of the nine has explained, in language a player would actually read, what the difference is between playing on a licensed site and an unlicensed one.

Read: this has been treated as the regulator's job, but the regulator speaks in compliance language and players do not read it. With half the handle in the black market, the persuadable audience is half the market, and nobody is speaking to it. Whoever first puts the licensed-versus-unlicensed difference into language players understand takes position in that half first.

The creative-level constraint continues to run. Since ASC Circular 2026-008 took effect on 24 June, advertising in any medium may not show or mention roulette, baccarat, blackjack, slot machines, cards, chips or dice.

Q4
Offer structure

Two formats remain: DigiPlus runs events, everyone else runs rebates, and nobody switched this week. The last new offer dates to July; the sports offer that expired on 7/19 and the CasinoPlus 1.5% that expired on 7/31 both sit empty.

1.5% is a regulatory ceiling. Under current PAGCOR rules, cash rebates calculated on turnover or deposits are capped at 1.5% for electronic gaming operators (excluding casino table and arcade-type games), and cash-back on net losses is capped at 15%.

Read: the quiet in this column now has an explanation. S&P attributes the leader's 40-50% share to product and user engagement rather than price, and the rest have no room for a price war in a shrinking market. Rebate ceiling fixed, demand contracting, and an additional bill for re-architecting acquiring: stacked together, the remainder of this year will not produce meaningful price competition. What still separates brands is the rebate calculation basis, crediting speed and terms readability.

Q5
Differentiation

The week's three storylines sit on separate layers. Competitive: S&P wrote the share structure as 40-50% against 15-20%. Structural: PAGCOR's decoupling goes from GCG to the Office of the President, implemented by executive order. Enforcement: the central bank closed 8,000+ disguised merchant accounts, with channelisation at roughly 50%. The nine operators themselves did nothing, and offers stayed still.

Conclusion: the three variables in this market are now each pinned down. Share structure is settled at one dominant operator plus a distant second. The regulatory apparatus is being rebuilt, and after the split the regulator lives on fees and fines. Payment enforcement is tightening, and the half of the handle sitting in the black market is being squeezed back. All three point the same way: the market gets smaller, more concentrated and stricter, but the licensed share of it gets larger.

Proposals: three. First, put partners' deposit approval and decline curves into routine reporting, and ask for the last three months now, because they move before promotional metrics do. Second, prepare player-facing material explaining the licensed-versus-unlicensed difference in plain language rather than compliance language; none of the nine has done it. Third, budget next year's compliant-creative scheduling buffer and backup versions on the assumption of denser audits and faster penalties from 2027. These are proposals, not conclusions.

Card 2 · PAGCOR / regulatory structure9/15-9/18
High impact

PAGCOR's split still awaits an order as GCG has not finished.

PAGCOR chairman and chief executive Alejandro Tengco (in post since 2022) told the IAG Academy Summit 2026 keynote at Newport World Resorts, Manila on 15 September that the proposal to separate the agency's regulatory functions from its casino-operating functions remains under GCG review, with a decision expected soon.
The route has three stages: GCG completes its review and sends the proposal to the Office of the President; if the President finds merit, an executive order implements the separation; after implementation PAGCOR gives up the casino-operator role and regulates only. His stated reason: "You do not want a regulator to regulate its own operations." The question has been discussed since 2022, and trade coverage generally expects implementation no earlier than late 2026 or early 2027. In separate remarks on 18 September he said the regulatory framework will keep evolving as conditions change and experience accumulates, with continuing review of what works and where improvement is needed.
Read: the first concrete procedural nodes in four years are worth recording, but the thing to watch is not the timeline; it is the post-split financial structure. PAGCOR today is both the regulator and one of the largest casino operators, and operating income carries part of its costs. Once that is removed, the regulator's revenue is fees and fines. Fees are already rising, with the second minimum guarantee fee step effective 1 January 2027. What is left to move is enforcement intensity and penalty severity. For anyone handling marketing work the consequence is that audits get denser and the interval from detection to penalty gets shorter, while creative review and revision schedules are built on the current rhythm. The second consequence is that the counterparty changes. After the split the successor body takes over regulatory business, and nobody has said whether existing memoranda, circulars and case rulings need re-confirmation. Compliance documents spanning years should leave room to re-confirm rather than citing current numbers as fixed.

Card 3 · BSP / payment enforcement9/16-9/17
High impact

BSP shut 8,000+ merchant accounts over fake shopfront cash-in.

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas closed more than 8,000 merchant accounts tied to unlicensed online casinos. The accounts were registered with payment providers as bakeries, salons and small shops, and were in fact used to process betting funds. The detection signal the central bank described was large volumes of very small payments appearing late at night.
The clean-up belongs to the same line as the draft Regulations to Strengthen Integrity Controls in Payment Transactions, posted for public consultation on 7 September. The draft requires payment providers to collect more information before onboarding a new merchant, including beneficial owners and relevant licences; comments close 2 October, 11 days from now. PAGCOR's position is that it is aware of these innocuous-sounding fronts operating as unregistered gaming businesses, but has no jurisdiction over the payment side and is coordinating with the central bank. A second figure from the same occasion: channelisation to licensed sites now runs at roughly 50%, against roughly 75% before the e-wallet delinking, a gap of about 25 percentage points.
Read: the 8,000 figure has to be read together with the 50%, not separately. Half the handle sits in the black market, and what lets the black market hold it is precisely the easy cash-in those 8,000 disguised fronts provide, since black-market sites cannot contract with a bank for acquiring directly and can only borrow a shell. Removing the shells cuts a slice out of the black market's deposit capacity. For licensees that is a medium-term positive, because the displaced handle has to go somewhere and legitimate sites are the only option still standing. Two short-term problems come first. One: once bank-side suspicious transaction detection tightens, the test is transaction pattern rather than licence status, so legitimate small high-frequency deposits fall into the same sieve and false declines rise first. Two: that 25-point gap quantifies, for the first time, where the August 2025 delinking pushed the handle, and the draft is a second round of tightening. Last issue recommended putting acquiring architecture into due diligence; this issue adds the measurable indicator: deposit approval and decline rates. Those two curves move before any promotional metric does.

Medium impact

DigiPlus won two IAG awards on in-house live formats.

The 2026 Inside Asian Gaming Academy Awards gala was held at Newport World Resorts, Manila on 15 September. DigiPlus took both online gaming division awards, Best iGaming Operator and Best iGaming Innovation, as the inaugural winner of each.
The innovation award cited the company's in-house livestreamed digital bingo and perya (traditional Filipino fairground games) formats, with the citation naming three features: local content, proprietary production and real-time interaction. The operator award cited market leadership and overall contribution to the Philippine online industry. DigiPlus president Ping Chen said in the winner's statement that the two awards affirm the company's contribution to the growth and innovation of gaming across Asia-Pacific.

§06Implications · threats / opportunities

Implications (threats / opportunities)

Entry parameters (all internal estimates)
  • Re-base share structure on S&P: leader 40-50%, second 15-20%, the other 48 licensees splitting roughly the last third. Recompute first-year share targets and acquisition cost on that basis. Basis in §04 card 1, not repeated elsewhere.
  • Absorption is the only remaining route to share: S&P judges consolidation favours incumbents, licences have been frozen since March 2024 and licensees are down from 74 to 48. Organic room is limited, so absorbing likely exiters' players and licences must be the primary route, judged on minimum guarantee fee capacity and 9/30 accreditation progress.
  • The regulatory counterparty may change around 2027: after GCG the proposal goes to the Office of the President and is implemented by executive order, with trade coverage expecting no earlier than late 2026 to early 2027. Contracts running past 2027 should cite "the rules in force of PAGCOR or its successor in function" rather than pinning circular numbers.
  • After the split the regulator lives on fees and fines: operating income disappears and fees are already rising (minimum guarantee fee stage two effective 2027/01/01, 102 days from now), so expect denser enforcement and faster penalties. Budget compliant-creative buffers and backup versions on that basis.
  • The observable payment indicator is now defined: deposit approval and decline curves, broken out by payment channel, as a routine partner reporting item. Channelisation at roughly 50% (roughly 75% before delinking) is an industry benchmark, not a single-operator metric.
  • The two advertising bills must be costed separately: Senate SB 2347 bans advertising, endorsement, placement, merchandise and sponsorship; House HB 10982 additionally bans bonuses, free bets and referral codes as promotional mechanics, and names influencers and affiliates as subjects of the ban with retroactive penalties. They have not been consolidated, so plan a contingency version against the stricter House text.
  • The rebate ceiling is drawn by the regulator: currently 1.5% on turnover or deposits (excluding casino table and arcade-type games) and 15% cash-back on net losses. Differentiation is only available in the calculation basis, crediting speed and terms readability.
  • Differentiation has moved to local content: both head brands entered this year's innovation award with in-house content. Capability inventories must include the cost and cycle of in-house content, compared against the exposure the same budget buys in media.
  • Creative-level constraint: ASC Circular 2026-008, in force since 2026/06/24, bars advertising from showing or mentioning roulette, baccarat, blackjack, slot machines, cards, chips or dice.
  • Two near-term deadlines: B2B supplier accreditation closes 9/30, 9 days from now, with unaccredited systems ceasing operation from 10/1; BSP draft comments close 10/2, 11 days from now. The responsible gambling creative retention period expired on 9/15, 6 days ago, and successor requirements remain unpublished.
ThreatsThreats
  • The market is one dominant operator, and any model built on "head brands in parallel" will be wrong throughout. S&P puts the leader at 40-50% and the second at 15-20%. Cost of inaction (internal estimate): with share targets set on the old assumption, first-year acquisition cost is understated by an estimated 30-50%, and budget is misallocated toward competing for non-leader clients.
  • After the split the regulator lives on fees and fines, so enforcement density and penalty speed rise. Operating income disappears and minimum guarantee fee stage two takes effect 2027/01/01. Cost of inaction (internal estimate): with compliant creative still scheduled on this year's rhythm and no backup versions, a single audit stalls a campaign for an estimated 2-4 weeks, with recovery on spend already placed estimated below 50%.
  • Legitimate deposits will be wrongly declined while the payment layer tightens, because the test is transaction pattern rather than licence status. The central bank closed 8,000+ disguised merchant accounts on a late-night micro-payment signal. Cost of inaction (internal estimate): without a prior baseline for partners' deposit approval rates, an estimated extra 2-3 weeks passes between noticing false declines and locating the cause, with acquisition spend in that period close to fully wasted.
  • Differentiation has moved to local content production while media optimisation capability is devaluing. Both head brands entered the same award with in-house content. Cost of inaction (internal estimate): with a capability mix still weighted to media buying, the addressable brief range over the next 12 months narrows by an estimated 30-40%, with continuing erosion of pricing power.
  • Content may go dark after the 30 September accreditation deadline, now 9 days away with no sign of a further extension. Unaccredited systems cease operation from 1 October. Cost of inaction (internal estimate): with 9 days left, a Q4 lead campaign still tied to unaccredited supplier games would need emergency content substitution from 1 October, with creative rework and media adjustment estimated at 20-40% of that campaign's budget.
OpportunitiesOpportunities
  • Make deposit approval and decline rates a routine reporting item (internal estimate ~₱80K-₱200K in advisory and integration time / 3-4 weeks). Broken out by payment channel, with a baseline and alert thresholds. Nobody is asking for this today, and whoever establishes the baseline first locates false declines fastest when they occur. Note: requesting transaction-level data touches partners' commercial confidentiality and personal data scope, so agree confidentiality and data scope first and take aggregates only, not records.
  • Produce player-facing material explaining the licensed-versus-unlicensed difference (internal estimate ~₱120K-₱280K / one-off). In language players understand, not compliance language. With half the handle in the black market the persuadable audience is half the market, and none of the nine is speaking to it. Note: the material must not carry promotional rewards or referral codes, and must not name any specific unlicensed site, or it risks being classed as advertising or drawing a dispute.
  • File a named comment on the draft before 2 October, seeking distinct treatment for licensed sites at the payment layer (internal estimate ~₱80K-₱200K / 11 days left). The point is to let payment providers' risk rules recognise licensed status so legitimate and black-market sites do not fall into the same sieve. The direction favours licensees and aligns with the central bank's own policy aim. Note: filing identifies your business model publicly, so confirm first that your existing architecture will not itself be examined as a result.
  • Cost out in-house local content and decide between building and outsourcing (internal estimate ~₱150K-₱400K / 4-6 weeks including one pilot). Work out the real cost of one short drama episode or one livestreamed format and compare it against the exposure the same budget buys in media. Note: keep the pilot small enough to abandon, and do not commit to a long contract or a standing team at the first attempt; the quality bar for local content is set by local audiences and external production resources may not clear it.
