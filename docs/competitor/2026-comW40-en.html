1. DigiPlus lost its Brazil launchpad on 9/25 over a betting ban.
Brazilian President Lula signed a provisional measure (an executive act effective immediately, subject to congressional ratification) on September 25 banning the operation, offering, intermediation and advertising of fixed-odds betting and online gaming. Brazil was meant to be the first overseas market for DigiPlus (parent of BingoPlus, ArenaPlus and GameZone): it applied for a federal license in August 2024 and was authorized in January 2025; its GamePlus brand soft-launched in September 2025 and was paused weeks later. On September 28 the company told the PSE it does not expect a material impact. A week earlier the ArenaPlus head had called Brazil "a bit aggressive" and said South Africa licenses are in hand. See §04 card 1. (Context.ph 9/28 · BusinessWorld 9/21)

2. Solaire Online to advertise within a month on a 20% GGR goal.
Bloomberry (listed parent of the Solaire integrated resorts) President and COO Greg Hawkins said at the IAG Academy Summit in Manila that he wants online at about 20% of consolidated group GGR by end-2027. He said premium site Solaire Online "has never been marketed" and will start active marketing over the next month or so; mass-market FUNaloMAX has run on the group's own platform for months, deliberately without the Solaire name. On September 25 Maybank Securities upgraded Bloomberry to Buy, citing online ramp-up as a catalyst. See §04 card 2. (IAG 9/20 · IAG 9/25)

3. DigiPlus margin seen at 14.5% on costlier player acquisition.
S&P forecasts DigiPlus EBITDA margin at 14.5-16% in 2026-27, below the ~18% 2024-25 average, blaming higher acquisition and retention costs after the August 2025 e-wallet delinking. It also said MAU recovered modestly in H1 2026 but may not regain pre-delinking levels within two years, and that stricter enforcement and the proposed minimum fee will squeeze smaller operators and consolidate the market. See §04 card 3. (BusinessWorld 9/22 · InsiderPH 9/21)

§02Strategic Read · 5Q

Strategic read · five questions (neutral)

Q1
Market space

The change this week was on the supply side: one more buyer, one fewer overseas outlet. Bloomberry set online at about 20% of group GGR by end-2027 and said Solaire Online starts active marketing in about a month. DigiPlus's first overseas market, Brazil, was banned outright by presidential measure on September 25.

The regulator's own numbers are falling too. PAGCOR's 2025 annual report sets a 2026 minimum after-tax net income target of ₱4.87B against ₱17.46B actual in 2025, noting it is a roadmap floor, not a forecast. New online licenses have been frozen since March 2024 and licensees fell from 74 to 48.

Read: the market is not growing, but the number of players chasing it is not shrinking. The leader lost an overseas outlet, a new buyer is moving in from land-based, and total GGR is still down year on year. For an entrant, Q4 is not a cheap-traffic window.

Q2
Player focus

No new player-side data this week. The one usable line is from S&P: DigiPlus MAU recovered modestly in H1 2026 but may not reach pre-delinking levels within two years.

Read: MAU not recovering means players lost at delinking did not all come back to licensed sites, consistent with last issue's ~50% channelization figure. Players compare how easy it is to deposit, not whose rebate is higher.

Q3
Unmet gaps

The six standing gaps are still open: plain-language compliance creative, alternative payment how-tos, promo term calculators, KYC walk-throughs, license verification explainers, and retention content beyond promos. The payment-change player comms plan and the licensed-vs-unlicensed explainer added in the last two issues are also untouched.

A ninth gap this week comes from Solaire's two-brand structure. None of the nine uses separate brands for premium and mass audiences; BingoPlus, ArenaPlus and GameZone split by game type, not by customer.

Read: Solaire uses a resort-named premium site to carry land-based VIP trust and an unbranded site to go after the mass market. No one in Philippine online has cut the market this way. Single-brand operators risk losing part of the premium segment to a rival with physical-world trust.

Creative constraints still apply. Since ASC (Ad Standards Council) Memo 2026-008 took effect on June 24, ads on any medium cannot show or mention casino games or paraphernalia such as roulette, baccarat, blackjack, slots, cards, chips or dice.

Q4
Price structure

Still two models: DigiPlus runs events, the rest run rebates; nobody switched. Last new offer was in July; the sports offer that expired 7/19 and CasinoPlus's 1.5% that expired 7/31 are both unfilled.

1.5% is a regulatory cap. Under current PAGCOR rules, cash rebates based on wagers or deposits are capped at 1.5% (excluding table and arcade games) and cashback on net losses at 15%.

Read: S&P's margin figure gives the quiet here another explanation. The leader's margin is falling because of acquisition and retention cost, not rebates. No one has room to raise rebates; once Solaire starts, competition hits placement and KOL prices first, not pricing.

Q5
Differentiation

All three threads sit on the same layer: cost of competing. Overseas: DigiPlus's Brazil market is gone and the company tells the PSE it is immaterial. New bidder: Solaire Online starts marketing in about a month, targeting 20% of group GGR. Cost: S&P sees DigiPlus margin at 14.5-16%, blamed on costlier acquisition after delinking. The nine operators made no pricing moves.

Conclusion: Q4 competition in Philippine online will be fought on acquisition cost. The leader's resources stay home, a new bidder arrives with a physical brand, and each player already costs more after delinking. Pricing stays quiet; placement, KOL and local-content prices move first.

Proposal: Three things. First, lock Q4 Philippine online media and KOL buys early, before Solaire starts. Second, raise acquisition-cost assumptions and add Solaire Online and FUNaloMAX creative to weekly monitoring. Third, add change-in-law clauses to cross-market contracts; Brazil went from signature to blocking in under two weeks. These are suggestions, not conclusions.

Card 1 · DigiPlus / Brazil9/21-9/28
High

DigiPlus lost its Brazil launchpad on 9/25 over a betting ban.

Brazilian President Lula (Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva) signed a provisional measure on September 25 banning the operation, offering, intermediation and advertising of fixed-odds betting and online gaming. It took effect on publication and must be ratified by Congress within up to 120 days. Per industry reports, players must request withdrawals by October 5 and licensed sites are blocked from October 6.
Brazil was meant to be DigiPlus's first overseas market. The company applied for a federal license in August 2024 and received definitive authorization in January 2025. Its GamePlus brand soft-launched in September 2025 and was paused weeks later for a rework; it has not relaunched. On September 28 DigiPlus told the PSE (Philippine Stock Exchange) it is assessing the development and, based on its assessment to date, does not expect the measure to have a material impact on its financial condition or results. The filing did not state the size of the Brazil investment or current exposure.
A week earlier, on September 21, ArenaPlus head Erick Su said picking a market as competitive as Brazil had been "a bit aggressive", that policy there had been turbulent, and that Brazil was live but on hold pending review. The same report said DigiPlus obtained three South African gaming licenses in April 2026 and is preparing to launch within the required window, and is applying in New Zealand.
Read: "Not material" is credible. GamePlus ran for a few weeks, Brazil never produced real revenue, and the loss is mostly license and set-up cost, which was not disclosed. Two things matter more. First, one fewer overseas option. South Africa has not launched and New Zealand is still an application, so for at least a while DigiPlus management attention and marketing budget will sit in the Philippines. Anyone running marketing in the Philippines should plan Q4 assuming the leader spends more at home, not less. Second, Brazil went from signature to site blocking in under two weeks, with ads and sponsorship banned the same day. The Philippines has two ad-ban bills (SB 2347, HB 10982), neither yet in substantive committee review, but Brazil is a reminder that when rules flip, the time left to pull creative can be counted in days.

The 10/5 withdrawal and 10/6 blocking dates are per iGaming Business; the daily monitor logged "offline around 10/5" when reading the text on 9/26. Both are shown. The DigiPlus filing itself was not obtained; quotes are via Context.ph and InsiderPH. Brazil investment size, any write-down and whether GamePlus formally exits were not found and are not inferred. High

Solaire Online to advertise within a month on a 20% GGR goal.

Bloomberry President and COO Greg Hawkins told IAG at the IAG Academy Summit in Manila that he wants online to reach about 20% of consolidated group GGR by end-2027. The story ran September 20; the Summit was September 15.
He also laid out the two-brand setup. Mass-market FUNaloMAX relaunched on the group's own platform months ago (official launch July 3), deliberately without the Solaire name, and is building its own database. Premium Solaire Online is moving to the same platform; in his words it has been "quite a solid earnings performer" but "has never been marketed", and over the next month or so it will start to be actively marketed. Bloomberry deliberately avoids GGR-share or shared-platform models. At the same event, Hann Casino Resorts said its online product is hosted by PhilWeb and it has set no similar share target.
On September 25 Maybank Securities upgraded Bloomberry from Hold to Buy, forecasting a 2026 core loss of about ₱2.3B and a 2027 profit of about ₱421M, and listed the ramp-up of its e-gaming platforms as a catalyst to watch.
Read: An online brand with built-in resort trust that has never paid for traffic says it will start buying within about a month, and it has a deliberately unbranded mass site underneath. For the nine incumbents that is a new bidder, not a new product. A 20% target means this is not a test; Maybank putting online growth into its upgrade means the market will track execution, so management is under pressure to move the numbers. The brand split matters too. Solaire Online carries land-based VIP trust for the premium end; FUNaloMAX, without the name, can run harder because mistakes do not touch the resort. For anyone running marketing, expect one more bidder on Philippine online placements, KOLs and networks in October and November; if they need numbers fast, the best-converting slots get bid up first.

Implications (threats / opportunities)
