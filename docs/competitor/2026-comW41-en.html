§02Strategic Read · 5Q

Strategic read · five questions (neutral)

Q1
Market space

The leader's revenue is still shrinking. Maybank cites DigiPlus H1 revenue down ~31% to ₱32.9B, with Q2 down 36.8%; it cut 2026-2028 revenue forecasts by 18-32%, to about ₱65.4B in 2026 and ₱64.9B in 2027, on weaker H1 results and slower growth plus inflation eating into players' disposable income.

Read: player numbers are not shrinking; spend per player is. The market will not grow in Q4 on returning players. Everyone is chasing less money in the same pockets.

Q2
Player focus

Maybank's figures: DigiPlus Q2 MAU about 5.75M, "stabilised"; monthly spend per active user about ₱805 against an assumed ₱1,250.

Read: players are still playing, just more cheaply. Low-threshold, frequent small rewards will appeal; big first-deposit bonuses less so. ArenaPlus's "daily" caps point the same way.

Q3
Unmet gaps

The six standing gaps are still open: plain-language compliance creative, alternative payment how-tos, promo term calculators, KYC walk-throughs, license verification explainers, and retention content beyond promos. The payment-change comms plan, licensed-vs-unlicensed explainer and audience-split branding are also untouched.

Promo term calculators became more urgent this week. ArenaPlus quotes caps in pesos (up to ₱2,888) while the regulatory cap is a percentage (1.5%), so players cannot tell what they will actually get.

Read: as cap formats diverge, whoever first shows players "wager this much, get this much back" owns term clarity.

Creative constraints still apply. Since ASC (Ad Standards Council) Memo 2026-008 took effect on June 24, ads on any medium cannot show or mention casino games or paraphernalia such as roulette, baccarat, blackjack, slots, cards, chips or dice.

Q4
Price structure

Still two models: DigiPlus runs events, the rest run rebates. What is new is wording: ArenaPlus writes rebate, reload and cashback as "up to ₱X", not percentages.

Under current PAGCOR rules, cash rebates on wagers or deposits are capped at 1.5% (excluding table and arcade games) and cashback on net losses at 15%. The ads do not show what wager "up to ₱2,888" maps to or whether it sits within 1.5%.

Read: peso caps make the headline number bigger without necessarily raising what players get. Whether PAGCOR sees the format as compliant has not been publicly addressed (see §06 threat 3).

Q5
Differentiation

This week's routes: BingoPlus sponsors a music awards show, ArenaPlus pivots its ads from sports to game rebates, CasinoPlus's release stresses a jackpot paid in full, and GameZone, OKBet, Bet88, PlayTime and OKADA PLAY made no moves.

Conclusion: the leader's problem has shifted from too few players to too little spend per player, and its smallest brand, ArenaPlus, moved first on rebate wording. Q4 competition will turn on two things: small, frequent reward design, and how clearly terms are written.

Proposal: Two things. First, set entry-model ARPU near ₱805, not pre-delinking figures. Second, attach a worked "what you actually get" example to every promo and do not adopt peso-cap wording. These are suggestions, not conclusions.

Card 3 · PAGCOR / B2B accreditation9/30
Mid

PAGCOR set to stop unaccredited systems 10/1 as grace lapses.

A July 20 memo from PAGCOR's Electronic Gaming Licensing Department, approved by the board on July 16, moved the B2B supplier accreditation grace period from July 31 to September 30. It covers suppliers contracted to GSAs that applied by May 31; later applicants are excluded. Suppliers that do not finish by September 30 have their electronic gaming systems, online platforms, games and equipment decommissioned from October 1. Accreditation requires the fee, documents and probity report, an ocular inspection and system testing, and a performance cash deposit.
As of October 5, searches and the news index found no further extension or new memo, and no list of decommissioned suppliers or reports of games pulled from licensed platforms.
Read: The deadline is now live, but there is no visible trace of enforcement yet. Not seen is not the same as not happening: PAGCOR may not publish a list, and games may simply disappear inside platforms. For anyone running marketing, the risk is in scheduling: a Q4 lead game from an unaccredited supplier could vanish after the creative is done.
⚠️ Caveat: whether the accredited list is public and how many suppliers are accredited were not found in primary sources. Grace terms per GGRAsia 8/3; the memo itself was not read.
⚠️ Single-channel: industry coverage and in-window searches only; player complaints about missing games could not be sampled.
Sources: Pagcor extends deadline to Sept 30 for online gaming B2B accreditation · GGRAsia 8/3
Proposal (for Client X): Confirm supplier accreditation for every Q4 lead game with the platform, in writing; do not put unconfirmed games in lead slots.

§06Implications · Threats / Opportunities

Implications (threats / opportunities)

Entry parameters (all internal estimates)
  • Plan ARPU at ₱805: Maybank puts DigiPlus monthly spend per active user at about ₱805 against an assumed ₱1,250. Use ₱805 as base case and ₱1,250 as upside. Basis in §04 card 1; not repeated elsewhere.
  • Budget acquisition cost to rise: S&P sees the leader's EBITDA margin falling from ~18% to 14.5-16% on higher acquisition and retention cost after delinking; Maybank cites 16.7% actual for H1.
  • Player scale reference: DigiPlus Q2 MAU about 5.75M (BingoPlus ~3.35M, GameZone ~1.83M, ArenaPlus ~570K).
  • The rebate ceiling is regulatory: 1.5% cash rebate on wagers or deposits (excluding table and arcade games), 15% cashback on net losses.
  • Creative constraints: ASC Memo 2026-008, effective 2026/06/24, bars showing or mentioning casino games and paraphernalia in ads.
  • Deadlines: B2B accreditation grace ended 9/30, 5 days ago; BSP comments closed 10/2, 3 days ago, no final circular seen; MGF phase 2 on 2027/01/01, 88 days out.
ThreatsThreats
  • Payback built on old ARPU will stretch. ₱805 monthly spend against an assumed ₱1,250. Cost of inaction (internal estimate): planning on ₱1,250 understates payback by over a third, and first-year acquisition budget may need a mid-year top-up.
  • Peso-cap wording makes rivals' headline numbers look better. ArenaPlus's "up to ₱2,888" against a plain "1.5%". Cost of inaction (internal estimate): percentage-only creative without a worked example could see 10-25% lower click-through on the same placement.
  • Copying peso caps or slot-reel creative puts the compliance risk on you. Classification awaits PAGCOR and ASC readings. Cost of inaction (internal estimate): if asked to revise or pull ads, rework and resubmission take about 1-2 weeks of dead airtime.
  • After the grace period, lead games may quietly disappear. Unaccredited systems stop from 10/1, with no public list seen. Cost of inaction (internal estimate): a lead campaign tied to an unaccredited supplier's games would need emergency swaps eating an estimated 20-40% of its budget.
  • With the leader's profits under pressure, Q4 promo intensity may rise. Core net income forecasts were cut by over 40%, and the smallest brand has already moved in ads. Cost of inaction (internal estimate): a lead campaign colliding with a DigiPlus push could see share of voice diluted by 30-50% for that period.
OpportunitiesOpportunities
  • Small, frequent reward design (internal estimate ~₱50K-₱150K product and creative time / 2-3 weeks). For players spending ~₱805 a month, daily or weekly small rewards fit better than big first-deposit bonuses. Note: daily rewards are still bound by the 1.5% cap; do the math first.
  • Make term clarity the difference (internal estimate ~₱30K-₱80K / one-off). Add "wager this, get this back" examples to creative, filling the promo-calculator gap. Note: examples must match the terms word for word, or clarity becomes a new source of complaints.
  • Offer supplier-accreditation checks as a service (internal estimate ~₱20K-₱60K staff time / one-off). Build an accreditation status list of lead games for partner platforms before scheduling. Note: rely on the platform's written confirmation; do not judge status yourself.
  • The casual-game audience is now visible (internal estimate ~₱40K-₱120K test budget / 1 month). GameZone, at ~1.83M MAU, is DigiPlus's second-largest brand; the casual and arcade audience is three times the sports one. Note: MAU is not revenue and casual players may spend less; measure ARPU in any test.
